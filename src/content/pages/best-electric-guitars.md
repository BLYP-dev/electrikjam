<div class="wp-block-group is-vertical is-layout-flex wp-container-core-group-is-layout-fe9cc265 wp-block-group-is-layout-flex"><h3 class="has-link-color wp-elements-f20687f23363ef9cbedd633ef1b74d04 wp-block-post-title has-text-color has-main-color has-medium-font-size"><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/new-guitar-releases/gretsch-g5260t-electromatic-jet-baritone-with-bigsby/" target="_self">Gretsch G5260T Electromatic Jet Baritone With Bigsby</a></h3>

<div class="field-brief_overview_of_the_guitar wp-block-acf-field-blocks-acf-text has-small-font-size">The G5260T comes loaded with Gretsch mini-humbuckers, and no, they’re not just a marketing gimmick. These things are often compared to Broad’Tron-style pickups meaning they’re hotter than vintage Filter’Trons but still cleaner than full-size humbuckers.</div>


<details class="wp-block-details is-layout-flow wp-block-details-is-layout-flow"><summary>Why We Like This Guitar</summary><div class="field-notable_features_&amp;_testing_notes wp-block-acf-field-blocks-acf-text">Here&#8217;s a quick overview of my notes after spending a solid few months getting to know this epic baritone guitar.<br />
<br />
<strong>Bridge pickup</strong><br />
<br />
Bright and snarly with a surprising amount of body. There&#8217;s enough bite to slice through a fuzz pedal without losing the bottom end. Think early Queens of the Stone Age meets surf noir.<br />
<br />
<strong>Neck pickup</strong><br />
<br />
Warm, full, and shockingly articulate. It’s got this piano-like low end that doesn’t fall apart in reverb or delay. Perfect for ambient chord washes or thick, slow lead lines.<br />
<br />
<strong>Middle position</strong><br />
<br />
Big and open. This setting really shines when you want to fill out the sonic space—drone-y chords, tremolo picking, that sort of thing.<br />
<br />
These pickups aren’t just good “for the price”—they’re genuinely good, period. And more importantly, they don’t choke out under gain or low tunings.<br />
<br />
I ran the neck pickup through a lo-fi slapback delay and a plate reverb, and it sounded like Ennio Morricone got into doom metal. Not joking—it’s that cinematic.<br />
<br />
<strong>The Feel: Long Scale, Bigsby Drama, and Classic Gretsch Vibes</strong><br />
<br />
One thing that immediately separates this baritone from others like the Squier Classic Vibe Baritone is the scale length. At 29.75&#8243;, this is longer than most baritones on the market—and you feel that.<br />
<br />
The upside? It handles B, A, or even drop-G tuning without feeling like a wet noodle. Seriously—it stays tight and in tune better than most.<br />
<br />
<strong>The neck</strong><br />
<br />
It’s a thin-U shape, super comfortable, and feels more modern than vintage. Great for sliding around and doing wide bends even with thick strings.<br />
<br />
<strong>Fretwork and finish</strong><br />
<br />
Genuinely solid. No sharp ends, no weird dead spots. You can tell this is one of those pro-feel guitars hiding in a mid-price shell.<br />
<br />
Now let’s talk about the Bigsby B50. This is where the vibe factor cranks up to 11.<br />
<br />
It’s not made for divebombing, obviously. But if you want subtle warble, shimmery chords, and slow-motion bends—it’s brilliant. I’ve used it for surfy leads and ambient swells, and it just feels right.<br />
<br />
As long as the nut is cut properly, tuning stability is surprisingly solid. I gave it a graphite lube and haven’t had any major issues, even with pretty aggressive Bigsby use.<br />
<br />
<strong>Where It Really Shines</strong><br />
<br />
This guitar isn’t trying to be everything to everyone. But what it is good at, it does extremely well.<br />
<br />
<strong>Surf / Spaghetti-Western / Dark Country</strong><br />
<br />
That piano-like low end and Bigsby shimmer? Perfect for tremolo-drenched chords, twangy lead lines, or haunted desert soundscapes. If you want to write the soundtrack to your imaginary Western revenge movie—this is your guitar.<br />
<br />
<strong>Grunge / Doom / Alternative Rock</strong><br />
<br />
I’ve thrown everything from Big Muff to a Rat at it, and it handles it with class. It doesn’t get muddy, and the mini-humbuckers help preserve definition even under woolly fuzz tones.<br />
<br />
<strong>Ambient / Shoegaze / Indie</strong><br />
<br />
Pair the neck pickup with some modulated delay and a shimmer reverb, and you’re in post-rock heaven. The long scale gives you tons of sustain, and the notes bloom out in a really expressive way.<br />
<br />
<strong>Where It’s Not at Home</strong><br />
<br />
Look, if you’re after ultra-modern metal or tight, compressed chugs, this probably isn’t your weapon. It can do rock and heavy alt styles just fine, but if you’re expecting EMG-level output or 24-fret shred access, you might want to check something in the Schecter<br />
or Ibanez world instead.<br />
<br />
That said, if you’re more interested in vibe, tone, and cinematic character than note-for-note shredding, you’re squarely in the G5260T’s sweet spot.<br />
<br />
<strong>Wrapping Up&#8230;</strong><br />
<br />
The Gretsch G5260T Electromatic Jet Baritone is not just a cool-looking oddball—it’s a genuinely expressive, flexible baritone guitar that feels way more expensive than it is. The pickups are killer, the scale length keeps things tight, and the Bigsby adds just the right amount of drama without becoming a tuning liability.<br />
<br />
It’s not a metal monster. It’s not a one-trick pony either. What it is is one of the most musical and fun baritone guitars I’ve played, especially for genres that need clarity, character, and low-end presence.<br />
<br />
If you&#8217;re building a rig around dark surf, drone, fuzz, or ambient noise? This thing belongs on your shortlist.</div></details>

<details class="wp-block-details is-layout-flow wp-block-details-is-layout-flow"><summary>Why We Like This Guitar</summary><div class="field-notable_features_&amp;_testing_notes wp-block-acf-field-blocks-acf-text">The Manson Meta Series MBM-2H in Satin Olive Green isn’t just a cool-looking signature model—it’s a full-on workhorse designed for players who actually gig, rehearse, and record. Whether you&#8217;re a Muse diehard or just want a guitar with some bite and flair, this thing brings a lot to the table without the boutique price tag.<br />
<br />
I got some hands-on time with this one, and here’s what stood out.<br />
<h2><strong>First Impressions: Built For Action</strong></h2><br />
Right away, the compound radius neck feels like it was designed by someone who actually plays live. It’s super comfy for rhythm playing lower down and flattens out perfectly for soloing up top. That matching headstock in olive green? It’s subtle, but it pulls the whole aesthetic together. Clean, pro, and modern.<br />
<h3><strong>The Features That Matter</strong></h3><br />
Manson Humbuckers That Actually Deliver: These aren’t throwaway pickups. They’re full-bodied, dynamic, and respond really well to amp gain. You get tight rock tones when cranked, but they clean up nicely with a little roll-off on the volume knob. If you need versatility without swapping guitars mid-set, these do the job.<br />
<h4><strong>That Bellamy-Style Kill Button</strong></h4><br />
If you’re into experimental textures or love that signature Muse stutter effect, the kill button is right there at your fingertips—easy to access, but tucked away enough that you’re not hitting it by accident. It’s a small feature that adds a lot of personality.<br />
<h4><strong>Cort Locking Tuners for Real-World Use</strong></h4><br />
Locking tuners are a must for any gigging musician, and Cort’s staggered set here holds tuning impressively well. They also make string changes way quicker—especially handy if you&#8217;re breaking a string five minutes before soundcheck.<br />
<h2><strong>Who’s It For?</strong></h2><br />
Obviously, Muse fans will be all over this but you don’t have to be a Bellamy fanboy to appreciate what’s going on here. If you need a solid rock guitar that looks slick, feels great, and doesn’t flinch under pressure, this one’s worth a serious look. Is it better than an entry-level PRS? Probably not, although it is damn close and I think its unusual enough to stand out from the crowd. I also kind of reminds me of an Abasi guitar as well. Not a bad thing.</div></details>

<p>A common question for beginners is: <strong>How much should I spend on my first guitar?</strong> </p>



<p>It comes up all the time, and honestly, it depends on your budget and what you want to do with the guitar. </p>



<p>The good news? </p>



<p>Budget guitars have come a long way, and some of the more affordable options today are fantastic instruments.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>How Much Should I Spend On My First Guitar?</strong></h3>



<p>Expect to spend between $150 and $500 for a good beginner guitar from a reputable brand. </p>



<p>Stick with trusted names like Epiphone, Squier, Harley Benton, or Schecter. </p>



<p>The more you pay, the better the features, but that doesn’t mean you need to drop a fortune for your first guitar.</p>



<p>If you’re unsure whether you’ll stick with guitar, go for something like a Squier Telecaster or an Epiphone Les Paul. </p>



<p>These are solid, affordable choices that won’t break the bank.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large has-custom-border"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="683" src="/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/best-harley-benton-telecaster-1024x683.png" alt="best harley benton telecaster" class="wp-image-11882" style="border-radius:20px" title="" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/best-harley-benton-telecaster-1024x683.png 1024w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/05/best-harley-benton-telecaster-600x400.png 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/05/best-harley-benton-telecaster-300x200.png 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/05/best-harley-benton-telecaster-768x512.png 768w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/05/best-harley-benton-telecaster.png 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption"><strong>Ding ding, we have a new brand!</strong> We selected a color palette that evokes warmth and depth, inviting customers to immerse themselves in the brand.</figcaption></figure>



<p>If you know you’re committed to learning, consider spending a bit more so you can &#8220;grow into&#8221; the guitar. </p>



<p>A higher-priced guitar often offers better sound, playability, and durability.</p>



<p>And remember, if your budget allows, there’s no rule that says your first guitar has to be cheap. If you can afford something better and it excites you, go for it!</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Why Budget Guitars Are Worth It</strong></h3>



<p><strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/best-electric-guitars/best-budget-guitars/" data-lasso-id="269093">We’ve tested plenty of budget guitars</a></strong>, and a lot of them are great for beginners.</p>



<p>But keep this in mind: you get what you pay for. </p>



<p>A $150 guitar will get you started, but spending a little more can get you better pickups, nicer finishes, and smoother playability. </p>



<p>That said, we’ve found some <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/best-electric-guitars/best-budget-guitars/" data-lasso-id="269094">budget gems that punch way above their price</a></strong>, and these are what we recommend new players use right now. </p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What To Look For In Your First Guitar</strong></h3>



<p>Not all budget brands are created equal, so it’s important to stick with reliable, name-brand guitars. </p>



<p>There are a lot of cheap, no-name guitars on sites like Amazon that you should avoid. </p>



<p>We recommend brands that we’ve personally tested, like <strong>Harley Benton, Epiphone, and Squier</strong>—they’re affordable but built with quality components.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Try Before You Buy</strong></h3>



<p>If you can, visit a local music store and try some guitars in person. You don’t need to know how to play yet; just holding a guitar can give you a feel for what you might like. </p>



<p>Here’s what to check for:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Body Comfort</strong>: Is the guitar comfortable to hold?</li>



<li><strong>Neck Feel</strong>: Every neck is different, so find one that feels good in your hand.</li>



<li><strong>Weight</strong>: Some guitars are heavier than others. Make sure you’re comfortable with the weight.</li>
</ul>



<p>Don’t be nervous about trying out guitars in-store. Music store employees are used to beginners, so let them know you’re new and they’ll guide you to a solid choice. Stick to trusted brands, and you’ll be in good hands.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Want to Play Like Your Guitar Heroes?</strong></h3>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large has-custom-border"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="683" src="/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Melvins-1024x683.png" alt="" class="wp-image-13850" style="border-radius:20px" title="" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Melvins-1024x683.png 1024w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Melvins-600x400.png 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Melvins-300x200.png 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Melvins-768x512.png 768w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Melvins.png 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption"><strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/buzz-osbornes-guitar-rig-35-years-of-evolution/" data-type="post" data-id="1565" data-lasso-id="269095">King Buzzo</a></strong>. One of Our Personal Guitar Heroes <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/electrical-guitar-company-101-guide/" data-type="post" data-id="14894" data-lasso-id="269096">(Playing An Electrical Guitar Company Guitar)</a></strong></figcaption></figure>



<p>A lot of people want to get the same guitar as their favorite player—and that’s totally fine! </p>



<p>If you love Slash, try out a Les Paul. If you’re into Kurt Cobain, grab a Jaguar or a Mustang. </p>



<p>Just make sure it feels good to you, and don’t be afraid to try different guitars until you find the right fit.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Things to Avoid with Your First Guitar</strong></h3>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large has-custom-border"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="683" src="/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Fender-American-Professional-II-Jazzmaster-vs-Fender-American-Professional-II-Telecaster-1024x683.png" alt="Fender American Professional II Jazzmaster vs Fender American Professional II Telecaster" class="wp-image-15750" style="border-radius:20px" title="" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Fender-American-Professional-II-Jazzmaster-vs-Fender-American-Professional-II-Telecaster-1024x683.png 1024w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Fender-American-Professional-II-Jazzmaster-vs-Fender-American-Professional-II-Telecaster-600x400.png 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Fender-American-Professional-II-Jazzmaster-vs-Fender-American-Professional-II-Telecaster-300x200.png 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Fender-American-Professional-II-Jazzmaster-vs-Fender-American-Professional-II-Telecaster-768x512.png 768w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Fender-American-Professional-II-Jazzmaster-vs-Fender-American-Professional-II-Telecaster.png 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption"><strong>Hardtail</strong> (Great For Beginners, Left), <strong>Tremolo Bridge</strong> (Not So Great, Right)</figcaption></figure>



<p><strong>Tremolo Bridges: AVOID!</strong></p>



<p>Here’s my top advice for beginners: stay away from guitars with a whammy bar (also known as a Tremolo Bridge or &#8220;trem&#8221;). </p>



<p>They look cool, but they can be a nightmare to deal with as a beginner. Stick to a guitar with a fixed bridge for easier tuning and maintenance.</p>



<p>And don’t worry about what anyone says about certain guitars being “wrong” for certain genres. </p>



<p>You can play metal on a Stratocaster, blues on a Flying V, or rock on anything in between. Get the guitar that appeals to you, and ignore the noise.</p>



<p>When it comes to your first guitar, stick to trusted brands, focus on comfort and feel, and don’t overthink it. </p>



<p>Whether you’re spending $150 or $500, just make sure you’re getting a guitar that makes you want to play.</p>
