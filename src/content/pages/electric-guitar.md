<p>Hey, I&#8217;m Chris – that&#8217;s me above. I&#8217;ve been playing guitars for over two decades, had a major record label deal in the mid-2000s, and I&#8217;ve worked in studios in LA and Atlanta with everyone from Katy Perry to Baroness and KYLESA. </p>



<p>My weapon of choice? Guitar. Always has been. And if you&#8217;re new and want to learn about how electric guitars work, inside and out, I made this article for you. </p>



<p class="has-main-color has-text-color has-base-font-size">Electric guitars haven’t really changed much since they first appeared on the scene over 70 years ago. The shape, the way they play, and the electronics –&nbsp;it’s all the same.&nbsp;</p>



<p>Of course, there have been advancements: new materials, new types of woods, massive developments in pickup technology, and fancier additions like locking tuners and floating tremolos.&nbsp;</p>



<p>On a macro scale, though, they’re the same as always. Learning how each component of your electric guitar works is always optimal. A good workman knows how his tools function, and what to do when they go wrong.&nbsp;</p>



<p>This post will take you on a journey from absolute n00b to knowing more than most advanced players. Check out the supporting posts linked throughout, dive into the subjects, go down the rabbit hole.&nbsp;</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading has-medium-font-size" style="letter-spacing:0px"><strong>A Brief History of The Electric Guitar</strong></h3>
</div>



<div class="wp-block-column is-layout-flow wp-block-column-is-layout-flow" style="flex-basis:60%">
<p>Let&#8217;s hop in our time machine and zip back to the 1930s. Musicians were getting frustrated because their acoustic guitars couldn&#8217;t be heard over the other instruments in big band settings. </p>



<p>Enter our heroes: innovators like Leo Fender and Les Paul.<br></p>



<p>These guys thought, &#8220;Hey, what if we could make a guitar that plugs into an amplifier?&#8221; And boom! The electric guitar was born. From the 1950s Fender Telecaster to the iconic Gibson Les Paul, electric guitars have been shaping the sound of music for decades.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading has-medium-font-size" style="letter-spacing:0px"><strong>Why Everyone&#8217;s Crazy About Electric Guitars</strong></h3>
</div>



<div class="wp-block-column is-layout-flow wp-block-column-is-layout-flow" style="flex-basis:60%">
<p>So, why are electric guitars so darn popular? Well, let me count the ways:</p>



<p>They&#8217;re versatile as heck: Want to play blues? Check. Metal? You got it. Jazz? No problem. </p>



<p>Electric guitars can do it all. </p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>With an amp, you can go from bedroom player to stadium rocker with just a turn of a knob.</li>



<li>Effects galore: Pedals and effects units let you twist and warp your sound in mind-bending ways. Fancy sounding like you&#8217;re playing underwater? There&#8217;s a pedal for that!</li>



<li>Rock star factor: Let&#8217;s face it, when you picture a guitar god like Jimi Hendrix or Eric Clapton, they&#8217;re usually wielding an electric axe.</li>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading has-medium-font-size" style="letter-spacing:0px"><strong>How Do Electric Guitars Work?</strong></h3>
</div>



<div class="wp-block-column is-layout-flow wp-block-column-is-layout-flow" style="flex-basis:60%">
<p>Alright, time for a quick science lesson (don&#8217;t worry, it&#8217;s the fun kind). </p>



<p>Here&#8217;s the basic rundown:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>You strum or pick the strings.</li>



<li>The strings vibrate over the pickups (remember those?).</li>



<li>The pickups, which are basically magnets wrapped in wire, convert those vibrations into electrical signals.</li>



<li>The signals travel through your cable to your amplifier.</li>



<li>The amp boosts the signal and sends it to the speaker.</li>



<li>The speaker converts the electrical signal back into sound waves.</li>
</ul>



<p>And that&#8217;s just the beginning! Add some effects pedals into the mix, and you can create everything from subtle echoes to face-melting distortion.</p>

<p>Now that you&#8217;ve got the basics down, let&#8217;s talk about the different types of electric guitars out there. It&#8217;s like walking into an ice cream shop &#8211; there are so many flavors to choose from, and they&#8217;re all delicious in their own way!</p>



<div class="wp-block-columns alignwide is-layout-flex wp-container-core-columns-is-layout-28f84493 wp-block-columns-is-layout-flex">
<div class="wp-block-column is-vertically-aligned-stretch is-layout-flow wp-block-column-is-layout-flow" style="flex-basis:40%">
<h3 class="wp-block-heading has-medium-font-size" style="letter-spacing:0px"><strong>Solid-Body Electric Guitars: The Workhorses</strong></h3>
</div>



<div class="wp-block-column is-layout-flow wp-block-column-is-layout-flow" style="flex-basis:60%">
<p>First up, we&#8217;ve got solid-body electric guitars. These bad boys are the most common type you&#8217;ll see, and for good reason:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>They&#8217;re built like tanks, so they can handle your wildest rock star antics.</li>



<li>They&#8217;ve got amazing sustain, meaning your notes will ring out for days (well, almost).</li>



<li>They&#8217;re super versatile, fitting into pretty much any genre you can think of.</li>
</ul>



<p>Think of guitars like the Fender Stratocaster or the Gibson Les Paul. These are the go-to axes for many players, from beginners to pros. They&#8217;re like the Swiss Army knives of the guitar world &#8211; they can do it all!</p>


</div>
</div>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading has-medium-font-size" style="letter-spacing:0px"><strong>Semi-Hollow Body Electric Guitars: The Best of Both Worlds</strong></h3>
</div>



<div class="wp-block-column is-layout-flow wp-block-column-is-layout-flow" style="flex-basis:60%">
<p>Next up, we&#8217;ve got semi-hollow body guitars. These are like the cool older cousin of solid-body guitars. They&#8217;ve got a solid center block with hollow &#8220;wings&#8221; on either side. Here&#8217;s why some players swear by them:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>They&#8217;ve got a warmer, rounder tone than solid-bodies.</li>



<li>They can give you some lovely resonance and sustain.</li>



<li>They&#8217;re great for styles like blues, jazz, and indie rock.</li>

<p>Famous models include the Gibson ES-335 &#8211; it&#8217;s been used by everyone from Chuck Berry to Dave Grohl. Pretty good company, right?</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading has-medium-font-size" style="letter-spacing:0px"><strong>Hollow Body Electric Guitars: The Jazz Cats&#8217; Delight</strong></h3>
</div>



<div class="wp-block-column is-layout-flow wp-block-column-is-layout-flow" style="flex-basis:60%">
<p>Now, let&#8217;s talk about hollow body electric guitars. These are like the sophisticated older sibling in the electric guitar family. </p>



<p>They look a bit like acoustic guitars that have been electrified (which is basically what they are). Here&#8217;s why jazz players and blues aficionados love them:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>They&#8217;ve got a deep, rich tone that&#8217;s perfect for mellower styles.</li>



<li>They can produce some beautiful, complex harmonics.</li>



<li>They&#8217;re great for clean, warm tones.</li>
</ul>



<p>The Gibson ES-175 is a classic example &#8211; it&#8217;s been the weapon of choice for jazz legends like Pat Metheny. But you can use it for rock and metal too; Josh Homme recorded Songs For The Deaf on the Epiphone version (it costs like $300). </p>

<div class="wp-block-column is-layout-flow wp-block-column-is-layout-flow" style="flex-basis:60%">

<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>7-string guitars give you an extra low string, perfect for heavy riffs and extended chords.</li>



<li>8-string guitars and beyond? Now we&#8217;re in djent and progressive metal territory!</li>

<p>These guitars aren&#8217;t just for metal, though. They can add depth and complexity to any style of music.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading has-large-font-size"><strong>Different Types of Guitar Tuning</strong></h2>



<p>If you’re smart, you’ll learn in Standard Tuning. Once you understand the neck, chords, scale patterns, and the notes, you can <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/most-common-guitar-tunings/" data-type="post" data-id="5246" data-lasso-id="268179">experiment with alternative tunings</a></strong> like Drop D, D Standard, and more.</p>



<div class="wp-block-group is-nowrap is-layout-flex wp-container-core-group-is-layout-977a0762 wp-block-group-is-layout-flex">
<div class="wp-block-outermost-icon-block"><div class="icon-container has-icon-color has-main-color" style="color:#1E0E2E;width:28px"><svg xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" viewbox="0 0 24 24" aria-hidden="true"><path d="M16.7 7.1l-6.3 8.5-3.3-2.5-.9 1.2 4.5 3.4L17.9 8z"></path></svg></div></div>



<p><strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/standard-tuning-guitar-explained-history/" data-type="post" data-id="11231" data-lasso-id="268180">Standard Tuning</a></strong>: Standard Tuning is what the vast majority of popular and classic guitar music is written in. Think of it as basecamp for your guitar, a place where you can build and understand how everything works.</p>
</div>



<div class="wp-block-group is-nowrap is-layout-flex wp-container-core-group-is-layout-977a0762 wp-block-group-is-layout-flex">
<div class="wp-block-outermost-icon-block"><div class="icon-container has-icon-color has-main-color" style="color:#1E0E2E;width:28px"><svg xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" viewbox="0 0 24 24" aria-hidden="true"><path d="M16.7 7.1l-6.3 8.5-3.3-2.5-.9 1.2 4.5 3.4L17.9 8z"></path></svg></div></div>



<p><strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/d-standard-tuning-guide-how-to/" data-type="post" data-id="3692" data-lasso-id="268181">D Standard Tuning</a></strong>: D Standard Tuning is the same as Standard Tuning, except all of your strings are tuned DOWN a whole step. Standard Tuning looks like this: EADGBE, while D Standard looks like this DGCFAD.</p>
</div>



<div class="wp-block-group is-nowrap is-layout-flex wp-container-core-group-is-layout-977a0762 wp-block-group-is-layout-flex">
<div class="wp-block-outermost-icon-block"><div class="icon-container has-icon-color has-main-color" style="color:#1E0E2E;width:28px"><svg xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" viewbox="0 0 24 24" aria-hidden="true"><path d="M16.7 7.1l-6.3 8.5-3.3-2.5-.9 1.2 4.5 3.4L17.9 8z"></path></svg></div></div>



<p><strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/drop-d-tuning-guitar-guide/" data-type="post" data-id="5202" data-lasso-id="268182">Drop D Tuning</a></strong>: Drop D Tuning is where you turn the low e-string down a whole step to D while leaving all the other strings in Standard Tuning. This allows for one-finger power chords..</p>
</div>



<div class="wp-block-group is-nowrap is-layout-flex wp-container-core-group-is-layout-977a0762 wp-block-group-is-layout-flex">
<div class="wp-block-outermost-icon-block"><div class="icon-container has-icon-color has-main-color" style="color:#1E0E2E;width:28px"><svg xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" viewbox="0 0 24 24" aria-hidden="true"><path d="M16.7 7.1l-6.3 8.5-3.3-2.5-.9 1.2 4.5 3.4L17.9 8z"></path></svg></div></div>



<p><strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/most-common-guitar-tunings/" data-type="post" data-id="5246" data-lasso-id="268183">C Standard Tuning</a></strong>: C Standard Tuning is like D Standard Tuning except you’re tuning down another whole step. Josh Homme and other stoner rock bands like this tuning; it’s low and sounds incredibly heavy.</p>

<p>There’s plenty of guitar brands out there. Too many, to be honest. And they all do things slightly differently. You have the top brass: Fender, Gibson, Ibanez, and PRS.&nbsp;</p>



<p>And then you have more affordable brands like Harley Benton, Epiphone, Squier, and Yamaha.&nbsp;</p>



<p>You’ll want to be looking at brands like Harley Benton, Epiphone, Squier, and other more affordable brands. As a beginner, there’s no point spending thousands on a high-end guitar you cannot play.&nbsp;</p>



<p>Here’s a breakdown of all the most well known and respected electric guitar brands that we <strong>A) recommend and rate, and B) should be on your radar as a new player.</strong> </p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Fender: The Cool Kid on the Block</strong></h3>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large has-custom-border"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="683" src="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Fender-Meteora-vs-Fender-Telecaster-1024x683.png" alt="Fender Meteora vs Fender Telecaster" class="wp-image-12762" style="border-radius:20px" title="" srcset="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Fender-Meteora-vs-Fender-Telecaster-1024x683.png 1024w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Fender-Meteora-vs-Fender-Telecaster-600x400.png 600w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Fender-Meteora-vs-Fender-Telecaster-300x200.png 300w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Fender-Meteora-vs-Fender-Telecaster-768x512.png 768w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Fender-Meteora-vs-Fender-Telecaster.png 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<p>Fender is the brand to beat right now. It’s a legacy brand, sure, but unlike Gibson, Fender moves with the times. It listens to players, adjusts and evolves with the demands of modern guitarists.&nbsp;</p>



<p>It does custom shop, mainline models, and Player Series models for the aspiring guitarist that doesn’t want to spend $1000+.&nbsp;</p>



<p>And then you have its Squier brand which is where you’ll find affordable versions of its mainline models –&nbsp;from teles to strats to the Jazzmaster and the Mustang.&nbsp;</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>They invented the Stratocaster and Telecaster, two of the most iconic electric guitars ever.</li>



<li>Their guitars are known for their bright, crisp tones and versatility.</li>



<li>From Jimi Hendrix to John Mayer, countless legends have rocked Fenders.</li>
</ul>



<p>Fender guitars are great for genres like rock, blues, country, and pop. If you want that classic &#8220;twang,&#8221; Fender&#8217;s your brand.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Gibson: The Timeless Classic</strong></h3>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large has-custom-border"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="683" src="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/New-Gibson-Guitars-2024-1024x683.png" alt="New Gibson Guitars 2024" class="wp-image-14197" style="border-radius:20px" title="" srcset="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/New-Gibson-Guitars-2024-1024x683.png 1024w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/New-Gibson-Guitars-2024-600x400.png 600w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/New-Gibson-Guitars-2024-300x200.png 300w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/New-Gibson-Guitars-2024-768x512.png 768w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/New-Gibson-Guitars-2024.png 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<p>Gibson’s about as iconic as it gets with electric guitars. Ask 100 people what they think of when you say, “electric guitar”, and a good 70% of them will see a Gibson Les Paul in their mind’s eye.&nbsp;</p>



<p>Played by the greats and loved by nearly all who own one, Gibson is currently the biggest guitar brand on the planet with a 30% share of the market.&nbsp;</p>



<p>But its guitars are HELLA expensive. You’ll pay over $800 for the most basic-bitch Gibson. </p>



<p>But don’t do that; for the same money you can get much, much more bang for your buck by going with a high-end, Inspired By Gibson models from Epiphone. </p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>They&#8217;re the creators of the legendary Les Paul and SG models.</li>



<li>Gibson guitars are known for their warm, rich tones and amazing sustain.</li>



<li>Rock gods like Jimmy Page and Slash have made Gibson guitars famous.</li>
</ul>



<p>If you&#8217;re into rock, metal, or blues, a Gibson might be your dream guitar. But you&#8217;ll probably have to wait until either your record goes platinum or you open your first dental practice before you can afford one&#8230; </p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Ibanez: The Speed Demon</strong></h3>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large has-custom-border"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="683" src="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Ibanez-Signature-Guitars-A-Complete-Overview-2023-1024x683.png" alt="Ibanez Signature Guitars A Complete Overview [2023]" class="wp-image-13466" style="border-radius:20px" title="" srcset="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Ibanez-Signature-Guitars-A-Complete-Overview-2023-1024x683.png 1024w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Ibanez-Signature-Guitars-A-Complete-Overview-2023-600x400.png 600w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Ibanez-Signature-Guitars-A-Complete-Overview-2023-300x200.png 300w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Ibanez-Signature-Guitars-A-Complete-Overview-2023-768x512.png 768w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Ibanez-Signature-Guitars-A-Complete-Overview-2023.png 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<p>Now, let&#8217;s talk about Ibanez. If Fender is the cool kid and Gibson is the classic rocker, Ibanez is the speed demon that loves to shred. </p>



<p>And unlike Gibson and Fender (to an extent), Ibanez makes guitars for players at every stage of their journey – from its budget, beginner models right they way up to its high-end, professional models. </p>



<p>If you like choice, exotic pickups, fast necks, and you want to play fast, Ibanez is a great brand to get acquainted with. </p>



<p>Here&#8217;s why:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>They&#8217;re known for their fast, thin necks that are perfect for lightning-quick solos.</li>



<li><a class="wpil_keyword_link" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/ibanez-guitars/" title="Ibanez guitars" data-wpil-keyword-link="linked" data-lasso-id="268194">Ibanez guitars</a> often feature high-output pickups great for heavy genres.</li>



<li>Their RG series is a favorite among metal and hard rock players.</li>
</ul>



<p>If you dream of playing blistering solos or chunky metal riffs, Ibanez might be your jam.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Schecter: The Heavy Hitter</strong></h3>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large has-custom-border"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="683" src="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/SCHECTER-GUITARS-1-1024x683.png" alt="SCHECTER GUITARS (1)" class="wp-image-14017" style="border-radius:20px" title="" srcset="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/SCHECTER-GUITARS-1-1024x683.png 1024w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/SCHECTER-GUITARS-1-600x400.png 600w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/SCHECTER-GUITARS-1-300x200.png 300w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/SCHECTER-GUITARS-1-768x512.png 768w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/SCHECTER-GUITARS-1.png 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<p>Last but not least, we&#8217;ve got Schecter. These guys are like the new kid in school who showed up one day and immediately started turning heads. </p>



<p>Schecter operates in the middle to upper end of the premium segment of the market. You&#8217;ll pay anywhere from $600 to $2000 for a Schecter guitar. The quality of Schecter&#8217;s guitars, on all levels of its pricing spectrum, however, is what really sets them apart. </p>



<p>I&#8217;m basically a Schecter fanboy; they&#8217;re all I really play. I love them and, for the money, I don&#8217;t think there&#8217;s a better option on the market right now. </p>



<p>Here&#8217;s the scoop:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Schecter guitars are built with heavy music in mind.</li>



<li>They&#8217;re known for their high-quality construction and aggressive looks.</li>



<li>Models like the C-1 and Hellraiser are favorites in the metal and hard rock scenes.</li>
</ul>



<p>If you want to chug out some heavy riffs or play some face-melting solos, Schecter&#8217;s got your back.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading has-large-font-size"><strong>Other Noteworthy Brands: The Supporting Cast</strong></h2>



<p>Of course, these aren&#8217;t the only players in the game. There are tons of other great brands out there:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Epiphone</strong>: Gibson&#8217;s more affordable sister brand. Great quality at lower prices.</li>



<li><strong>PRS</strong> (Paul Reed Smith): Known for their beautiful finishes and versatile sounds.</li>



<li><strong>Yamaha</strong>: Makes solid, reliable guitars great for beginners and pros alike.</li>
</ul>



<p>Remember, no single brand is the &#8220;best.&#8221; Each has its strengths, and the right one for you depends on your playing style, the genres you love, and what feels good in your hands.</p>
