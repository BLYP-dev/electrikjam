<p id="htoc-here-s-how-they-break-down-in-actual-use">Here’s how they break down in actual use:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Bridge pickup:</strong> Bright and percussive, with plenty of snap. Even in B–B tuning, it’s got that sharp Tele “cut” that stops low notes from getting muddy. Great for surfy leads or sharp country licks with a little slapback.</li>



<li><strong>Neck pickup</strong>: Warm and rounded. If you roll the tone back just a hair, it’s perfect for “tic-tac” bass lines or even some doom-adjacent textures. It’s thumpy, but still tight enough not to wash out under reverb or delay.</li>



<li><strong>Middle position</strong>: That classic Tele jangle. Almost acoustic-sounding in a weirdly cool way. I found it especially useful for layering ambient clean parts where I wanted clarity but also a bit of sparkle.</li>

<p id="htoc-this-was-one-of-the-big-surprises-for-me-these-pickups-aren-t-high-output-by-any-means-around-7-2-7-3k-from-what-i-ve-seen-but-they-take-low-to-medium-gain-pretty-well-hit-them-with-a-light-overdrive-or-edge-of-breakup-fuzz-and-you-get-this-clangy-percussive-response-that-s-perfect-for-darker-country-tones-or-post-punk-chugging">This was one of the big surprises for me. These pickups aren’t high-output by any means (around 7.2–7.3kΩ from what I’ve seen), but they take low-to-medium gain pretty well. </p>



<p id="htoc-this-was-one-of-the-big-surprises-for-me-these-pickups-aren-t-high-output-by-any-means-around-7-2-7-3k-from-what-i-ve-seen-but-they-take-low-to-medium-gain-pretty-well-hit-them-with-a-light-overdrive-or-edge-of-breakup-fuzz-and-you-get-this-clangy-percussive-response-that-s-perfect-for-darker-country-tones-or-post-punk-chugging1">Hit them with a light overdrive or edge-of-breakup fuzz and you get this clangy, percussive response that’s perfect for darker country tones or post-punk chugging.</p>



<p id="htoc-but-here-s-the-catch-like-any-single-coil-especially-ones-with-baritone-sized-strings-ringing-out-it-can-get-noisy-with-more-aggressive-dirt-a-simple-noise-gate-or-a-compressor-pedal-helps-keep-things-tight-without-squashing-the-dynamics">But here’s the catch: like any single-coil, especially ones with baritone-sized strings ringing out, it can get noisy with more aggressive dirt. </p>



<p id="htoc-but-here-s-the-catch-like-any-single-coil-especially-ones-with-baritone-sized-strings-ringing-out-it-can-get-noisy-with-more-aggressive-dirt-a-simple-noise-gate-or-a-compressor-pedal-helps-keep-things-tight-without-squashing-the-dynamics1">A simple noise gate or a compressor pedal helps keep things tight without squashing the dynamics.</p>



<p id="htoc-so-if-you-re-looking-for-doom-or-sludge-tones-you-ll-get-there-but-probably-not-with-the-stock-setup-if-that-s-your-thing-maybe-start-looking-at-p90-loaded-optionsor-full-humbuckers">So if you&#8217;re looking for doom or sludge tones? <p id="htoc-so-if-you-re-looking-for-doom-or-sludge-tones-you-ll-get-there-but-probably-not-with-the-stock-setup-if-that-s-your-thing-maybe-start-looking-at-p90-loaded-optionsor-full-humbuckers1">If that’s your thing, maybe start looking at P90-loaded options or full humbuckers.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="htoc-what-it-s-best-at">What It’s Best At</h2>



<p id="htoc-after-putting-in-quite-a-few-hours-with-this-baritone-tele-here-s-where-i-think-it-really-shines">After putting in quite a lot of time in with this baritone Tele, here’s where I think it really shines:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Classic low-end clean tones</strong>: Think Duane Eddy, “Rebel Rouser,” and all that vintage instrumental rock stuff. It has punch without being muddy.</li>



<li><strong>Country and Americana</strong>: That bridge pickup is born for chicken pickin’—just tuned down a fourth. Throw in a slapback delay and it’s golden.</li>



<li><strong>Ambient/post-rock textures</strong>: The 27&#8243; scale gives you tons of sustain, and chords stay defined even under heavy modulation and reverb.</li>



<li><strong>Alt-country / dark country</strong>: With a bit of dirt, you can easily get that moody, cinematic tone that sits somewhere between outlaw and Western noir.</li>
