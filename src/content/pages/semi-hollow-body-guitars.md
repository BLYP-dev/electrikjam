<h2 class="wp-block-heading has-text-align-center" style="letter-spacing:-1px"><strong>What is A Semi-Hollow Body Guitar?</strong></h2>



<figure class="wp-block-image alignwide size-full"><img decoding="async" width="1200" height="800" src="/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/What-Pickups-Does-Josh-Homme-Use.png" alt="What Pickups Does Josh Homme Use" class="wp-image-7071" title="" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/What-Pickups-Does-Josh-Homme-Use.png 1200w, /wp-content/uploads/2022/03/What-Pickups-Does-Josh-Homme-Use-600x400.png 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2022/03/What-Pickups-Does-Josh-Homme-Use-300x200.png 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2022/03/What-Pickups-Does-Josh-Homme-Use-1024x683.png 1024w, /wp-content/uploads/2022/03/What-Pickups-Does-Josh-Homme-Use-768x512.png 768w, /wp-content/uploads/2022/03/What-Pickups-Does-Josh-Homme-Use-1080x720.png 1080w" sizes="(max-width: 1200px) 100vw, 1200px" /></figure>



<p>Semi-hollow body guitars hit the scene in the late 1950s, designed for players who wanted the rich resonance of hollow-body guitars without the feedback issues. </p>



<p>The goal? A guitar that could handle higher volumes and still deliver warm, resonant tones. </p>



<p>It worked. </p>



<p>Today, these guitars are staples in genres ranging from blues and jazz to rock and indie.</p>



<p class="has-main-color has-text-color has-base-font-size">A semi-hollow body guitar has a solid block running through its center, with hollow wings on either side. </p>



<p class="has-main-color has-text-color has-base-font-size">This design gives you the best of both worlds: <strong>reduced feedback and longer sustain while retaining the depth and richness that hollow-body guitars are known for. </strong></p>



<p class="has-main-color has-text-color has-base-font-size">The result is an instrument celebrated for its warm, versatile sound.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading has-medium-font-size" style="letter-spacing:0px"><strong>Semi-Hollow Design and Tone</strong></h3>
</div>



<div class="wp-block-column is-layout-flow wp-block-column-is-layout-flow" style="flex-basis:60%">
<p>The semi-hollow body design features a solid center block flanked by hollow wings. </p>



<p>This construction helps control feedback while giving the guitar a warmer, more resonant tone compared to a fully solid body. </p>



<p>It’s like getting the best of both worlds: <strong>you keep the sustain and punch of a solid-body but add the depth and character of a hollow-body.</strong></p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading has-medium-font-size" style="letter-spacing:0px"><strong>Wood Choices</strong></h3>
</div>



<div class="wp-block-column is-layout-flow wp-block-column-is-layout-flow" style="flex-basis:60%">
<p>The type of wood used in a semi-hollow guitar has a big impact on its tone:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Maple</strong>: Bright and punchy, it’s commonly used for the top, back, and sides of the guitar.</li>



<li><strong>Mahogany</strong>: Known for adding warmth and depth, this wood is often chosen for the body or neck.</li>



<li><strong>Spruce</strong>: Sometimes used for the top to deliver a more acoustic-like tone</li>
</ul>


</div>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading has-medium-font-size" style="letter-spacing:0px"><strong>How Do Semi-Hollow Body Guitars Work?</strong></h3>
</div>



<div class="wp-block-column is-layout-flow wp-block-column-is-layout-flow" style="flex-basis:60%">
<p>The semi-hollow body construction creates a tone that sits somewhere between solid-body and hollow-body guitars. </p>



<p>This unique sound makes semi-hollow guitars ideal for genres like jazz, blues, and rockabilly. </p>



<p>The resonance from the hollow chambers gives notes a fuller, richer sound, while the solid center block helps maintain clarity and reduce feedback, even at higher volumes. </p>


</div>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading has-medium-font-size" style="letter-spacing:0px"><strong>Pickups and Electronics</strong></h3>
</div>



<div class="wp-block-column is-layout-flow wp-block-column-is-layout-flow" style="flex-basis:60%">
<p>The pickups and electronics in a semi-hollow guitar play a crucial role in shaping its sound. Here’s what you’ll typically find:</p>



<p><strong>Common Pickup Choices</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Humbuckers</strong>: Known for their thick, warm sound and minimal noise, humbuckers are a popular choice in semi-hollow guitars. They deliver a full, rich tone with less hum than single-coils, making them perfect for genres like jazz, blues, and rock.</li>



<li><strong>P-90s</strong>: For players looking for a bit more bite and brightness, P-90 pickups offer a grittier, more aggressive tone compared to humbuckers. They excel in cutting through the mix while still providing that semi-hollow warmth.</li>
</ul>



<p><strong>Controls and Wiring</strong></p>



<p>Similar to solid-body guitars, semi-hollow guitars usually feature standard controls like volume and tone knobs, along with pickup selectors. </p>



<p>The wiring allows for a wide range of tonal options, letting you dial in everything from smooth, mellow tones to sharp, edgy sounds.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading has-medium-font-size" style="letter-spacing:0px"><strong>Bridge Types</strong></h3>
</div>



<div class="wp-block-column is-layout-flow wp-block-column-is-layout-flow" style="flex-basis:60%">
<p>The bridge plays a vital role in a guitar’s tone and sustain. In semi-hollow body guitars, you’ll typically see two common bridge types:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Trapeze Tailpieces</strong>: Often found on vintage and classic models, trapeze tailpieces add a retro aesthetic and give the guitar a slightly different feel and resonance. They’re known for their unique, airy tone that appeals to fans of vintage-style guitars.</li>

<p>The quality of the hardware—tuners, bridge, tailpiece—affects more than just tuning stability. </p>



<p>Good hardware improves the overall feel and playability of the guitar, ensuring that it stays in tune and responds well to your playing style. </p>



<p>Upgrading components like tuners or the bridge can significantly enhance a guitar’s performance, making it easier to play and more enjoyable over time.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading has-medium-font-size" style="letter-spacing:0px"><strong>Versus Hollow Body Electric Guitars: The Jazz Cats&#8217; Delight</strong></h3>
</div>



<div class="wp-block-column is-layout-flow wp-block-column-is-layout-flow" style="flex-basis:60%">
<p>Now, let&#8217;s talk about hollow body electric guitars. These are like the sophisticated older sibling in the electric guitar family. </p>



<p>They look a bit like acoustic guitars that have been electrified (which is basically what they are). Here&#8217;s why jazz players and blues aficionados love them:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>They&#8217;ve got a deep, rich tone that&#8217;s perfect for mellower styles.</li>



<li>They can produce some beautiful, complex harmonics.</li>



<li>They&#8217;re great for clean, warm tones.</li>
</ul>



<p>The Gibson ES-175 is a classic example &#8211; it&#8217;s been the weapon of choice for jazz legends like Pat Metheny. But you can use it for rock and metal too; Josh Homme recorded Songs For The Deaf on the Epiphone version (it costs like $300). </p>
