<h2 class="wp-block-heading has-text-align-center" style="letter-spacing:-1px"><strong>What is An Extended Range Guitar?</strong></h2>



<figure class="wp-block-image alignwide size-full"><img decoding="async" width="1200" height="800" src="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Epiphone-Matt-heafy-Origins.png" alt="Epiphone Matt Heafy origins" class="wp-image-8324" title="" srcset="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Epiphone-Matt-heafy-Origins.png 1200w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Epiphone-Matt-heafy-Origins-600x400.png 600w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Epiphone-Matt-heafy-Origins-300x200.png 300w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Epiphone-Matt-heafy-Origins-1024x683.png 1024w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Epiphone-Matt-heafy-Origins-768x512.png 768w" sizes="(max-width: 1200px) 100vw, 1200px" /></figure>



<p>Extended Range Guitars (ERGs) are guitars with more than the standard six strings – you can have 7, 8, and now 9 string guitars. </p>



<p>These instruments have been around for centuries in various forms, but their modern resurgence has sparked new creativity across many genres.</p>



<p>ERGs trace back to the Renaissance, with instruments like the theorbo and lute pushing the boundaries of what stringed instruments could do. </p>



<p>Fast forward to the 20th century, and guitar innovators started experimenting with extra strings, leading to today&#8217;s popular ERGs with seven, eight, or even nine strings. </p>



<p>Brands like Ibanez, Schecter, and ESP have helped bring these instruments into the mainstream.</p>



<p>From jazz to metal, guitarists are using ERGs to expand their tonal range and explore new sounds.</p>



<p>These guitars offer more than the standard six strings, letting players explore deeper, more complex sounds. </p>



<p>Whether you&#8217;re into metal, jazz, or just love experimenting with new tones, ERGs open up a world of creative possibilities.</p>



<p class="has-main-color has-text-color has-base-font-size">In this guide, we&#8217;ll break down everything you need to know about ERGs—how they started, why they matter, and which ones are worth checking out. </p>



<p class="has-main-color has-text-color has-base-font-size">No matter your skill level, you&#8217;ll find tips to help you get the most out of these powerful instruments. </p>



<p class="has-main-color has-text-color has-base-font-size">Ready to jump in? Let’s get started.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading has-medium-font-size" style="letter-spacing:0px"><strong>Wider Tonal Range</strong></h3>
</div>



<div class="wp-block-column is-layout-flow wp-block-column-is-layout-flow" style="flex-basis:60%">
<p>Extended-range guitars (ERGs) offer you access to a broader tonal range than standard 6-string guitars, allowing you to hit those deeper, lower notes that are perfect for genres like metal, jazz, and experimental music. </p>



<p>For example, a 7-string guitar adds a low B string, giving you an extended bass range that’s crucial for the heavy, down-tuned riffs found in metal subgenres like djent and deathcore. </p>



<p>Bands like <strong>Meshuggah</strong> and <strong>Periphery</strong> have made this sound a defining feature of their music, relying on that extra string to add crushing weight and complexity to their riffs.</p>



<p>In jazz, ERGs provide the opportunity to explore new harmonic possibilities. </p>



<p>A player like <strong>Tosin Abasi</strong> from Animals as Leaders uses 8-string guitars to blend jazz-inspired chord voicings with progressive metal, creating intricate layers of melody and rhythm that wouldn’t be possible on a standard 6-string.</p>



<p>Adding an extra string opens up entirely new creative possibilities. A 7-string or 8-string guitar not only extends your lower range but also allows for more versatility in your playing. </p>



<p>You can transition seamlessly from heavy, low-end chugs to intricate, melodic lines without switching instruments. </p>



<p>ERGs are becoming a favorite in modern metal and beyond because they push the boundaries of what a guitar can do. </p>



<p>Whether you’re experimenting with complex chord structures, tapping into new tunings, or blending genres, extended-range guitars provide the tools to expand your musical landscape.</p>



<p>Guitars like the <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/ibanez-guitars/" data-type="page" data-id="13406" data-lasso-id="268504">Ibanez RG7321</a></strong> or the <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/guitar-brands/schecter-guitars/" data-type="guitar-brands" data-id="14013" data-lasso-id="268505"><strong>Schecter Omen-8</strong> </a>are great examples of budget-friendly ERGs that deliver on both playability and tone, making them accessible for players looking to explore new sonic territories without breaking the bank. </p>



<p>Meanwhile, higher-end models like the <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/the-top-5-extended-range-guitars-for-2021-going-low/" data-type="post" data-id="2358" data-lasso-id="268506">Strandberg Boden 8</a></strong> offer ergonomic design and cutting-edge features for serious musicians pushing the boundaries of their craft.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading has-medium-font-size" style="letter-spacing:0px"><strong>Lower Tunings</strong></h3>
</div>



<div class="wp-block-column is-layout-flow wp-block-column-is-layout-flow" style="flex-basis:60%">
<p>If you’re chasing that heavy, chuggy sound, extended-range guitars (ERGs) excel in lower tunings. </p>



<p>These guitars let you tap into deep, resonant tones that regular 6-string guitars simply can’t reach. </p>



<p>With a 7-string, you can drop down to a low B or even lower, creating the thick, powerful sound that drives modern metal. </p>



<p>Think of bands like <strong>Slipknot</strong> or <strong>Korn</strong>, where those extra low notes bring an aggressive, earth-shaking presence to their music.</p>



<p>But ERGs aren’t just for metal. They’re also perfect for complex jazz harmonies, allowing you to play extended chord voicings that wouldn’t be possible on a standard guitar. </p>



<p>Jazz fusion players like <strong>Charlie Hunter</strong> use 7 and 8-string guitars to blend bass lines and melodies on a single instrument, opening up new possibilities for improvisation and composition.</p>



<p>Whether you’re hammering out crushing metal riffs or navigating intricate jazz progressions, ERGs deliver the sound and versatility you need to explore new musical territories.</p>

<p>Icons like <strong>Steve Vai</strong>, <strong>Javier Reyes</strong>, and <strong>Stephen Carpenter</strong> have fully embraced extended-range guitars (ERGs), pushing the limits of what’s possible on the instrument. </p>



<p><strong>Steve Vai</strong>, with his 7-string Ibanez Universe, pioneered the use of extended-range guitars in rock and instrumental music, expanding the guitar’s potential for complex, melodic compositions. </p>



<p><strong>Javier Reyes</strong>, of <strong>Animals as Leaders</strong>, uses 8-string guitars to blend intricate rhythms and harmonies, fusing elements of metal, jazz, and classical music into a seamless, innovative sound. </p>



<p><strong>Stephen Carpenter</strong>, from <strong>Deftones</strong>, took his heavy, downtuned riffs to the next level by incorporating 7- and 8-string guitars into his music, giving Deftones&#8217; sound an even deeper, more powerful resonance.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading has-text-align-center" style="letter-spacing:-1px"><strong>Types of Extended Range Guitars</strong></h2>

<p>Extended Range Guitars (ERGs) come in various forms, each offering unique tonal possibilities. Here’s a quick breakdown of the most popular types:</p>

<div class="wp-block-column is-vertically-aligned-stretch is-layout-flow wp-block-column-is-layout-flow" style="flex-basis:40%">
<h3 class="wp-block-heading has-medium-font-size" style="letter-spacing:0px"><strong>7-String Guitars</strong></h3>
</div>



<div class="wp-block-column is-layout-flow wp-block-column-is-layout-flow" style="flex-basis:60%">
<p>7-string guitars are the go-to for many modern metal players. Adding that low B string gives you a deeper, more powerful sound that regular 6-strings just can’t reach. </p>



<p>You’ll find these guitars everywhere in metal because they strike a perfect balance: they let you get those heavy, crushing riffs while still feeling familiar enough if you’re used to a standard guitar.</p>



<p>Take <strong>Misha Mansoor</strong> from <strong>Periphery</strong>—he’s a huge advocate of 7-strings, using models like the <strong>Jackson Juggernaut</strong> to create his signature mix of complex rhythms and soaring leads. </p>



<p><strong>Dino Cazares</strong> of <strong>Fear Factory</strong> also relies on 7-string guitars, like his <strong>Ibanez DCM100</strong>, to deliver those punishing, down-tuned riffs that have defined the band’s sound.</p>



<p>The versatility of 7-strings makes them perfect for more than just metal, too. </p>



<p><strong>John Petrucci</strong> of <strong>Dream Theater</strong> uses his <strong>Music Man Majesty 7</strong> to blend progressive metal with intricate, melodic playing. </p>



<p>Whether you’re diving into djent, progressive metal, or just want a fuller, heavier tone, 7-string guitars offer endless creative possibilities while keeping things grounded enough for you to experiment with ease.</p>


</div>
</div>



<hr class="wp-block-separator alignwide has-text-color has-border-light-color has-alpha-channel-opacity has-border-light-background-color has-background is-style-separator-thin"/>



<div class="wp-block-columns alignwide is-layout-flex wp-container-core-columns-is-layout-28f84493 wp-block-columns-is-layout-flex">
<div class="wp-block-column is-vertically-aligned-stretch is-layout-flow wp-block-column-is-layout-flow" style="flex-basis:40%">
<h3 class="wp-block-heading has-medium-font-size" style="letter-spacing:0px"><strong>8-String Guitars</strong></h3>
</div>



<div class="wp-block-column is-layout-flow wp-block-column-is-layout-flow" style="flex-basis:60%">
<p>8-string guitars take things even further by adding two extra low strings, usually tuned to B and F# or A and Eb. </p>



<p>This expands your tonal range dramatically, giving you access to deep, earth-shaking lows that are perfect for heavy styles like metal and djent. </p>



<p>These guitars are built for crushing low-end riffs that demand power and precision.</p>



<p>Look at Tosin Abasi of Animals as Leaders—he’s a master of the 8-string, using his Ibanez TAM100 to combine jaw-dropping technicality with deep, resonant tones. </p>



<p><strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/what-guitars-do-meshuggah-use/" data-type="post" data-id="843" data-lasso-id="268511">Fredrik Thordendal from Meshuggah</a></strong> helped pioneer the use of 8-strings in djent, using models like the Ibanez M80M to create their signature polyrhythmic, downtuned sound.</p>



<p>With an 8-string, you’re not just adding more low notes; you’re expanding your creative possibilities. </p>



<p>These guitars let you explore new tunings, complex chord structures, and heavier riffs that simply wouldn’t be possible on a standard guitar. </p>



<p>If you’re ready to push the limits of what a guitar can do, an 8-string is the perfect tool to take your music to the next level.</p>


</div>
</div>



<hr class="wp-block-separator alignwide has-text-color has-border-light-color has-alpha-channel-opacity has-border-light-background-color has-background is-style-separator-thin"/>

<div class="wp-block-column is-vertically-aligned-stretch is-layout-flow wp-block-column-is-layout-flow" style="flex-basis:40%">
<h3 class="wp-block-heading has-medium-font-size" style="letter-spacing:0px"><strong>9-String Guitars</strong></h3>
</div>



<div class="wp-block-column is-layout-flow wp-block-column-is-layout-flow" style="flex-basis:60%">
<p>9-string guitars are less common, but they take extended range to a whole new level. </p>



<p>With that extra string, you’re diving into ultra-low tunings that open up entirely new sonic possibilities. </p>



<p>These guitars are perfect for experimental music and players looking to push the boundaries of what’s possible on guitar.</p>



<p>For example, artists like Josh Travis of Emmure and Glass Cloud use 9-strings to create brutal, bone-crushing riffs that hit frequencies most guitars can’t touch. </p>



<p>His Legator Ninja 9 gives him the extended range to explore new depths in his music while still allowing for complex chord voicings and intricate lead work.</p>



<p>9-string guitars aren’t just about going low—they’re about expanding your entire musical vocabulary. </p>



<p>Whether you’re crafting heavy, down-tuned riffs for metal or exploring avant-garde, experimental styles, a 9-string guitar gives you the tools to create sounds that stand out and push guitar playing into uncharted territory.</p>


</div>
</div>



<hr class="wp-block-separator alignwide has-text-color has-border-light-color has-alpha-channel-opacity has-border-light-background-color has-background is-style-separator-thin"/>



<div class="wp-block-columns alignwide is-layout-flex wp-container-core-columns-is-layout-28f84493 wp-block-columns-is-layout-flex">
<div class="wp-block-column is-vertically-aligned-stretch is-layout-flow wp-block-column-is-layout-flow" style="flex-basis:40%">
<h3 class="wp-block-heading has-medium-font-size" style="letter-spacing:0px"><strong>Baritone Guitars</strong></h3>
</div>



<div class="wp-block-column is-layout-flow wp-block-column-is-layout-flow" style="flex-basis:60%">
<p>Baritone guitars are a favorite for heavier music styles because they’re tuned lower—typically a fourth or fifth lower than standard guitars. </p>



<p>This tuning gives you deeper, darker tones that are perfect for crushing riffs and heavy rhythm-focused playing.</p>



<p>Take <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/deftones-101-guide/" data-type="post" data-id="14645" data-lasso-id="268514">Stephen Carpenter from Deftones</a></strong>, who uses baritone guitars like the ESP SC-607B to create those signature, downtuned walls of sound that define the band’s heavier tracks. </p>



<p>Baritone guitars let you get those thick, rumbling low notes without needing an extra string, making them a great option for players who want more low-end power while keeping a more traditional setup.</p>



<p>Baritones are especially popular in genres like doom metal, stoner rock, and post-metal, where the focus is on deep, resonant tones that standard guitars can’t quite deliver. </p>



<p>Whether you’re locking in with the bass on rhythm sections or crafting slow, heavy riffs, a baritone guitar gives you that extra depth and darkness to make your music hit harder.</p>
