<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Try Before You Buy</strong></h3>



<p>If you can, visit your local music shop and try out a few guitars in person. It doesn’t matter if you can’t play yet; just holding the guitar will tell you a lot.</p>



<p>Here are some key things to look for:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Body Shape and Comfort</strong>: Is the guitar comfortable to hold? Try out different shapes like Strats, Les Pauls, and SGs.</li>



<li><strong>Neck Feel:</strong> Every guitar neck feels different. Some are slim and fast, while others are thicker. Find what feels right in your hand.</li>



<li><strong>Weight</strong>: Some guitars are heavier than others. Make sure the weight feels manageable for you.</li>



<li><strong>Aesthetic</strong>: Don’t underestimate the power of loving how your guitar looks. You’ll be more motivated to play something you think looks badass.</li>
</ul>



<p>Even if you’re brand new, don’t be shy—music store employees are used to helping beginners find the right guitar. </p>



<p>Tell them what you’re into, and they’ll help guide you toward something that suits your style.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Pick a Guitar That Fits You</strong></h3>



<p>Another thing to consider is choosing a guitar based on the style of music you want to play. </p>



<p>If your favorite guitarist plays a Les Paul, go ahead and check out something like an Epiphone Les Paul Standard. </p>



<p>If you’re into metal, maybe try a Kramer or Ibanez model that’s built for speed and heavy tones.</p>



<p>At the end of the day, the right guitar is the one that feels good in your hands and inspires you to play. </p>



<p>Whether that’s a classic rock icon like a Stratocaster or a high-output metal machine, trust your gut and go with what excites you.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What to Avoid with Your First Guitar</strong></h3>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-full has-custom-border"><img decoding="async" width="771" height="771" src="/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/christoper-horton.jpg" alt="Christopher Horton" class="wp-image-500" style="border-radius:20px" title="" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/christoper-horton.jpg 771w, /wp-content/uploads/2020/09/christoper-horton-300x300.jpg 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2020/09/christoper-horton-100x100.jpg 100w, /wp-content/uploads/2020/09/christoper-horton-600x600.jpg 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2020/09/christoper-horton-150x150.jpg 150w, /wp-content/uploads/2020/09/christoper-horton-768x768.jpg 768w" sizes="(max-width: 771px) 100vw, 771px" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption"><strong>That&#8217;s NOT A Gibson!</strong> This is me, back when my band was signed to a major label in the early-2000s. I toured and recorded with an Epiphone Les Paul Custom –&nbsp;it cost me $399. </figcaption></figure>



<p>Here’s a piece of advice we give all beginners: stay away from tremolo bridges (those whammy bars) for your first guitar. </p>



<p>They might look cool, but they’re a pain to deal with when you’re just learning. </p>



<p>Tuning stability is key, and tremolo bridges can be a headache, especially when you’re starting out. Stick with a fixed bridge for your first guitar—it’ll make your life easier.</p>



<p>Also, don’t fall into the trap of thinking you can only play certain genres on certain guitars. </p>



<p>Sure, some guitars are designed with a specific style in mind, but you can play anything from metal to jazz on the same guitar if it’s set up right. </p>



<p>Pick a guitar that inspires you, and don’t worry about what the “rules” say.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Final Thoughts: Finding Your (Perfect) First Guitar</strong></h2>



<p>Buying your first guitar doesn’t have to be overwhelming. Stick with reliable brands, try before you buy if you can, and remember that comfort and feel are just as important as looks and sound. </p>



<p>Whether you’ve got $200 or $500 to spend, there’s a killer guitar out there for you. </p>



<p>Me? If I were starting out again today for the first time, I&#8217;d be looking at either the <strong><a href="#Squier-Affinity-Series">Squier Affinity Series (most likely a Strat model)</a></strong> or the <a href="#Ibanez-GIO-GRG121SP"><strong>Ibanez</strong> <strong>GIO GRG121SP</strong></a>. Both are exceptional guitars for the money you&#8217;ll pay for them. </p>



<p>Whatever you go for, just make sure it’s one you’re excited to pick up every day and play — that’s what playing and sticking with it is all about.</p>
