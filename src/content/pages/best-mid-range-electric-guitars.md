<h2 class="wp-block-heading has-text-align-center has-x-large-font-size" style="letter-spacing:-1px"><strong>Things To Keep In Mind When Buying Mid-Range (Sub-$999) Guitars</strong></h2>
</div>



<div class="wp-block-group has-global-padding is-layout-constrained wp-block-group-is-layout-constrained">

<p>You&#8217;re probably wondering: <strong>Do I need to spend a thousand bucks or more to get a great guitar? </strong></p>



<p>The answer is no—especially if you&#8217;re shopping in the mid-range, where $400 to $999 can get you something outstanding.</p>



<p>Right now, mid-range guitars offer incredible value, and it’s not just for beginners. </p>



<p>Even seasoned players find gems in this price range; Jack White loves his MIM Fender guitars these days. </p>



<p>I&#8217;ve been playing for 25+ years and the mid-range market is where ALL the best options are right now. In fact, guitars in this price point have literally never been better. </p>



<p>You basically get pro-level build quality and tone for better than 80% less than a custom shop / main line model. </p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Where to Focus in the Mid-Range</strong></h3>



<p>When you hit that $400 to $999 mark, you’re looking at guitars that can easily handle studio work or live gigs without breaking a sweat. </p>



<p>Brands like Squier, PRS SE, Schecter, and Epiphone dominate this range, offering solid craftsmanship, reliable hardware, and electronics that won’t leave you hanging.</p>



<p>Here’s a pro tip: <strong>focus on build quality and materials</strong>. </p>



<p>At this price point, many guitars feature better pickups, improved electronics, and solid construction compared to their budget counterparts. </p>



<p>For example, <strong><a href="#PRS-SE-DGT">the PRS SE DGT offers top-notch craftsmanship</a></strong> with its mahogany body and maple top, dual humbuckers, and exceptional versatility. It&#8217;s designed to handle everything from crisp cleans to heavy distortion.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What to Avoid</strong></h3>



<p>In this price range, avoid guitars that cut corners on essential components like pickups or tuners. </p>



<p>Just because a guitar looks nice doesn’t mean it performs well. A flashy finish won’t save you if the tuning stability or electronics are subpar.</p>



<p>Also, think twice before diving into guitars with complex tremolo systems (like Floyd Rose bridges) unless you specifically need that feature. </p>



<p>While they can be great for advanced players, they can be a headache to maintain if you&#8217;re not used to them.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Test It Out in Person if You Can</strong></h3>



<p>Whenever possible, head to your local music shop and get your hands on a few guitars. It’s the best way to feel how a neck suits your hand, how comfortable the body is, and how the guitar resonates when you play it unplugged.</p>



<p>Here’s what to look for:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Neck profile</strong>: Does it feel comfortable for your playing style? Some necks are thinner and faster, while others are chunkier.</li>



<li><strong>Weight</strong>: Some guitars, like a Les Paul, can be quite heavy. Make sure you’re okay with the weight, especially if you plan to play standing for long periods.</li>



<li><strong>Playability</strong>: Check for smooth fret edges and a low action without fret buzz. You want a guitar that feels like an extension of your hands.</li>
</ul>
