<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="don-t-fear-the-barred-f-chord">Don’t Fear The Barred F Chord</h2>



<p>I know doing a full barre for F Major can feel impossible when you first start out. But getting it down is essential to your playing, especially if you want to play rock and metal music. </p>



<p>And while you can and will be able to get away with a two-finger F power chord, it is well worth learning to play both F Major and F Minor in their full-barred shapes (see below). </p>



<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="444" src="/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/F-Minor-and-F-Major-Guitar-Chords-1024x444.png" alt="F Minor and F Major Guitar Chords" class="wp-image-6951" title="" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/F-Minor-and-F-Major-Guitar-Chords-1024x444.png 1024w, /wp-content/uploads/2022/02/F-Minor-and-F-Major-Guitar-Chords-600x260.png 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2022/02/F-Minor-and-F-Major-Guitar-Chords-300x130.png 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2022/02/F-Minor-and-F-Major-Guitar-Chords-768x333.png 768w, /wp-content/uploads/2022/02/F-Minor-and-F-Major-Guitar-Chords.png 1034w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<p>To practice the fully barred F Major / Minor chord, spend a good 20 minutes every day switching from a G or C chord to a fully barred F Major or Minor barre chord. </p>



<p>At first, your fingers won’t know what they’re doing. </p>



<p>After a week, you’ll have it pretty much nailed. Just make it a focus of your practice every day until you have it down – do not RELY on the easy F chord shape. It doesn’t sound as good.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="the-easy-way-to-play-f-chord-on-guitar">How To Play F Chord: The No Barre Method</h2>



<p>If you don’t want to barre your F chord, there is an alternative way of playing the F Major chord, although you will have to mute out both the low and high strings. Here’s how to play an F Chord (Major) without barring.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large"><img decoding="async" width="845" height="1024" src="/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/easy-f-chord-no-barre-guitar-845x1024.png" alt="easy f chord no barre guitar" class="wp-image-6947" title=""></figure>



<p>The great thing about this F Chord shape is that you can quickly transition into a C Major chord and as anyone that plays guitar knows, C to F sounds divine (especially if you then go to G). </p>



<p>All of these chord shapes essentially happen in the same place too, so there are barely any major movements.</p>



<p>If you’re looking for a non-barre method for playing an F chord, this is the easiest and simplest one to start using. </p>



<p>This easy F chord uses four fingers, with your first and second fingers sitting on fret one (B string) and fret two on the G string. </p>



<p>Your third and fourth fingers then sit on fret three on the A and D strings. Just remember to mute your low and high E strings.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="does-easy-f-chord-sound-as-good-as-barred-f-chord">Does This Easy F Chord Sound As Good As Barred F Chord?</h2>



<p>Because you’re losing the low E string, the easy F chord does lack the punch and overall tonality of its barred big brother (the properly barred F Chord). </p>



<p>It still sounds good, though, especially when tossed in between some other open chords like a C or a G.</p>



<p>If you’re going for a heavier sound, however, you will want to use the proper barre chord version of F – it incorporates both the low E and the high E string and it delivers more low-end thanks to the low E string, and more tonal nuance. I much prefer the barred version of F than the easy version.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="what-about-playing-f-power-chords">What About Playing F Power Chords?</h2>



<p>If you cannot manage a full barre chord for your F chord, or you’re looking for a heavier, more minor sound, go with an F power chord shape instead. You can do this with use two fingers, no barring necessary. If you’re playing <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/90s-metal-bands/" data-type="post" data-id="4108" data-lasso-id="84707">faster, heavier music</a></strong> the F power chord is likely all you’ll ever need anyway.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="how-to-play-f-sus-4-chords-on-guitar">How To Play F SUS 4 Chords on Guitar</h2>



<p>If you want to add more nuance to your playing and your overall sound, incorporating SUS chords is a very simple hack to make your guitar stand out in the mix. Here’s how to play a variety of F SUS 4 chords on the <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/22-frets-vs-24-frets-explained/" data-lasso-id="120182">guitar</a>.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="731" src="/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Play-The-F-Sus-4-Chord-1024x731.png" alt="how to play The F Sus 4 Chord" class="wp-image-6948" title=""></figure>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="what-is-an-f-sus-4-chord">What is an F SUS 4 Chord?</h2>



<p><strong>An F SUS 4 chord contains the notes F, Bb, and C, whereby you take the 1 (the root note),m the fourth, and the fifth notes of the major scale to create a chord. </strong></p>



<p><strong>This effectively creates an F Major chord shape and the omission of the 3rd and 4th give it its “suspended” feel, hence the name SUS = suspended.</strong></p>



<p>SUS chords like the F SUS 4 Chord are great for adding tension to your playing. To relieve the tension, you simply resolve back to the F Major chord. </p>



<p>For instance, <a data-lasso-id="120217" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/10-beautiful-chord-progressions-for-guitar-no-theory-required/">try this chord progression:</a> <strong>F Major &gt; F SUS 4 &gt; F Major </strong>. Sounds good, right? Nice and nuanced and packed with tension. This is exactly what SUS chords are designed for.</p>

<p>This section delves into the various incarnations of the F chord, providing insights and techniques to broaden your guitar repertoire and enhance your musical expression.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="htoc-learning-the-f-barre-chord-is-tricky-but-practice-it-daily-and-you-ll-have-it-down-in-no-time">Learning The F Barre Chord is Tricky But Practice It Daily And You&#8217;ll Have It Down In No Time</h2>



<p>The F Major chord is a chord shape that usually messes with beginners quite a bit. You’re basically playing right next to the nut, so this can make things trickier –&nbsp;the frets feel larger and the tension is higher. </p>



<p><strong><a data-lasso-id="84706" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/d-standard-tuning-guide-how-to/" data-type="post" data-id="3692">Making a barre chord</a></strong> this high up is always tricky for beginners.</p>



<p>And this is annoying because one of the greatest sounds your guitar can make is a properly barred F Major chord –&nbsp;it sounds glorious. </p>



<p>Being able to properly play an F Major chord is something you’re just going to have to get used it – it does take practice but it is fairly easy to lock into your muscle memory.</p>



<p>But don&#8217;t feel bad. Even the greats –&nbsp;like Billy Gibbons – struggled getting to grips with the F chord. </p>



<p>But a barre chord isn’t the ONLY way to play an F Chord. Oh no, there are quite a few different ways to play an F chord on the guitar, as well as loads of variations like F power chords and F SUS chords too. </p>



<p>In this guide, we’ll outline ALL the different ways to play an F Chord on your guitar.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="Songs_That_Use_F_Chord_">Songs That Use F Chord </h2>

<p>This way, you’ll learn the F chord and a few new songs in the process. Double bonus!</p>



<p>Plenty of EPIC tunes use the F Chord. Nearly all modern rock and metal will have an F note at some point, though these will usually be power chords which are a lot easier to play as you only need to two fingers. </p>
