<p><strong>The Matrix 4 trailer just dropped and the main song used in it is Hunter S Thompson Favorite, White Rabbit by Jefferson Airplane</strong></p>



<hr class="wp-block-separator is-style-wide"/>



<p>It’s been a while in the making, but the Matrix 4 trailer has finally dropped, revealing tidbits of information about what we can expect from the fourth – and potentially last – installment of The Matrix.</p>



<p>Directed by Lana Wachowski and written by David Mitchell (not the one from Peep Show), Aleksandar Hemon, and Wachowski, the fourth installment in the Matrix universe features plenty of new faces, as well as a more psychedelic soundtrack.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What’s The Song In The Matrix 4 Trailer?</strong></h2>



<p>If you’re not down with ‘60s psychedelic music, you probably have no idea who the song is by in the Matrix 4 trailer. It’s actually a pretty well-known, classic tune by <strong>Jefferson Airplane </strong>called <strong><em>White Rabbit.</em></strong></p>

<p>Released in 1967, White Rabbit was written by Grace Slick while she was still in her previous band, <strong><em>The Great Society</em></strong>. Slick left <strong><em>The Great Society</em></strong>, joined <strong><em>Jefferson Airplane</em></strong>, and brought two of her tracks with her, <strong><em>White Rabbit</em></strong> and <strong><em>Somebody To Love</em></strong>.</p>



<p>The song, <strong><em>White Rabbit</em></strong>, is based on Lewis Carroll’s Alice’s Adventures in Wonderland. But due to the nature of the lyrics, as well as Lewis’ story, the song kind of became the official soundtrack of the counter culture in the 1960s.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What Do The White Rabbit Lyrics Mean?</strong></h2>



<p>Slick said the song was about expanding your mind, not necessarily with drugs but with books and adventure –&nbsp;it was about being curious. Slick also stated on record that the song was inspired by fairy tales and how they presented a warped version of reality to young girls.</p>



<p>The idea that they would meet or be saved by prince charming, for instance, irritated her. But with Alice in Wonderland, there was no Prince Charming. Alice was in a strange and weird place, and the only choice she had was to press on and explore.</p>



<p>The lyric “feed your head” which many interpreted as a reference to drugs, notably, magic mushrooms and LSD, was actually about reading books – although the drug thing works just as good.</p>
