<p>While it can be daunting at first glance, learning a DAW like <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/what-is-cubase/" data-type="post" data-id="12265" target="_blank" rel="noopener" data-lasso-id="245039">Cubase </a></strong>can be an easy experience. Focusing on how to do simple functions like pull up your plugins, and creating a new track are simple and rewarding. You should absolutely approach something like <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/what-is-cubase/" data-lasso-id="251751">Cubase</a> as an instrument, one that will take time and practice.</p>



<p>Building a home studio has never been easier or more affordable, artists can make entire albums without leaving their house. So is Cubase 12 good for beginners that are trying to start their own home studio, and begin sharing their music? Absolutely!</p>

Among the myriad of options, Cubase stands as a powerhouse DAW that has been empowering artists for decades. In this article, we will explore why learning a DAW like Cubase 12 is an excellent idea for beginners, especially for those setting up their home studios.</p>

It can be as simple as recording a few tracks for a demo, or as complex as mixing/mastering an entire professional album.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="the-user-friendly-interface">The User-Friendly Interface</h2>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="683" src="/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Is-Cubase-12-Good-for-Beginners-1-1024x683.png" alt="Is Cubase 12 Good for Beginners" class="wp-image-12398" title="" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Is-Cubase-12-Good-for-Beginners-1-1024x683.png 1024w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Is-Cubase-12-Good-for-Beginners-1-600x400.png 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Is-Cubase-12-Good-for-Beginners-1-300x200.png 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Is-Cubase-12-Good-for-Beginners-1-768x512.png 768w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Is-Cubase-12-Good-for-Beginners-1.png 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<p>One of the main reasons artists choose one DAW over another is the user interface. By far, the UI is going to be the &#8220;make or break&#8221; feature of any DAW, especially when it comes to beginners. If the interface is too complicated, crowded, or hard to understand? It can be too overwhelming for a beginner.</p>



<p>Cubase 12 has an easy-to-use interface, but it doesn&#8217;t lack any advanced features that you will need later on, as your skills improve. This makes the DAW easy to jump in to in the beginning, with most advanced features<em> initially </em>out of the way.</p>



<p>As a beginner, the learning curve of a new DAW can seem difficult. However, Cubase 12 excels in providing a user-friendly interface that eases the transition into music production:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Project Setup:</strong> Cubase 12&#8217;s project setup is straightforward, allowing beginners to create new projects with ease. Users can choose from various project templates tailored for different music styles, making it easier to get started.</li>



<li><strong>Track Creation:</strong> Adding tracks in Cubase 12 is a breeze, with a simple drag-and-drop functionality. Whether it&#8217;s audio, MIDI, or instrument tracks, beginners can swiftly arrange and manage their projects.</li>



<li><strong>Transport Controls: </strong>The transport controls, such as play, stop, and record, are conveniently placed at the top of the interface, ensuring seamless control during recording and playback. This includes tempo control as well.</li>



<li><strong>Inspector and MixConsole:</strong> The Inspector and MixConsole provide a wealth of information and control over individual tracks and the overall mix, ensuring an intuitive mixing and editing experience.</li>
</ul>



<p>One of the most valuable tools that Cubase 12 features is the Project Templates. You can make your own templates, or choose from any of the presets. This allows the user to custom-tailor a template for your needs, whether it is a single track or full MIDI project.</p>



<p>If you have another DAW or freeware option that you have been making demos with, you can easily drag and drop those files into Cubase. Here, you can fine-tune scratch tracks and even phone recordings! This is a great way to get down your music/riff ideas quickly, before you forget them.</p>



<p>The basic features of Cubase 12 are easy to grasp, and there are many<strong><a href="https://youtu.be/ubR-xvLPaY4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" data-lasso-id="245041"> tutorials on YouTube </a></strong>that can get you started on your journey. As you get more familiar with the program, the more advanced features will become important, especially MIDI editing.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="feature-rich-functionality">Feature-Rich Functionality</h2>



<p>Once you get settled in with the basic functions of Cubase 12, you will eventually start to delve into the more advanced features. These are features that many DAWs share, and Cubase 12 makes it very easy and accessible for beginners and intermediate users alike.</p>



<p>Despite its beginner-friendly interface, Cubase 12 is packed with advanced features that cater to all skill levels. Let&#8217;s explore some of its standout functionalities:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>MIDI Editing:</strong> Cubase 12&#8217;s MIDI editing tools are intuitive and powerful. The Key Editor allows for easy manipulation of MIDI notes, including adjusting velocity and creating complex musical arrangements.</li>



<li><strong>Audio Editing:</strong> Editing audio is made simple in Cubase 12 with the Sample Editor. Users can perform precise cutting, copying, and time-stretching of audio clips to achieve seamless audio edits.</li>



<li><strong>MixConsole: </strong>The MixConsole provides a comprehensive mixing environment, with flexible routing, advanced automation, and a wide range of audio effects. Beginners can achieve polished and professional-sounding mixes with ease.</li>



<li><strong>:</strong> Cubase 12 offers an impressive collection of virtual instruments and effects that cater to various musical genres. From realistic emulations of acoustic instruments to cutting-edge synthesizers, users can explore a world of sonic possibilities.</li>
</ul>



<p>These features are where the magic of Cubase 12 really takes off, and they are tools that professional producers use all the time. These are all very important features to learn how to utilize, and the simple UI makes it a breeze to start using these features on your tracks.</p>



<p><strong>MIDI Editing </strong>is a very important tool these days, since so many artists use MIDI-based instruments to create songs. A good example is programming drums, which has become a huge tool for home studios that cannot be loud in a domestic environment with a &#8220;real&#8221; drum set. But MIDI can also control synths, and other instruments.</p>



<p>When it comes to <strong>Audio Editing</strong>, Cubase 12 makes this very easy to grasp as well. Learning to cut, copy, and paste your tracks will save you time. Especially when it comes to parts that repeat in a song, or samples that you have chosen to place in the mix.</p>



<p><strong>VST Instruments and Effects</strong> are a staple of Cubase 12, and every version of Cubase since the very beginning has virtual instruments. The original &#8220;Cubit&#8221; program was a MIDI editor, and it has always been a staple of Cubase. However, the 12th edition has more instruments and plugins than ever! </p>



<p>Let&#8217;s take a look at these plugins and programs built-in to Cubase 12 that make the DAW such a powerful studio ecosystem. These are powerful tools for a beginner, but with a little practice, you can unleash your creativity in a multitude of applications.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="included-plugins-for-professional-sounds">Included Plugins for Professional Sounds</h2>

<p>This is exactly what separates Cubase from other DAWs, and these plugins offer a full ecosystem that can take beginners from simple demos to fully-realized songs with professional results. The included Cubase plugins actually rival many expensive 3rd party products.</p>



<p>This is a great example of how Cubase can grow with you from beginner, intermediate, and eventually professional skill sets. So while these features may not seem important for the beginner user, they will eventually be very important to your final product, and overall mixes.</p>



<p>Cubase 12 comes equipped with high-quality plugins that empower users to create professional-sounding songs without the need for additional purchases:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>HALion Sonic SE:</strong> This powerful virtual instrument offers a wide range of sounds and instruments, providing a solid foundation for music production. Modeled after analog synths, with tons of expansions.</li>



<li><strong>Groove Agent SE:</strong> Ideal for beat making and rhythm creation, Groove Agent SE offers a plethora of drum kits and grooves that inspire creativity.</li>



<li><strong>Voxengo CurveEQ:</strong> This versatile EQ plugin allows users to shape the tonal balance of their tracks, achieving a polished and balanced mix.</li>
</ul>



<p>These are just a few of the advanced tools at your disposal, and each one is included in Cubase. The <strong>HALion Sonic </strong>synth is a virtual analog synth at heart, but also works as a sequencer, sample organizer, and it has a vast library of sounds to choose from.</p>



<p><strong>Groove Agent</strong> is a fully functional drum machine with a ton of different sounds and samples to choose from that spans many genres. The plugin also has tons of expansion packs that range from Metal to Trap music. You can program each beat, or use the loops included.</p>



<p>The Cubase 12 ecosystem allows you to create all kinds of different types of music projects, without any need for 3rd party plugins from other companies. However, you have the option to use most 3rd party plugins if you wish, and most work seamlessly with Cubase.</p>
