<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="Things_To_Keep_In_Mind?_"><strong>Things To Keep In Mind? </strong></h2>



<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="683" src="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Positive-Grid-Spark-Link-1024x683.png" alt="Positive Grid Spark Link" class="wp-image-14314" title="" srcset="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Positive-Grid-Spark-Link-1024x683.png 1024w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Positive-Grid-Spark-Link-600x400.png 600w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Positive-Grid-Spark-Link-300x200.png 300w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Positive-Grid-Spark-Link-768x512.png 768w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Positive-Grid-Spark-Link.png 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<p>Wireless guitar systems have been around for ages now, but there is a good reason why most professional players do not use them. </p>



<p>And when they do, more often than not they&#8217;re using high-end proprietary models developed by their own in-house team. </p>



<p>As a player, there&#8217;s a bunch of reasons why I don&#8217;t use wireless guitar systems. The most important ones – based on my own, personal experience – are listed out below: </p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Latency:</strong> This is the main concern for many guitarists. Even with the best wireless systems, there can be a slight delay between playing a note and hearing it through the amp. This can be imperceptible to some, but for others, it can be distracting and make it difficult to play in time with other musicians or backing tracks.</li>



<li><strong>Tone:</strong> Some players report a slight difference in tone when using a wireless system compared to a cable. This can be due to various factors like the type of wireless system, compression used in the signal transmission, and even the cable itself (longer cables can slightly roll off high frequencies). However, the difference may be subtle and not noticeable to everyone.</li>



<li><strong>Reliability:</strong> While modern wireless systems are generally reliable, there&#8217;s always a chance of signal interference, dropouts, or other issues. This can be a major concern for professional musicians who need their gear to work flawlessly during performances.</li>



<li><strong>Cost:</strong> Wireless systems can be significantly more expensive than a good quality cable, especially for high-end models with features like multiple channels or long range.</li>



<li><strong>Battery Life:</strong> Wireless systems require batteries to operate, which means you need to be mindful of their charge and have replacements on hand, especially for extended playing sessions.</li>



<li><strong>Additional Gear:</strong> Using a wireless system adds extra gear to your setup, including the transmitter, receiver, and sometimes additional cables, which can be cumbersome and add complexity.</li>
</ul>



<p>Call me old-fashioned, but I just prefer using a cable. With a cable, less stuff can go wrong. And in a live situation, this is the name of the game. You already have a million and one things to think about. Adding in an extra layer of tech, while convenient, often creates more problems than it solves.</p>
