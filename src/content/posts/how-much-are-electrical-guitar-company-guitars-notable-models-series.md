<p><strong>How much do Electrical Guitar Company guitars cost? As you’d expect, they ain’t cheap and even if you can afford one, the wait times for delivery are pretty, pretty, pretty long…</strong></p>



<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity is-style-dots"/>



<div class="wp-block-ultimate-post-wrapper ultp-block-4aabb2"><div class="ultp-wrapper-block">
<p><strong>Key Takeaways:</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Electrical Guitar Company (EGC) offers a range of handcrafted guitars and basses made from aluminum and acrylic.</li>



<li><strong>Prices for EGC guitars start at around $3,000</strong> for the aluminum-bodied Series 1 and <strong>$3,500 for the acrylic-bodied Series 2.</strong></li>



<li><strong>EGC bass guitars start at approximately $3,500</strong> for the aluminum-bodied Series 1 Bass and $4,000 for the acrylic-bodied Series 2 Bass.</li>



<li>Custom options and finishes can increase the price of EGC instruments further.</li>



<li>Order wait times vary, but EGC&#8217;s latest updates suggest around 12 months for delivery from date of payment. </li>
</ul>
</div></div>



<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity is-style-dots"/>



<p>If you want a guitar that literally no one else has, there is one brand that stands out from the crowd: The Electrical Guitar Company. Not only are its guitars made from truly unique material – aluminium and acrylic – but they’re also played by some of the best guitarists working today, from Old Man Gloom’s Aron Turner to <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/buzz-osbornes-guitar-rig-35-years-of-evolution/" data-type="post" data-id="1565" data-lasso-id="265360">King Buzzo Osbourne</a></strong>. </p>



<p>But how much will you pay for one? ECG does quite a few models, so we’ve broken down the pricing for some of its most popular models below. </p>

<ol class="wp-block-list">
<li>EGC Series Two Acrylic: Features EGC humbuckers (guitar) or bass pickups, a 3-ply plastic pickguard, and a wrinkle black powder-coated rear plate.</li>



<li>EGC 500 Generation 2 Acrylic: Equipped with balanced reverse wound/reverse polarity EGC single coils and a period-correct aluminum bridge.</li>



<li>EGC 500B Longhorn: A bass model with a 34″ scale, hard tail string-through EGC bridge, lightweight aluminum tuners, and an EGC split coil humbucker bass pickup.</li>

<li>TT2: Features EGC humbuckers (guitar) or bass pickups and a larger 3-ply pickguard.</li>



<li>EGC1000S and EGC1000A: Both models come with EGC humbuckers (guitar) or bass pickups, a polished pickguard, and a wrinkle black powder-coated rear plate.</li>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Pricing and Availability</strong></h2>



<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="683" src="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/The-Electrical-Guitar-Company-101-1024x683.png" alt="The Electrical Guitar Company 101" class="wp-image-14895" title="" srcset="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/The-Electrical-Guitar-Company-101-1024x683.png 1024w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/The-Electrical-Guitar-Company-101-600x400.png 600w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/The-Electrical-Guitar-Company-101-300x200.png 300w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/The-Electrical-Guitar-Company-101-768x512.png 768w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/The-Electrical-Guitar-Company-101.png 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<p>The aluminum-bodied Series 1 guitars start at around $3,000, while the acrylic-bodied Series 2 models begin at approximately $3,500. Custom options and finishes can further increase the price.</p>



<p>EGC&#8217;s bass guitars are also available, with prices starting at around $3,500 for the aluminum-bodied Series 1 Bass and $4,000 for the acrylic-bodied Series 2 Bass.</p>



<p>Electrical Guitar Company&#8217;s wait times for new orders can vary significantly depending on their current production schedule and the specific customizations requested for each build. </p>



<p>As of April 2024, the estimated lead time for new orders is <a href="https://www.electricalguitarcompany.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" data-lasso-id="265361">approximately 12 months</a> from the date of deposit payment until the instrument is completed and shipped. </p>



<p>It&#8217;s essential to keep in mind that this timeframe is subject to change based on the company&#8217;s workload and the complexity of the customizations requested.</p>
