<p><strong>History, band members, best album, influences –&nbsp;you&#8217;ll find it all inside our Electric Wizard 101 guide</strong></p>



<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity is-style-dots"/>



<p>Electric Wizard, hailing from Dorset, England, aren&#8217;t just a band: they&#8217;re an institution. Known for low-end fuzz, massive riffs, and a penchant for all things grim, the band has been a <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/best-doom-albums/" data-type="post" data-id="13764" data-lasso-id="263304">staple of the doom/stoner scene</a></strong> for decades. </p>



<p>But what if you&#8217;re just getting started? Where to begin with Electric Wizard? What&#8217;s the best album? Will your mom approve? Let&#8217;s get down to basics and start from the beginning.  </p>



<p>By the end of this, you&#8217;ll know enough to be totally conversational in all things pertaining to ELECTRIC WIZARD&#8230;</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Members: Past &amp; Present</strong></h2>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Jus Oborn:</strong> The mastermind behind Electric Wizard, Jus Oborn has been the band&#8217;s vocalist, rhythm guitarist, and occasional bassist since its inception. His down-tuned, fuzzy riffs and haunting vocals are the backbone of Electric Wizard&#8217;s signature sound.</li>



<li><strong>Liz Buckingham:</strong> Joining the band in 2003, Liz Buckingham&#8217;s lead guitar work has been an integral part of Electric Wizard&#8217;s sound since the release of &#8220;We Live.&#8221; Her playing style perfectly complements Oborn&#8217;s, creating a wall of heavy, psychedelic doom.</li>



<li><strong>Other Notable Members:</strong> Throughout the years, Electric Wizard has seen a rotating cast of members, including founding drummer Mark Greening, bassist Tim Bagshaw, and various other drummers and bassists who have contributed to the band&#8217;s evolution.</li>
</ul>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Discography: A Legacy of Doom</strong></h2>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="683" src="/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/electric-wizard-dopethrone-album-1024x683.png" alt="" class="wp-image-13929" title="" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/electric-wizard-dopethrone-album-1024x683.png 1024w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/02/electric-wizard-dopethrone-album-600x400.png 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/02/electric-wizard-dopethrone-album-300x200.png 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/02/electric-wizard-dopethrone-album-768x512.png 768w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/02/electric-wizard-dopethrone-album.png 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<p><strong>Genre-Defining Albums</strong></p>



<p>Electric Wizard&#8217;s discography boasts two albums that have been hailed as genre landmarks: <strong>&#8220;Come My Fanatics…&#8221; (1997) and &#8220;Dopethrone&#8221; (2000). </strong></p>



<p>These records showcase the band&#8217;s ability to craft monolithic, cannabis-fueled doom that has inspired countless acts in their wake.</p>



<p><strong>Other Notable Releases</strong></p>



<p>From their self-titled debut (1994) to their most recent opus, &#8220;Wizard Bloody Wizard&#8221; (2017), Electric Wizard has consistently pushed the boundaries of heavy music. </p>



<p>Albums like &#8220;Let Us Prey&#8221; (2002), &#8220;Witchcult Today&#8221; (2007), and &#8220;Time to Die&#8221; (2014) further solidified their status as doom metal icons.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>History: A Journey Through the Occult and Heavy Riffs 📜</strong></h2>



<p><strong>The Early Years</strong></p>



<p>Electric Wizard&#8217;s origins can be traced back to 1988 when Jus Oborn formed the band Lord of Putrefaction. </p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p>I found all the Lovecraft when I was a child and a lot of biker and scifi stuff, too. I started to dig horror books and comics from a very early age. I was also obsessed with witchcraft and satanism. I loved a book called&nbsp;<em>S</em><em>atan’s Child</em>&nbsp;by Peter Cave, and I loved satanic movies like&nbsp;<em>The Devil Rides Out.&nbsp;</em>Yeah, over and over again the same obsessions resurface. I guess these things really impacted on my consciousness because of my youth and drugs.&nbsp;It’s kept a thematic and aesthetic continuity throughout the Wizard history because of that, so that’s cool and very honest to ourselves as well.</p>
<cite>Jus Oborn</cite></blockquote>



<p>After several name changes and lineup shifts, Electric Wizard was born in 1993, taking their name from two Black Sabbath songs.</p>



<p><strong>Rise to Prominence</strong></p>



<p>With the release of &#8220;Come My Fanatics…&#8221; and &#8220;Dopethrone,&#8221; Electric Wizard gained a dedicated following and critical acclaim. </p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p>“Vinum Sabbathi” immediately takes hold and sets the tone for much of what’s to follow: drugs and the occult. “Now I’m a slave to the black drug, forced to serve this black god,” bellows Oborn. It encapsulates addiction in the form of servitude to a higher being, as well as someone’s failure to achieve a higher plane of consciousness through substances. The track is a strong opener and signals the descent into the abyss.</p>
<cite><strong><a href="https://metalinjection.net/reviews/retrospective/electric-wizard-dopethrone-retrospective" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" data-lasso-id="263305">Metal Injection</a></strong></cite></blockquote>



<p>Despite personal setbacks (drugs, getting arrested, fighting, and drinking way too much for way too long) and lineup changes, the band continued to release new material throughout the 2000s.</p>



<p>For me, they never quite topped <em>Dopethrone</em>, it was definitely a case where lightning was put in a bottle and this – as all music fans know – does not happen very often at all. </p>



<p><strong>Recent History and Legacy</strong></p>



<p>The 2010s saw Electric Wizard continue to tour and release new music, cementing their legacy as one of the most important bands in the history of doom metal. </p>



<p>With a new biography, &#8220;Come My Fanatics: A Journey Into The World Of Electric Wizard,&#8221; released in 2023, the band doesn&#8217;t look like they plan on throwing the towel in any time soon. </p>



<p>As for a new Electric Wizard album? Who knows. It&#8217;s been awhile, so I reckon we&#8217;ll get at least one more from these grim bastards before they call it a day. </p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Complete</strong> <strong>Discography</strong></h2>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Electric Wizard (1994)</strong></h3>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Release Date</strong>: 1994</li>



<li><strong>Label</strong>: Rise Above Records</li>



<li><strong>Notable Tracks</strong>: &#8220;Black Butterfly,&#8221; &#8220;Electric Wizard,&#8221; &#8220;Mourning Prayer&#8221;</li>
</ul>



<p><strong>Overview</strong>: The debut album set the tone for Electric Wizard&#8217;s journey. With its heavy, slow riffs and dark themes, it was a solid introduction to their doom metal sound, laying the groundwork for what was to come.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Come My Fanatics… (1997)</strong></h3>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Release Date</strong>: 1997</li>



<li><strong>Label</strong>: Rise Above Records</li>



<li><strong>Notable Tracks</strong>: &#8220;Return Trip,&#8221; &#8220;Wizard in Black,&#8221; &#8220;Doom-Mantia&#8221;</li>
</ul>



<p><strong>Overview</strong>: This album marked a significant step forward in the band&#8217;s evolution, with longer, more developed tracks that showcased their ability to create enveloping atmospheres of dread and heaviness.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Dopethrone (2000)</strong></h3>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Release Date</strong>: 2000</li>



<li><strong>Label</strong>: Rise Above Records</li>



<li><strong>Notable Tracks</strong>: &#8220;Funeralopolis,&#8221; &#8220;Vinum Sabbathi,&#8221; &#8220;Dopethrone&#8221;</li>
</ul>



<p><strong>Overview</strong>: Often hailed as Electric Wizard&#8217;s magnum opus, &#8220;Dopethrone&#8221; is a cornerstone of doom metal. Its colossal sound, combined with themes of witchcraft, horror, and drug use, created a cult classic that continues to influence the genre.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Let Us Prey (2002)</strong></h3>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Release Date</strong>: 2002</li>



<li><strong>Label</strong>: Rise Above Records</li>



<li><strong>Notable Tracks</strong>: &#8220;A Chosen Few,&#8221; &#8220;Master of Alchemy,&#8221; &#8220;Priestess of Mars&#8221;</li>
</ul>



<p><strong>Overview</strong>: This album continued to explore the depths of doom, though with slightly more experimental and varied song structures. It&#8217;s known for its dense, foreboding soundscapes and intricate arrangements.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>We Live (2004)</strong></h3>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Release Date</strong>: 2004</li>



<li><strong>Label</strong>: Rise Above Records</li>



<li><strong>Notable Tracks</strong>: &#8220;Eko Eko Azarak,&#8221; &#8220;We Live,&#8221; &#8220;Flower of Evil a.k.a. Malfiore&#8221;</li>
</ul>



<p><strong>Overview</strong>: Marking the introduction of a new lineup, &#8220;We Live&#8221; brought a fresh energy to the band&#8217;s sound. It retains the classic Electric Wizard heaviness but with a renewed focus on melodic elements and lyrical themes.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Witchcult Today (2007)</strong></h3>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Release Date</strong>: 2007</li>



<li><strong>Label</strong>: Rise Above Records</li>



<li><strong>Notable Tracks</strong>: &#8220;Witchcult Today,&#8221; &#8220;Satanic Rites of Drugula,&#8221; &#8220;The Chosen Few&#8221;</li>
</ul>



<p><strong>Overview</strong>: Recorded with vintage equipment for an analog sound, &#8220;Witchcult Today&#8221; is a return to form, combining catchy riffs with an occult rock influence. It&#8217;s both accessible and deeply rooted in the band&#8217;s doom metal foundations.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Black Masses (2010)</strong></h3>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Release Date</strong>: 2010</li>



<li><strong>Label</strong>: Rise Above Records</li>



<li><strong>Notable Tracks</strong>: &#8220;Black Mass,&#8221; &#8220;Venus in Furs,&#8221; &#8220;Patterns of Evil&#8221;</li>
</ul>



<p><strong>Overview</strong>: &#8220;Black Masses&#8221; continues the thematic and musical motifs of its predecessors while experimenting with tempo and dynamics. It&#8217;s a hypnotic blend of doom, psychedelic rock, and metal.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Time to Die (2014)</strong></h3>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Release Date</strong>: 2014</li>



<li><strong>Label</strong>: Spinefarm Records</li>



<li><strong>Notable Tracks</strong>: &#8220;I am Nothing,&#8221; &#8220;Time to Die,&#8221; &#8220;SadioWitch&#8221;</li>
</ul>



<p><strong>Overview</strong>: This album delves into darker, more nihilistic themes, reflected in its title. The sound is raw, unfiltered, and as heavy as ever, showcasing Electric Wizard&#8217;s ability to evolve while staying true to their core.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Wizard Bloody Wizard (2017)</strong></h3>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Release Date</strong>: 2017</li>



<li><strong>Label</strong>: Spinefarm Records</li>



<li><strong>Notable Tracks</strong>: &#8220;See You in Hell,&#8221; &#8220;Wicked Caresses,&#8221; &#8220;Hear the Sirens Scream&#8221;</li>
</ul>



<p><strong>Overview</strong>: With &#8220;Wizard Bloody Wizard,&#8221; the band aimed for a more stripped-down, rock&#8217;n&#8217;roll approach to doom. It&#8217;s a departure from their heavier, more suffocating sound, demonstrating versatility and a willingness to experiment.</p>
