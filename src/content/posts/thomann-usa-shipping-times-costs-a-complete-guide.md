<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="Navigating_the_Waters_of_Customs_and_Additional_Charges">Navigating the Waters of Customs and Additional Charges</h2>



<p>While Thomann facilitates a smooth sailing transaction process, it&#8217;s essential to be aware of possible <strong>customs duties and/or handling fees</strong> that might be imposed upon the arrival of your order in the USA. </p>



<p>It&#8217;s advisable to <strong><a href="https://www.cbp.gov/trade/basic-import-export/internet-purchases" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" data-lasso-id="251543">check the customs regulations</a></strong> in your area to avoid any unexpected charges. And, believe me, this stuff is NOT simple. </p>



<div class="custom-snippet-div"></p>



<p><strong>Here&#8217;s some things to keep in mind when importing products (even guitars or MINI controllers) into the USA: </strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>🛍️ Handling logistics for clearing goods through U.S. Customs is economical but can be complicated.</li>



<li>🚢 If not instructed, your goods might remain at the initial port, causing delays.</li>



<li>📞 U.S. Customs doesn&#8217;t notify importers about cargo arrivals; shippers or their agents should.</li>



<li>📅 Goods unclaimed within 15 days are warehoused, leading to extra charges; after six months, they might be auctioned.</li>



<li>💰 The importer is responsible for any duty on imports; duty estimates from sellers might not always be accurate.</li>



<li>📦 Payment method for duty depends on how goods were shipped: postal, courier, or freight.</li>



<li>👤 Personal use imports have fewer regulations compared to commercial use, but there are exceptions.</li>



<li>📜 Formal entries require extensive paperwork, especially for goods over $2,500 or specific textile shipments.</li>
</ul>
