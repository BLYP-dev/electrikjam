<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Should You Buy The Gibson SG Standard &#8217;61? I Like It A LOT; Here&#8217;s Why I Recommend It&#8230;</strong></h2>



<p>The <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/gibson-sg-standard-61-reviews/" data-type="post" data-id="18119">Gibson SG Standard &#8217;61 </a></strong>is a <strong>fundamental rock machine</strong> that redefined speed and tone when it replaced the Les Paul in 1961. For many, it&#8217;s the true sound of the 1960s. </p>



<p>From The Doors to the Beatles, all the greats loved the SG for its focussed clarity, lightweight nature, and easy to play neck. </p>



<p>Here are <strong>15 Reasons</strong> the SG Standard &#8217;61 remains one of the greatest guitars ever made, and why I&#8217;d use one of these over a <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/gibson-les-paul-standard-60s-review/" data-type="post" data-id="18106">Gibson Les Paul</a></strong> any day of the week (and twice on a Sunday). </p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">The Build &amp; Feel (Reasons 1-5)</h3>



<ol start="1" class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Featherweight Construction:</strong> The <strong>thin, solid Mahogany body</strong> makes the SG incredibly light, typically clocking in well <strong>under 7 lbs</strong>. This is the ultimate touring guitar—no more back pain from a heavy Les Paul!</li>



<li><strong>The Slim Taper Neck Profile:</strong> It features the &#8217;60s-spec <strong>Slim Taper neck</strong>, which is significantly thinner and faster than the &#8217;50s profile. This neck is built for lead playing and speed.</li>



<li><strong>Unbeatable Upper Fret Access:</strong> The <strong>deep double-cutaway horns</strong> grant completely <strong>unimpeded access to all 22 frets</strong>, making the top of the neck just as playable as the bottom.</li>



<li><strong>Nitrocellulose Finish:</strong> The guitar wears a hand-sprayed <strong>gloss nitrocellulose lacquer finish</strong>. This thin coating maximizes resonance and allows the wood to &#8220;breathe&#8221; and age authentically, developing that classic vintage look over time.</li>



<li><strong>Iconic Body Design:</strong> The legendary <strong>double-horned shape</strong> is instantly recognizable and perfectly ergonomic for playing both standing up and sitting down.</li>
</ol>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">The Tone &amp; Electronics (Reasons 6-10)</h3>



<ol start="6" class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Hot Alnico V Pickups:</strong> It utilizes <strong>&#8217;61 R (Rhythm) and &#8217;61 T (Treble) Burstbuckers</strong> loaded with <strong>Alnico V magnets</strong>. These magnets provide higher output and a <strong>sharper attack</strong> compared to mellower &#8217;50s PAFs.</li>



<li><strong>Aggressive Midrange Bark:</strong> The combination of the thin Mahogany body and the Alnico V pickups gives the SG a signature, <strong>cutting, aggressive midrange bark</strong> that effortlessly slices through a dense band mix.</li>



<li><strong>Musical Volume Taper:</strong> The electronics feature <strong>hand-soldered CTS 500k audio taper pots</strong>. This ensures that when you roll the volume down, the tone <strong>cleans up gradually and musically</strong>, letting you control the dirt level purely from the guitar.</li>



<li><strong>Orange Drop Capacitors:</strong> The inclusion of these premium capacitors means that when you roll the tone knob down, the sound remains <strong>musical and usable</strong>, not just muddy—critical for achieving creamy, dark lead tones.</li>



<li><strong>Excellent Sustain:</strong> Despite its lightweight, the solid Mahogany construction and the <strong>ABR-1 Tune-O-Matic bridge</strong> bolted directly into the body ensure the guitar delivers <strong>fantastic sustain</strong> for big power chords and singing lead lines.</li>
</ol>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">The Vintage Authenticity &amp; Versatility (Reasons 11-15)</h3>



<ol start="11" class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>The &#8220;Les Paul SG&#8221; Heritage:</strong> The guitar carries serious historical significance: it was originally branded the <strong>Les Paul SG Standard</strong> (1961-1963), officially taking over from the original Les Paul body.</li>



<li><strong>Vintage Deluxe Tuners:</strong> It features <strong>Kluson Deluxe tuners</strong> with green Keystone buttons, providing a classic look and reliable tuning stability.</li>



<li><strong>Period-Correct Hardware Options:</strong> You can choose your hardware destiny: the highly stable <strong>Stop Tailpiece</strong>, the authentic <strong>Sideways Vibrola</strong> (&#8220;wiggle stick,&#8221; as it originally shipped in &#8217;61), or the popular <strong>Maestro Vibrola</strong> (the &#8217;63/&#8217;64 George Harrison spec).</li>
</ol>



<ol start="14" class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Classic Cherry Finish:</strong> The guitar is finished in the iconic <strong>vintage cherry finish</strong>, which allows the beautiful Mahogany grain to show through, instantly invoking the look of the &#8217;60s era.</li>



<li><strong>Gig-Ready Protection:</strong> Every SG Standard &#8217;61 ships with a <strong>deluxe Gibson Hardshell Case</strong>, guaranteeing it is protected and road-ready straight out of the dealer&#8217;s door.</li>
</ol>



<p>Sold? You bloody well should be. This reissue is a brilliant guitar; it plays beautifully, thanks to its slim neck and the pickups run super-hot. <strong><a class="thirstylink" rel="nofollow" target="_blank" title="Gibson SG Standard &#039;61" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/recommends/gibson-sg-standard-61/" data-shortcode="true">It is expensive</a></strong> (not insane money but the kind that&#8217;ll make you pause and think about your spouse). </p>



<p>But, then again, it is a Gibson. This is the way Gibson does things. And this thing will hold its value nicely too, likely appreciating if you hold onto it for long enough. </p>



<p>For long-time players and lovers of 1960s-soaked rock and roll, however, I reckon it serves up plenty of bang for your buck. </p>
