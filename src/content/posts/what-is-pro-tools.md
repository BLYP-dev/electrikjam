<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="Pro_Tools:_The_Industry_Standard?"><strong>What is Pro Tools? </strong></h2>



<p>Pro Tools has firmly established itself as a pioneering force, revolutionizing the realm of music production, recording, and audio engineering. Pro Tools has continually evolved, setting industry standards and shaping the sonic landscapes of countless musical compositions. </p>



<p>If you have been following the world of music production for many years, then the &#8220;industry standard&#8221; was Pro Tools and a Mac. Apple made very powerful computers, that could handle the multi-tracking recording process in the early days of digital recording.</p>



<p>Avid, the maker of Pro Tools, came from two designers that attended UC Berkeley in the 80s. Evan Brooks and Peter Gotcher started with electrical engineering in school, and the two friends spent a lot of time playing around with synths, sound cards, and drum machines.</p>



<p>From that, the two friends also studied <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/best-midi-keyboards/" data-type="post" data-id="12719" data-lasso-id="252835">MIDI </a></strong>sound design. Like most people in the late 80s and early 90s, it was clear to them that analog recording was taking a new direction. Everyone from <strong>Nine Inch Nails</strong> to pop radio hits were becoming &#8220;hybrid&#8221; projects.</p>



<p>Just like most of the first <strong>DAWs</strong> on the market, Pro Tools started as a tool to control drums, and other MIDI elements to be recorded to analog tape. This is the same story we hear from every DAW company, but somehow Pro Tools came out on top.</p>



<p>From the mid-90s until the late 2010s Pro Tools was the industry standard in most studios. While that has changed quite drastically with the beginning of home studios becoming the norm, with more DAWs to choose from. Pro Tools is still relevant, and offers a ton of features. Today we are going to look at every aspect of the famous DAW.</p>



<p>Let&#8217;s embark on a journey to unravel the multifaceted world of Pro Tools, exploring its origins, evolution, standout features, and diverse applications across the music industry.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="The_Beginning_of_Pro_Tools:_How_it_All_Started"><strong>The Beginning of Pro Tools: How it All Started&#8230;</strong></h2>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="683" src="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/pro-Tools-2-1024x683.png" alt="Pro Tools" class="wp-image-13209" title="" srcset="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/pro-Tools-2-1024x683.png 1024w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/pro-Tools-2-600x400.png 600w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/pro-Tools-2-300x200.png 300w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/pro-Tools-2-768x512.png 768w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/pro-Tools-2.png 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<p><strong>Pro Tools, developed by Avid Technology, emerged in the early 1990s as one of the first digital audio workstations, heralding a groundbreaking shift from analog recording to the digital domain. Its early iterations laid the foundation for contemporary music production, offering users an unprecedented level of flexibility, precision, and creative control over their audio recordings. </strong></p>



<p><strong>Over the years, Pro Tools underwent significant advancements, integrating cutting-edge technologies and innovative features that propelled it to the forefront of the professional audio industry.</strong></p>



<p>Most DAWs started in the late 80s and early 90s as MIDI controllers, and this story is no different. The difference is that Pro Tools was the first real digital DAW that worked well, and it even used specific hardware to give your computer a &#8220;boost&#8221;.</p>



<p>While other DAWs were struggling to work on the current computers of the 90s, <strong>Evan Brooks</strong> (creator) was making outboard computer gear to get the DAW working. But we are getting way ahead of ourselves, as this story actually started much earlier.</p>



<p><strong>Evan Brooks</strong> and <strong>Peter Gotcher</strong> started as students at UC Berkeley in California. While they both studied computer science, their hobbies were music and instruments. The arrival of the <strong><a data-lasso-id="252833" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairlight_CMI" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">Fairlight CMI </a></strong>blew everyone away, as it was the most impressive sampler to ever hit the market.</p>



<p>The Fairlight is responsible for many sounds you know from the 80s. The soundtrack to &#8220;<strong>The Terminator</strong>&#8221; was mostly done with the famous keyboard-based sampler. Evan and Peter took note of this trend, and started designing drum software and replacement chips.</p>



<p>This lead to the first product by the two new engineers,<strong> Digidrums</strong>. Five different upgrade chips were available, offering different alternate drum styles. The chips, easily switchable with the original ones, enjoyed remarkable success between the<strong> Drumulator</strong> users, selling around 60,000 units overall. </p>



<p>With the release of the first Macintosh Computer in 1984, the two decided to start taking advantage of the new graphical interface to design more products. The pair engineered the &#8220;<strong>Sound Designer</strong>&#8221; software for keyboards and synths. It was the first software shared over the internet as well!</p>



<p>The problem with making a full recording system digital at this time, was the computer hardware. The two innovators moved on to creating the first &#8220;tapeless recording studio&#8221; with added computer components. The program was called<strong> Sound Tools</strong>, and it was bundled with <strong>Sound Designer</strong> software which is still available today.</p>



<p>This was the beginning of Pro Tools, and many digital options started to pop up in the market. The problem, was computer storage. Even powerful home computers had limited storage, so the need for analog tape was still prevalent into the 90s.</p>



<p>The first Pro Tools system was launched on June 5, 1991.&nbsp;But it relied on <strong>Motorola</strong> hardware, and several other components that were still experimental. To have effects like reverb and time control, you need more DSP and storage, and external parts provided this much needed power.</p>



<p>This plan was revised several times, and Evan and Peter merged with <strong>Avid</strong>, which added more funds to the company to get a final working product. For most of the 90s added hardware cards and DSP elements were needed while computer technology started to catch up.</p>



<p>The late 90s saw the migration of most studios to digital, as finally computers had caught up to the ambitions of creators. The first Billboard Top 100 single to be done with Pro Tools was &#8220;<strong>Livin&#8217; La Vida Loca</strong>&#8221; in 1999. This was the beginning of fully digital studios, and Pro Tools was the name to buy.</p>



<p>Between 2001 and 2012, Avid released a ton of different versions of Pro Tools, both for computers and Native hardware systems. At the time, it was the only DAW that could mix surround sound, and it made every other DAW pale in comparison when it came to user functions.</p>



<p>These days, computers have no issue running Pro Tools, and it is a huge player in the DAW realm. Avid has also kept up with the hardware that allows you to control the DAW. Pro Tools can be a huge investment, but it is also a powerful tool.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="Different_Versions_of_Pro_Tools:_From_Past_to_Present"><strong>Different Versions of Pro Tools: From Past to Present</strong></h2>



<p><strong>Pro Tools 1-6: Pioneering the Digital Revolution</strong></p>



<p>The initial versions of Pro Tools introduced fundamental recording and editing capabilities, enabling users to manipulate audio tracks with unprecedented precision and efficiency. With its innovative non-linear editing system and comprehensive mix automation tools, Pro Tools quickly gained popularity among recording studios and professional musicians, setting a new standard for digital audio production.</p>



<p><strong>Pro Tools 7-10: Expanding Creative Horizons</strong></p>



<p>Subsequent iterations of Pro Tools witnessed the introduction of advanced features, such as improved MIDI sequencing, comprehensive virtual instrument libraries, and enhanced signal processing capabilities. These updates facilitated a seamless integration of digital audio and MIDI production, offering users a versatile platform for crafting intricate compositions and intricate sonic textures.</p>



<p><strong>Pro Tools 11-2021: Embracing the Future of Audio Engineering</strong></p>



<p>With the advent of Pro Tools 11, Avid ushered in a new era of audio engineering, introducing groundbreaking advancements in audio processing, mixing, and virtual instrument technology. The integration of 64-bit architecture, advanced metering options, and comprehensive signal processing plugins elevated Pro Tools to unprecedented heights, solidifying its position as an industry-leading DAW for professionals and enthusiasts alike.</p>



<p><strong>Current Version: Pro Tools 2022</strong></p>



<p>The latest iteration of Pro Tools, Pro Tools 2022, represents the pinnacle of Avid&#8217;s commitment to innovation and excellence in audio production. With its state-of-the-art audio engine, immersive mixing environment, and a comprehensive suite of creative tools, Pro Tools 2022 continues to redefine the boundaries of digital audio production, empowering users to bring their creative vision to life with unparalleled precision and depth.</p>

<p><strong>Advanced Editing Capabilities</strong>:</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="What_is_Dolby_Atmos?"><strong>What is Dolby Atmos?</strong></h2>



<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-full"><img decoding="async" width="1000" height="570" src="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Dolby-Atmos-logo.webp" alt="" class="wp-image-14623" title="" srcset="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Dolby-Atmos-logo.webp 1000w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Dolby-Atmos-logo-600x342.webp 600w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Dolby-Atmos-logo-300x171.webp 300w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Dolby-Atmos-logo-768x438.webp 768w" sizes="(max-width: 1000px) 100vw, 1000px" /></figure>



<p>Dolby Atmos is an advanced audio technology developed by Dolby Laboratories that revolutionizes the audio experience by introducing a three-dimensional audio format. </p>



<p>Unlike traditional surround sound systems, which are primarily based on channels, Dolby Atmos transcends the confines of channel-based audio, offering a more immersive and spatial audio experience that envelops the listener in a multidimensional soundscape.</p>



<p>It introduces a more precise and realistic representation of audio, enabling sound engineers and content creators to craft dynamic and lifelike sound environments that transcend the limitations of conventional audio formats.</p>



<p>The key feature of Dolby Atmos lies in its object-based audio approach, which allows sound engineers to treat individual audio elements as distinct sound objects that can be precisely placed and moved within a three-dimensional space.</p>



<p>Dolby Atmos supports a wide range of playback systems, including home theater setups, cinemas, and mobile devices, enabling consumers to enjoy immersive and spatial audio content across various entertainment platforms.</p>



<p>But so far, Pro Tools and Logic are at the top of the game. Many producers feel that Dolby Atmos is the future of mixing, since more headphones and devices support the format. If you have a recent Samsung phone, then you probably have Dolby built in, and can be used with streaming services.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="Critical_Considerations"><strong>Critical Considerations&#8230;</strong></h2>



<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="683" src="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Ableton-Live-11-Skrillex-1024x683.png" alt="Ableton Live" class="wp-image-12571" title="" srcset="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Ableton-Live-11-Skrillex-1024x683.png 1024w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Ableton-Live-11-Skrillex-600x400.png 600w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Ableton-Live-11-Skrillex-300x200.png 300w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Ableton-Live-11-Skrillex-768x512.png 768w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Ableton-Live-11-Skrillex.png 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<p>When it comes to using Pro Tools as your digital audio workstation (DAW) of choice, several key considerations can significantly impact your experience and workflow. Whether you&#8217;re a novice exploring the realm of music production or a seasoned professional navigating complex audio projects, being mindful of these essential factors can help you optimize your usage of Pro Tools and streamline your creative process.</p>



<p>Yes, Pro Tools is still somewhat considered as the &#8220;industry standard&#8221; in many circles. But there are also reasons why it is not used by everyone, and this is mostly due to extremes. What we mean by extremes, is that you have to pretty much commit to Avid&#8217;s ecosystem entirely. </p>

<p>It is hard to deny the impact that Avid has made on the recording industry. There were years of studios switching to digital, and Pro Tools was the best option at the time. It was the best option at the time, and one of the only options that was viewed as &#8220;pro&#8221;.</p>



<p>DAW software has come a long way since the 90s, with Avid leading the way in most cases. Most other DAWs have taken some features from Pro Tools over the years, but that is not exactly the case in the present. Many other DAWs offer similar experiences these days, so it can be hard to choose.</p>



<p>What used to be the &#8220;industry standard&#8221; has become a more level playing field over the last decade, and choosing a DAW has never been more confusing. Pro Tools is a brand name that most people can recognize, but it is important to understand who this DAW is for, and who will use it. </p>



<p>If you plan to build a professional studio that will have active clients within the music industry, then Pro Tools is still a great choice. The hardware options alone allow the user to personalize a full studio layout, with every product that you would need for recording.</p>
