<h2 class="wp-block-heading">KEY TAKEAWAYS: </h2>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Standard 7-string guitar tuning includes an additional low B string, extending the instrument&#8217;s sonic range and making it ideal for genres like metal and progressive rock.</li>



<li>Despite the added string, all familiar chords, scales, and modes apply (in Standard Tuning), with the lower B string adding depth and heaviness to your playing.</li>



<li>Power chords on a 7-string guitar are played similarly to a 6-stringer, with the B string enhancing their sound.</li>



<li>Popular 7-string guitar tunings include <strong><a href="#Popular_-String_Guitar_Tunings">Drop A, B Standard, and variations like Drop A with G tuned to F#, Drop Ab, Bb Standard, and Drop G#</a></strong>, each offering unique sound profiles and characteristics.</li>



<li>Some less common tunings like A Standard and GDADGBE provide intriguing tonal options for experimentation and creativity.</li>
</ul>



<p></div>



<p>Whether you’ve just bought a 7-string guitar or you’re seriously considering getting one, the most important you need to get your head around is <strong>7 string guitar tuning</strong>. </p>



<p>The addition of an extra string brings more tonal possibilities but also a new batch of tunings to work worth. And if you’re used to 6-string guitars, adding an extra string into the mix can be confusing. </p>



<p>I say <strong>“can”</strong> because once you understand a few basics things, switching from <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/standard-tuning-guitar-explained-history/" data-type="post" data-id="11231" data-lasso-id="251931">EADGBE</a></strong> to <strong>BEADGBE</strong> is actually really simple. </p>



<p>And we’re gonna cover everything you need to know about 7 string guitar tuning below. Let’s dive in…</p>



<div class="custom-snippet-div"></p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="How_To_Tune_A_-String_Guitar">How To Tune A 7-String Guitar</h2>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="Standard_-String_Guitar_Tuning:_A_Deep_Dive">Standard 7-String Guitar Tuning: A Deep Dive</h3>



<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="683" src="/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/7-string-standard-tuning-1024x683.png" alt="" class="wp-image-13026" title="" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/7-string-standard-tuning-1024x683.png 1024w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/10/7-string-standard-tuning-600x400.png 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/10/7-string-standard-tuning-300x200.png 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/10/7-string-standard-tuning-768x512.png 768w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/10/7-string-standard-tuning.png 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<p>From the beefiest string to the slickest, the standard tuning for a 7-string guitar rolls out as:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>B1</li>



<li>E2</li>



<li>A2</li>



<li>D3</li>



<li>G3</li>



<li>B3</li>



<li>E4</li>
</ul>



<p>But here&#8217;s the kicker: This isn’t just about adding another string. It&#8217;s about diving deeper into the sonic spectrum. </p>



<p>By introducing a low B string beneath the conventional 6-string setup, the range is extended meaning you can get A LOT lower than you can with a 6-string. </p>



<p>And for genres like metal and progressive rock? That&#8217;s just what the doctor ordered. </p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="Why_This_Tuning_Makes_Sense">Why This Tuning Makes Sense</h3>



<p>Think of the low B string as your guitar&#8217;s secret weapon. With it you can add a much deeper, heavier sound to your riffs. But once you move past the B string, your back into 6-string territory, so you should feel right at home. </p>



<p>All of the same chords, scales and modes apply here. But you now have an extra, lower B string up top for getting down and dirty.  </p>



<p>Plus, power chords work in EXACTLY the same way on a 7-string guitar as they do on a 6-stringer. You play them the same, only with the B string adding loads of extra depth and heaviness. </p>



<p>Still unsure? Here&#8217;s some pointers on playing power chords on a 7-string guitar: </p>



<h4 class="wp-block-heading" id="Playing_Power_Chords_on_the_Low_B_String:">Playing Power Chords on the Low B String:</h4>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Position your fingers:</strong> Place your index finger on the B string (the thickest string) at any fret. This is your root note.</li>



<li><strong>Add the fifth:</strong> Press down on the E string (the next string) two frets higher than your root note with your ring finger.</li>



<li><strong>Strum:</strong> Play these two strings together to produce a power chord. For instance, if you start on the 3rd fret of the B string, you&#8217;re playing a B power chord.</li>
</ul>



<h4 class="wp-block-heading" id="Expanding_the_Power_Chord:">Expanding the Power Chord:</h4>



<p>For a fuller sound, you can add the octave:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Add the octave:</strong> Using your pinky, press down on the A string (the third string from the top) on the same fret as your ring finger.</li>



<li><strong>Strum:</strong> Play the three strings together for a richer power chord.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="Popular_-String_Guitar_Tunings">Popular 7-String Guitar Tunings</h2>



<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="683" src="/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/7-String-Guitars-The-Ultimate-Beginners-Guide-2023-1024x683.png" alt="7-String-Guitars-The-Ultimate-Beginners-Guide-2023" class="wp-image-13034" title="" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/7-String-Guitars-The-Ultimate-Beginners-Guide-2023-1024x683.png 1024w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/10/7-String-Guitars-The-Ultimate-Beginners-Guide-2023-600x400.png 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/10/7-String-Guitars-The-Ultimate-Beginners-Guide-2023-300x200.png 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/10/7-String-Guitars-The-Ultimate-Beginners-Guide-2023-768x512.png 768w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/10/7-String-Guitars-The-Ultimate-Beginners-Guide-2023.png 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<p>Once you get the basics down in Standard Tuning on your 7-string guitar and you’re used to playing with that extra string, you can then start messing around with alternate tunings.&nbsp;</p>



<p>As with 6-string guitars, the possibilities for alternative tunings on a 7-string guitar are extremely varied. You can basically tune your guitar however you like (if you REALLY know what you’re doing).&nbsp;</p>



<p>Again, though, if you’re a beginner or just getting started it is probably best to stick to the most popular 7-string guitar tunings.&nbsp;</p>



<p>These tunings – broken down below – are tried and tested, so you don’t have to worry about anything. They all sound good and they all have their own, unique nuances. </p>



<div class="custom-snippet-div"></p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="Alternate_-String_Guitar_Tunings">Alternate 7-String Guitar Tunings</h2>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="Drop_A_AEADGBE">Drop A (AEADGBE)</h3>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Characteristics and Sound Profile:</strong> Drop A tuning provides a deep, resonant sound, emphasizing the lower frequencies. The dropped A string allows for heavier riffs and easier power chord formations, especially on the lower strings.</li>



<li><strong>Genres and Artists:</strong> This tuning is a favorite among metal and progressive rock musicians. Bands like Periphery, Animals as Leaders, and Meshuggah have tracks that utilize this tuning to its full potential.</li>
</ul>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="B_Standard_BEADGBE">B Standard (BEADGBE)</h3>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Characteristics and Sound Profile:</strong> B Standard is the most common tuning for 7-string guitars. It maintains the standard tuning of a 6-string guitar while adding a low B, offering a seamless transition for those familiar with 6-string configurations.</li>



<li><strong>Famous Songs and Bands:</strong> Dream Theater&#8217;s John Petrucci and Steve Vai are known to use B Standard tuning in some of their compositions. Songs like &#8220;The Mirror&#8221; by Dream Theater showcase the depth this tuning can provide.</li>
</ul>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="Drop_A_with_the_G_tuned_to_F">Drop A with the G tuned to F#</h3>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Why Some Guitarists Prefer This Variation:</strong> Tuning the G to F# in Drop A provides a more symmetrical feel across the strings, especially for those who play scales and solos. It offers a unique tonal palette and can make certain chord voicings and transitions smoother.</li>
</ul>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="Drop_Ab">Drop Ab</h3>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>The Unique Sound and Its Applications:</strong> Drop Ab offers a slightly higher pitch than Drop A, yet still maintains a heavy and robust sound. It&#8217;s a middle ground for those looking for depth without going too low.</li>
</ul>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="Bb_Standard">Bb Standard</h3>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Overview and Its Popularity:</strong> Bb Standard tuning is a half-step down from B Standard. It&#8217;s favored by guitarists looking for a slightly darker and warmer tone. It&#8217;s less common than B Standard but offers a unique sonic character.</li>
</ul>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="Drop_G">Drop G#</h3>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Characteristics and Its Niche:</strong> Drop G# takes the heaviness a notch further. It&#8217;s particularly favored by djent and progressive metal guitarists for its ultra-low tonality. It&#8217;s niche, but for those seeking a truly deep sound, it&#8217;s a go-to.</li>
</ul>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="A_Standard_and_GDADGbe">A Standard and GDADGBE</h3>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Exploring These Less Common But Intriguing Tunings:</strong> A Standard is a full step down from B Standard, providing a warm and rich tonality. GDADGbe, on the other hand, is an open tuning that offers unique chord voicings and is great for slide guitar techniques and ambient soundscapes.</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="Metal_and_Its_Many_Faces">Metal and Its Many Faces</h3>



<p>Here&#8217;s the deal: When it comes to the world of metal and its myriad sub-genres, two tunings reign supreme &#8211; Drop A and B Standard. </p>



<p>Why? They pack a punch. The depth and heaviness they bring to the table are unmatched, making them the go-to for metalheads worldwide. </p>



<p>Whether you&#8217;re chugging out riffs or diving into intricate solos, these tunings are the backbone of the metal sound.</p>

<p>But here&#8217;s a nugget of gold: <strong>Dream Theater&#8217;s iconic sound, especially in tracks like &#8220;The Mirror&#8221;, owes a lot to the B Standard tuning (BEADGBE)</strong> which helps create its powerful and melodious tone. </p>



<p>For rock and progressive enthusiasts, this tuning is a treasure trove of sonic possibilities.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="Experimental_and_Alternative:_The_Road_Less_Traveled">Experimental and Alternative: The Road Less Traveled</h3>



<p>For those who like to tread off the beaten path, tunings like GDADGbe beckon. It&#8217;s not mainstream, and that&#8217;s the beauty of it. </p>



<p>This open tuning offers a fresh palette of sounds, perfect for creating ambient landscapes and ethereal melodies. </p>



<p>Whether you&#8217;re into slide guitar techniques or crafting unique chord progressions, this tuning is a gateway to uncharted territories.</p>



<p><strong>Bottom line?</strong> Your tuning sets the stage for your sound. Interesting fact: <strong>this is why Dillinger Escape Plan opted to play in Standard Tuning</strong> – everybody else was down-tuning, so the tonal qualities of Standard Tuning gave the band&#8217;s sound a distinct edge. </p>
