<h3 class="wp-block-heading">The Dumbest Guitar Gadgets Of 2020: WARNING!</h3>



<p><strong>Before we begin, let me throw a little disclaimer out for everyone to read</strong>: These gadgets are not exactly &#8220;gadgets&#8221; as much as they are purely gimmicks. As guitarists, we are steeped in a kind of weird culture that is full of bias, and tradition. We often scoff at a new product that could <em>possibly</em> be beneficial and go about sniffing corks.</p>



<p>I mean, look <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/the-line-6-hx-stomp/" data-lasso-id="5749">how we treated  Line 6</a>  all of these years. Detestable!</p>



<p>But that&#8217;s the thing about <em>these</em> products. They have no real benefit when it comes to honing your skills as a guitarist. They are simply there to make a quick buck and prey on your buying impulse. These products are not beneficial, and never will be.</p>



<p>So the criteria for this list goes as such:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>It must be basically useless</strong></li><li><strong>It is obviously a gimmick</strong></li><li><strong>It tries to circumvent actual practice and routines</strong></li><li><strong>It is suspiciously cheap</strong></li><li><strong>No Guitar Teacher on earth would suggest these</strong></li></ul>



<p>Now that we have all of that out of the way, let&#8217;s take a step back and have a few laughs. Because at the end of the day, these are silly products. No need to handle them with kid gloves. They are self-aware silly, even. I will save the best for last!</p>



<hr class="wp-block-separator"/>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Pykmax UP: An Honorable Mention?</h2>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="576" src="/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Pikmax-1024x576.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-1315" title="" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Pikmax-1024x576.jpg 1024w, /wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Pikmax-600x338.jpg 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Pikmax-300x169.jpg 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Pikmax-768x432.jpg 768w, /wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Pikmax.jpg 1280w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /><figcaption>Even Ola looks a little confused&#8230;</figcaption></figure>



<p>Ok, this one straddles the line a little bit, which is why it&#8217;s going first. Does that make it completely useless, or just an honorable mention? I will let you decide. I know how<em> I feel</em> about it.</p>



<p>PykMax UP solves the problem of you dropping your pick. Or does it?</p>



<p>The advertisement says:</p>



<p>&#8220;<em>Ergonomic grip feels super comfortable in your hand &#8211; great for beginners and experts alike. Play comfortably for hours, enhance your performance, and never drop a pick!</em>&#8220;</p>



<p>I don&#8217;t know&#8230; picks <strong>will</strong> fly out of your hand when you are playing. It&#8217;s a natural phenomena for every <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/5-best-christmas-presents-for-guitar-players/" data-lasso-id="29817">guitar player</a>. It even happens to the pros. </p>



<p>What you do, is grab another one real quick. Most guitarists have a stash of picks near the guitar, ON the guitar, on the mic stand, or even on top of their amps. So there really isn&#8217;t a problem here.</p>



<p>I would go as far as to say that recovering on stage after you have dropped a pick is a tiny artform! Everyone has a different way of doing it, without being too obvious or stopping the song. I simply play with my fingers until I can grab another pick. Usually taped to the back of my headstock.</p>



<p>This is an idea I stole from <strong>Steve Vai</strong>. If you watch his performances, you&#8217;ll notice he has four or five picks attached to the back of his headstock. Although these days, neither Steve or myself have a problem with dropping picks. </p>



<p>Eventually it becomes something that might happen every so often, I suppose. For me, it would happen maybe <em>once</em> during a stretch a shows. The audience never noticed, and it didn&#8217;t ruin my night or anything. It certainly didn&#8217;t ruin guitar playing forever for me.</p>



<p>&#8220;<em>Even if you have trouble holding a regular guitar pick, Pykmax can help you start playing guitar again.</em>&#8220;</p>



<p>OK. Now this is where I have an issue calling this product outright &#8220;dumb&#8221;. <strong>You may have an injury or disability that no longer allows you to hold a pick. </strong>If that is the case, then it makes total sense to use a gadget like this to help you. This is the only reason I am not giving this product the Hell it deserves.</p>



<p>Otherwise, I feel like it is one of many products that act only as a gimmick. If you are a beginner, and start your playing experience with something like this, you may never hold a pick in a natural way that allows for accuracy and feel.</p>



<p>So PykMan, you get a half-pass. It&#8217;s a gimmick, but it <em>can</em> be useful under the right circumstances. Definitely not one of the dumbest guitar gadgets. Now that I have that out of the way, let&#8217;s get on to the really ridiculous stuff.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Riff BANDZ: The Ultimate Shortcut to Playing Better</h2>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="768" src="/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Riff-Bandz-1024x768.jpg" alt="Dumbest Guitar Gadgets" class="wp-image-1316" title="" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Riff-Bandz-1024x768.jpg 1024w, /wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Riff-Bandz-600x450.jpg 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Riff-Bandz-300x225.jpg 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Riff-Bandz-768x576.jpg 768w, /wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Riff-Bandz-1536x1152.jpg 1536w, /wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Riff-Bandz.jpg 2048w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /><figcaption>Umm&#8230;no.</figcaption></figure>



<p>When learning piano, hand posture is very important. Piano teachers will often put a coin on the top of your hand, to make sure the hand isn&#8217;t flopping about when playing. This ensures economy of movement in your hand, so you can hit the keys accurately.</p>



<p>Riff Bandz is <em>NOT</em> that kind of thing. The product is basically four rubber bands connected together that go over your fingers, limiting your movement and making it harder to reach frets. This isn&#8217;t prime &#8220;dumbest guitar gadgets&#8221;. But it definitely ranks high.</p>



<p><em>&#8220;Riff Bandz work for the same reason that swinging a weighted bat before stepping up to the plate works. Train with resistance, and when the resistance is removed the movement feels lighter and easier. Strengthen your fingers by using Riff Bandz resistance bands while you practice and play.&#8221;</em></p>



<p>It sounds like a pretty good idea right? I mean, baseball players do it!</p>



<p>You know what also helps you to strengthen your fingers and extend your range? <em>Playing your guitar.</em> There is no excuse to not have a proper practice routine set up that includes stretching your fingers. Just by sheer playing alone, you can achieve the same results.</p>



<p>It&#8217;s like <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/the-complete-guide-to-7-string-guitar/" data-lasso-id="428">switching to a 7 string guitar</a>, or a baritone guitar. You may struggle at first, but in a couple of days, you will get used to it. No gimmicks needed! Your fingers will adapt all by themselves.</p>



<p>Not to mention, it is also important to be playing the right guitar for you. Scale length is very important, as well as string gauge. This is all comes naturally, as you become more proficient. </p>



<p>We can even bring anatomy into this! Your fingers don&#8217;t really have a lot of muscles to begin with. If you are playing guitar properly, then you know that tendons play a much bigger role in <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/how-to-find-fretboard-notes-guitar/" data-lasso-id="429">how you approach the neck and fretboard</a>. Your fingers don&#8217;t get jacked like your arms when you hit the gym!</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The Dumbest Guitar Gadgets Of 2020: I Think We Are Done Here.</h2>



<p>Look, this is ultimately satire. I can&#8217;t help but giggle a little bit when I see these products. Like I said in the beginning, they prey on your impulses and you may even buy them out of curiosity. It is easy to poke fun at them. You find them everywhere on <a href="https://amazon.com" class="rank-math-link" data-lasso-id="434" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">Amazon</a> and <a href="https://www.wish.com/" class="rank-math-link" data-lasso-id="435" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">Wish</a>.</p>



<p>And believe me, there is no irony lost on my part. I am coming from years of practice and playing. I am not a beginner nor am I struggling to play guitar. But I remember those first days fondly, when I was at the very beginning. Every day I got a tiny bit better,<a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/learning-guitar-guide-what-to-expect/" class="rank-math-link" data-lasso-id="436"> and I was proud of it.</a></p>



<p>But there is a valuable lesson that is written in between the lines of all of this satire: <strong>There is no replacement for actual practice and good habits that come with patience and time.</strong> There are no shortcuts to proficiency. Anyone trying to sell you the &#8220;easy way&#8221; is probably just trying to make a quick buck.</p>



<p>That can apply to all aspects of life. Experience comes at a cost and in the end you are only cheating yourself, by trying to cheat. Learning is fun and rewarding! Never deprive yourself of the experience or sell yourself short.</p>
