<p><strong>Whether you&#8217;re using Cubase, Logic, Ableton or Reaper, there&#8217;s a baseline of spec you&#8217;ll need on your computer to record at home&#8230;</strong></p>



<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity is-style-dots"/>


<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity is-style-dots"/>



<p>If you want to record music at home, you’re going to need a computer. But not just any, old computer. <strong><a href="/choosing-a-music-production-pc-101/" data-type="post" data-id="15540" data-lasso-id="270300">You need a decent CPU, for starters, and RAM</a></strong> – AKA memory – and plenty of it. </p>



<p>Now, this doesn’t mean you need a beast of a rig to get started. You don’t have to spend thousands, and the computer you own right now might be all you need.&nbsp;</p>



<p>I’ve been recording music professionally, in varying states of financial dire straits for over two decades, so I know all too well what can be done with not much money.</p>



<p>This post outlines the absolute basic setup you’ll need to run a DAW and record music at home, either on a PC or a Mac. Let’s freaking go…&nbsp;</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="htoc-what-the-heck-is-a-daw-anyway"><strong>What the Heck is a DAW Anyway?</strong></h2>



<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-full"><img decoding="async" width="1000" height="562" src="/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/what-is-cubase.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-13791" title="" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/what-is-cubase.jpg 1000w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/01/what-is-cubase-600x337.jpg 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/01/what-is-cubase-300x169.jpg 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/01/what-is-cubase-768x432.jpg 768w" sizes="(max-width: 1000px) 100vw, 1000px" /></figure>



<p>First things first—if you&#8217;re new to this, you might be wondering what a DAW actually is. </p>



<p>In simple terms, a <strong><a href="/category/daws/" data-lasso-id="270301">DAW is software that lets you record</a></strong>, edit, mix, and produce audio files. Think of it as your virtual studio where all the magic happens.</p>



<p>And there&#8217;s loads of options: Cubase, Pro Tools, Ableton, Logic Pro, FL Studio, Garage Band – the list goes on and on. </p>



<p>Don&#8217;t get too hung up on what to use right away; they all work in a similar fashion at their core. </p>



<p>If you&#8217;re learning to record and produce music, choose one <strong><a href="/getting-started-with-reaper-daw/" data-type="post" data-id="7991" data-lasso-id="270302">(I like Reaper because its amazingly scalable and free)</a></strong>, and learn it inside out. </p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="htoc-basic-system-requirements"><strong>What Is The Minimum Requirement For A DAW?</strong></h2>



<p>DAWs are software. They run on your computer (duh) and in order to run one smoothly, you need a basic level of spec and hardware. </p>



<p>Here&#8217;s a quick rundown of the minimum system requirements for most modern DAWs:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li id="htoc-processor-intel-core-i5-or-amd-ryzen-5-and-for-mac-anything-that-s-not-over-5-years-old-should-be-fine"><strong>Processor</strong>: Intel Core i5 or AMD Ryzen 5; and for Mac anything that&#8217;s not over 5 years old should be fine. </li>



<li id="htoc-ram-8-gb"><strong>RAM</strong>: 8 GB</li>



<li id="htoc-storage-256-gb-ssd"><strong>Storage</strong>: 256 GB SSD</li>



<li id="htoc-operating-system-windows-10-11-64-bit-or-macos-10-15"><strong>Operating System</strong>: Windows 10/11 (64-bit) or macOS 10.15+</li>



<li id="htoc-graphics-directx-12-compatible-but-don-t-worry-daws-aren-t-too-graphics-heavy"><strong>Graphics</strong>: DirectX 12 compatible (but don&#8217;t worry, DAWs aren&#8217;t too graphics-heavy)</li>
</ul>

<p>Most people reading this article will be brand new to music production and recording in general.&nbsp;</p>



<p>That’s fine. Remember: everybody starts from zero –&nbsp;even the gods like Rubin and Albini.&nbsp;</p>



<p>And the upshot of this is that you can get away with spending less; you’ll only need to do the basics, so you won’t need a pro-grade rig just yet.&nbsp;</p>



<p>Based on my 20+ years of recording and producing, there’s three tiers of music production setups: <strong>cheap, intermediate, and professional</strong>. </p>



<p>You’ll likely be looking at the cheap and intermediate options. </p>

<li id="htoc-ram-8-gb1"><strong>RAM</strong>: 8 GB</li>



<li id="htoc-storage-256-gb-ssd1"><strong>Storage</strong>: 256 GB SSD</li>
</ul>



<p><strong>Best For</strong>: Basic recording, editing, and light plugin use.</p>



<p><strong>Great DAWs</strong>: REAPER, GarageBand</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="htoc-mid-range-setup"><strong>Mid-Range Setup</strong></h3>



<p><strong>Ideal For</strong>: Semi-professionals who need a bit more muscle.</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li id="htoc-processor-intel-core-i7-10th-gen-or-amd-ryzen-7-3700x"><strong>Processor</strong>: Intel Core i7 (10th gen+) or AMD Ryzen 7 3700X</li>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="htoc-laptop-vs-desktop-what-s-right-for-you"><strong>Laptop vs. Desktop: What&#8217;s Right for You?</strong></h3>



<p>Choosing between a laptop and a desktop can feel overwhelming, but it doesn&#8217;t have to be. It all boils down to your personal needs and circumstances.</p>



<h4 class="wp-block-heading" id="htoc-laptops"><strong>Laptops</strong></h4>



<p>If you&#8217;re someone who&#8217;s always on the move, or maybe you don&#8217;t have a dedicated space for a studio setup, a laptop might be your best bet. </p>



<p>Laptops are portable, space-saving, and convenient—they come with a built-in screen, keyboard, and trackpad, which means fewer accessories to worry about.</p>



<p>However, keep in mind that laptops generally offer less performance compared to desktops at the same price point. </p>



<p>They have limited upgradability, and extended heavy use can cause them to overheat, potentially slowing down performance. </p>



<p>But for starting out, especially if you&#8217;re focusing on the basics, a laptop can serve you well.</p>



<h4 class="wp-block-heading" id="htoc-desktops"><strong>Desktops</strong></h4>



<p>On the other hand, if you have a bit more space and don&#8217;t need to move your setup around, a desktop could be the way to go. </p>



<p>Desktops offer more power, customization, and upgradability. </p>



<p>You can start with a basic setup and gradually upgrade components like RAM, storage, or even the processor as your needs grow.</p>



<p>The downside? Desktops aren&#8217;t portable and require more space and a bit more technical know-how to set up. </p>



<p>But if you&#8217;re looking to invest in a system that can grow with you, a desktop might be worth considering.</p>

<p>Now, onto the classic debate: Mac or PC? Here&#8217;s the thing—both platforms have their merits, and your choice should align with your comfort level and specific needs.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="htoc-mac"><strong>Mac</strong></h3>



<p>Macs are known for their stability and user-friendly interface. If you&#8217;re already familiar with macOS, you&#8217;ll appreciate the seamless experience. </p>



<p>Macs come with CoreAudio drivers built-in, offering low latency, which is great for recording. </p>



<p>Plus, you have access to exclusive software like GarageBand and Logic Pro X, which are fantastic for beginners and pros alike.</p>



<p>However, Macs tend to be pricier, and upgrading components isn&#8217;t always straightforward. </p>



<p>If budget is a concern, or you like tinkering with your setup, this might be a limitation.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="htoc-pc"><strong>PC</strong></h3>



<p>PCs offer incredible flexibility and customization options. You can build a PC that fits your exact specifications and budget. </p>



<p>Upgrading components is typically easier and more cost-effective. </p>



<p>There&#8217;s also a vast array of software available for Windows users.</p>



<p>The trade-off is that PCs might require a bit more technical knowledge to manage drivers and ensure compatibility with your chosen DAW. </p>



<p>But don&#8217;t let that intimidate you—there are plenty of resources available to help you navigate any challenges.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="htoc-why-your-gear-isn-t-everything"><strong>Why Your Gear Isn&#8217;t Everything</strong></h2>



<p>It&#8217;s easy to get caught up in the specs and feel like you need the best equipment to produce quality music. </p>



<p>But here&#8217;s a secret: the most crucial element in your music production journey is <strong>you</strong>. </p>



<p>Your creativity, passion, and willingness to learn outweigh any piece of gear.</p>



<p>Many iconic tracks were produced on modest setups. </p>



<p>The gear you have is simply a tool to bring your ideas to life. </p>



<p>Starting with what you have or what fits comfortably within your budget is perfectly okay. </p>



<p>As you grow and develop your skills, you&#8217;ll have a clearer idea of what equipment you might want to invest in down the line.</p>

<p>It&#8217;s all about what you need right now and where you see yourself going.</p>
