<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="mastodon-hushed-and-grim-review"><strong>Hushed and Grim Review</strong></h2>



<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-full"><img decoding="async" width="1000" height="500" src="/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/mastodon-hushed-and-grim-review.webp" alt="" class="wp-image-5367" title="" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/mastodon-hushed-and-grim-review.webp 1000w, /wp-content/uploads/2021/11/mastodon-hushed-and-grim-review-600x300.webp 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2021/11/mastodon-hushed-and-grim-review-300x150.webp 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2021/11/mastodon-hushed-and-grim-review-768x384.webp 768w" sizes="(max-width: 1000px) 100vw, 1000px" /></figure>



<p>I’m what you’d describe as a “band boomer”. This is a term I have created but I think it aptly describes a lot of people. Do you usually prefer a band’s older albums? </p>



<p>Do you talk about how a certain band “used” to be back in the day? Lament how they’ve changed over the years? </p>



<p>If you do, you might be a band boomer too.</p>



<p>With Mastodon, I’m a big fan of its earlier stuff –&nbsp;<strong>Leviathan and Blood Mountain</strong>, specifically. I also love <strong>Crack The Skye</strong>. I like its other, newer albums too but nowhere near as much. </p>



<p>To me, albums like <strong>The Hunter</strong> and <strong>Once More Round The Sun</strong> just lack that special something that makes Mastodon, well, Mastodon to me.</p>



<p>I’m the same with TOOL, Slipknot, Deftones, OPETH, <a data-lasso-id="17574" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/new-metallica-album-release-date-new-songs-update/">Metallica</a>. But I do know that every band, <a data-lasso-id="195395" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/13-ghost-the-band-facts-you-probably-didnt-know/">like Ghost</a>, for instance, has the potential to completely outdo its previous best efforts. </p>



<p>It doesn’t happen very often, or sometimes even at all (hello, OPETH and Deftones), but when it does, and you’re listening in the right environment, it can become a near-mystical experience.</p>



<p>The good news here is that this isn’t just another record. The guys in Mastodon are very accomplished musicians, always have been, but on this record they’re just on fire, sounding as if all they’ve done during quarantine is sit inside a darkened room and practice their craft.</p>



<div class="custom-snippet-div"></p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What Happened To Troy Sanders&#8217; Vocals!? </strong></h2>



<p>The guitar work on <strong>Hushed and Grim</strong> is some of the best the band has ever done. Brann’s drumming has never sounded bigger or more technical. But for me, the stand-out performance on the record is Troy Sander’s vocal performance. He absolutely KILLS it in this record. So does Bran, but Troy’s vocals have improved so much I think it is worthy of noting above everything else on show here.</p>



<p>Troy has gone from what can best be described as a low-end growler to one of the most unique-sounding metal and/or rock vocalists working today. His range has tripled, seemingly, and the overall effect of this adds a dynamic to <strong>Hushed and Grim</strong> that hasn’t been present on any of the band’s earlier albums. I would have loved to hear more from <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/what-guitars-do-mastodon-use/" data-type="post" data-id="4856" data-lasso-id="14321">Brent Hinds</a></strong> on the vocal front, but I think he has stepped back to focus on his guitar playing. And that’s cool; his solo work on <strong>Hushed and Grim</strong> is frankly ridiculous.</p>



<p>All of the guys sound like they’re playing for their lives on this record. It has an urgency that, for me personally, just hasn’t been present on anything <strong>post-Blood Mountain</strong>. The record is heavy, yes, and it is technical. But it could also be described as some of Mastodon’s most accessible work to date. There are soaring choruses left, right, and center, catchy riffs and licks, and <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/mastodon-hushed-and-grim-lyrics/" data-lasso-id="15525">great lyrics on nearly all the tracks</a>.</p>



<p>I don’t want to ruin too much about the record; I hate track by track analyses of albums. Music is subjective. You need to listen to it yourself and make up your own mind. But I will go out on a limb here and say that this is the best record Mastodon has done since <strong>Crack The Skye</strong>, a record I think this album surpasses, both in its scope and the overall quality of the writing and performances.</p>



<p>I actually enjoyed every track on this album. I didn&#8217;t think any sounded like filler. Each track stands on its own. And that is an impressive feat on a double album that spans 90 minutes. A couple of my favorite tracks, however, would be <strong><a data-lasso-id="15527" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/mastodon-hushed-and-grim-lyrics/" data-type="post" data-id="5485">Pain With An Anchor, The Crux, Gigantium, Savage Lands, Had It All and Pushing The Tides</a></strong>. </p>



<p></div>



<div class="custom-snippet-div"></p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>The Evolution of Cold Dark Place</strong></h2>



<p><strong>Cold Dark Place</strong> was brilliant, showcasing a side of Mastodon we haven’t really seen since the mid-2000s. I think <strong>Hushed and Grim</strong> picks up where <strong>Cold Dark Place</strong> left off, leaving us hungry for more. It not only picks up but it adds in so much additional stuff it is kind of dizzying on the first listen though. Mastodon does things on this record that I never thought I’d hear them do.</p>



<p>The record is ballsy as hell. Whereas a lot of Mastodon’s work has been heavily reliant on <a class="wpil_keyword_link" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/best-electric-guitars/" title="guitars" data-wpil-keyword-link="linked" data-lasso-id="14322">guitars</a>, this record ditches guitar almost completely in some tracks and instead uses vocal melodies to lead the way. The guitars are there, of course, but they’re used for texture, not the thing that leads and moves the song forward. And this is a first for Mastodon.</p>



<p><strong>Hushed and Grim</strong> opens with a killer track that really sets the tone. Bran’s drumming on this record is incredible. And I like that Mastodon hit the ground running with the opener, <strong>Pain With An Anchor</strong>. The second track <strong>The Crux</strong> is equally heavy and equally impressive. The chorus is one of Mastodon’s catchiest to date, hooking you in right away.</p>



<p>But there’s just so much going on here that it is difficult to pin <strong>Hushed and Grim</strong> down. Yes, it is a metal album. But it also has plenty of rock elements too. There are some slower, experimental tracks that go well outside the usual “traditional” Mastodon output. <strong>Hushed and Grim</strong> is a deep, complex listen. You need to really sit down, or go for walk, to soak it all in. It does require a few listens to really appreciate what’s going on here.</p>



<p>The production is great, the guitar work exceptional. Bran’s drumming has never sounded better or bigger, and Bill – as always – is on fire from start to finish. Brent Hinds’ solo work is also incredible, arguably his best work to date on any Mastodon album. But for me, it is Troy’s vocals that really steal the show – he has improved so much between 2017 and now that he almost sounds like a completely different guy in parts.</p>



<p></div>



<div class="custom-snippet-div"></p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Mastodon &#8211; Hushed and Grim Review: Verdict</strong></h2>



<p>Overall, this is album is a modern classic. It has all the elements that made <strong>Leviathan, Blood Mountain, and Crack The Skye</strong> exceptional plus all the newer stuff we’ve come to love from the band. As noted earlier, I do like all of Mastodon’s albums, but for me, the big three are <strong>Leviathan, Blood Mountain, and Crack The Skye</strong>. I didn’t think the band would ever top these records. But after listening to <strong>Hushed and Grim</strong> on repeat since Friday, I can now safely say that they have.</p>



<p><strong>Hushed and Grim</strong> is perhaps Mastodon’s finest moment to date, the record that will propel them to new heights, earning them new fans, and new accolades. As far as I’m concerned, <strong>Hushed and Grim</strong> is the album of the year. And probably 2022 as well. It’s just flawless, jaw-dropping stuff from start to finish.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Pitchfork SLAMS Mastodon’s Hushed and Grim Record</strong></h2>



<p>Pitchfork has been fans of Mastodon ever since the band’s inception, however, it is NOT a fan of the new record, scoring it a meagre 5.7 (out of 10). Emperor of Sand, meanwhile, netted a rating of 7.6 out of 10.</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p><strong>After wading through a swamp of mostly bromidic alt-rock, there it is: the jewel of Hushed and Grim, and, incidentally, the song that sounds most adventurous, unlike anything in the band’s catalog. Evoking Meanderthal-era Torche, Mastodon hints that it could be the sludgiest nu-emo band on the planet. It’s ironically the one track I wish I could live inside for much longer, craving a third mystical guitar solo, even. “Gigantium” is proof-of-concept for another new version of Mastodon. But the band known for continually surprising listeners ultimately falls short, mostly hiding behind unexceptional, diluted alt-metal. Instead of letting this bold idea guide the way, it’s offered up as an apology affixed to the end of their least ambitious collection yet. Mastodon, once transgressive in its refusal to be put in a box, has shaved off its sharp edges and crawled inside.</strong></p>
<cite><strong><a href="https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/mastodon-hushed-and-grim/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" data-lasso-id="12847">PITCHFORK</a></strong></cite></blockquote>



<hr class="wp-block-separator has-css-opacity is-style-dots"/>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p><strong>This album is audacious and adventurous but isn’t perfect. I’m certain fans will disagree with me on this point but I found the album ran just a little long. There are without a doubt some absolute bangers here – in fact I’d go so far as to say the four piece from Georgia have written some of the best music of their career. Distilled down to 10 or 12 tracks Hushed &amp; Grim would be an absolute belter. But much like life, this is a marathon, not a sprint, and maybe I just need a bit of patience.</strong></p>
<cite><strong><a href="https://wallofsoundau.com/2021/10/27/mastodon-hushed-and-grim-album-review/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" data-lasso-id="12184">WALL OF SOUND</a></strong></cite></blockquote>



<hr class="wp-block-separator has-css-opacity is-style-dots"/>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p><strong>Hushed and Grim is the Mastodon I fell in love with years ago, listening to Leviathan on repeat while driving my family back and forth from the hospital to visit my sick father. This is thunder and lightning, the primal roar of the earth and the cold echo of space; this is the heat of the human heart suspended in otherwise confused darkness. There are a number of people who likely won’t give it the time of day due to a misguided sense that the band has lost the plot; for those of us who’ve witnessed them rebuild themselves into greatness once again, it is the greatest document of this second phase of the magical and rightly storied band’s career.</strong></p>
<cite><strong><a href="https://www.treblezine.com/mastodon-hushed-and-grim-review/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" data-lasso-id="12185">TREBLE</a></strong></cite></blockquote>



<hr class="wp-block-separator has-css-opacity is-style-dots"/>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p><strong>The most engaging aspect of MASTODON&#8217;s 2021 sound that really keeps the proceedings of &#8220;Hushed and Grim&#8221; rooted in emotional heaviness is bassist Troy Sanders&#8217;s vocal performance. Whether it&#8217;s by design or the natural strain of age, the tortured howls of his sound result in what may be his most compelling vocal performance. Sanders rasps work in conjunction with hearty bellows from guitarist Brent Hinds to lend a weight that keeps the dark blues-rock balladry of &#8220;The Beast&#8221; from coming off as a maudlin farce. Sanders&#8217;s weathered shrieks and emotive keyboard work also bolster &#8220;Skeleton of Splendor&#8221;&#8216;s spooky synth-prog as well as &#8220;Dagger&#8221;&#8216;s thunderous torment. Dailor also impresses with soaring melodic lead vocals on &#8220;Teardrinker&#8221;&#8216;s relatively straight-forward rock, but Sanders&#8217;s beautiful throat-shredding performance on &#8220;Gigantium&#8221;&#8216;s epic closing comedown can&#8217;t help but leave the lasting impression after initial listens are completed.</strong></p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>General Consensus? </strong></h2>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="683" src="/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/brent-hinds-guitar-1024x683.png" alt="brent hinds guitar" class="wp-image-14751" title="" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/brent-hinds-guitar-1024x683.png 1024w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/02/brent-hinds-guitar-600x400.png 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/02/brent-hinds-guitar-300x200.png 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/02/brent-hinds-guitar-768x512.png 768w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/02/brent-hinds-guitar.png 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<p>What&#8217;s the consensus on Mastodon&#8217;s Hushed and Grim – is it a hit, a miss, or a classic? I&#8217;ve mined through myriad reviews and below is a summary of the general consensus about the album (if you&#8217;re interested in that kind of thing): </p>



<ol class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Ambitiousness</strong>: The album is considered audacious, adventurous, and a monumental effort by the band, showcasing their willingness to innovate and push boundaries.</li>



<li><strong>Emotional depth</strong>: The album is described as Mastodon&#8217;s most personal work to date, with lyrics that delve into themes of grief, healing, and introspection.</li>



<li><strong>Vocal performances</strong>: Troy Sanders&#8217; vocal performance is widely praised, with his tortured howls and emotive delivery adding to the album&#8217;s emotional heaviness.</li>



<li><strong>Musical diversity</strong>: The album incorporates various influences, from heavy sludge metal to progressive rock, southern rock, and even hints of blues and emo.</li>



<li><strong>Musicianship</strong>: The band&#8217;s technical prowess shines through, with each member bringing their unique personality to the music, from intricate drumming to fluid guitar solos.</li>



<li><strong>Length and pacing</strong>: Some reviewers feel that the album&#8217;s length (15 tracks) is a bit excessive, and the pacing in the final fifth of the album is slower, which may not appeal to all listeners.</li>



<li><strong>Production</strong>: Producer David Bottrill is credited with allowing the band&#8217;s chemistry and individual strengths to shine through in the recordings.</li>
</ol>



<p>Overall, &#8220;Hushed and Grim&#8221; is considered a strong addition to Mastodon&#8217;s discography, showcasing their growth, emotional depth, and musical prowess. </p>



<p>While some critics find the album&#8217;s length and pacing to be a minor drawback, the general consensus is that the album is a powerful and engaging work of art.</p>



<p>Me? I think it is the band&#8217;s best work since Crack The Skye. A fine return to form. I cannot wait to see what Mastodon have in store for us next.
