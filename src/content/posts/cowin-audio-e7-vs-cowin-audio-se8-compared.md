<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Cowin Audio: What Is EQ Reference?</strong></h2>



<p>Recording with your guitar/band is usually something we all eventually do. If you plan on trying to make it in the music business or even just do it for practice&#8230; you eventually want to record and share a song. </p>



<p>There are steps that every PRO follows, and the most important one is the <em>final step</em>: <strong>Referencing and</strong> <strong>Playback</strong>. This is where you check your final mix and get ready for release.</p>



<p>If you have been recording for a while, you will probably know all about how to &#8220;reference&#8221; your recorded music. When digital recording first started, I was working in California and we had all kinds of studio monitors that you use when you are <em>actually recording</em>. </p>



<p>But when it came to referencing, <em>the car</em> was always our best option. Sound silly? <strong>Ask anyone that has been recording for a while!</strong></p>



<p>You might remember having to burn your tracks to a CD, just so you could go out to the car and listen to your song. The car was always a great &#8220;reference&#8221; place. When you had the doors closed, it was basically a soundproof reference monitor! </p>



<p>Those days are long gone, and now we can instantly upload a song to our phone, and do playback on multiple devices. However, the car stereo is still a valuable tool!</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What is Referencing?</h2>



<div class="custom-snippet-div"></p>



<p><strong>Referencing is usually defined as comparing your mix to a professionally recorded track. You find a song that is similar to yours in EQ and loudness, and try to &#8220;match it&#8221; when mixing and mastering. This process usually starts with studio monitors and headphones, but it is important to check your sound on other devices.</strong></p>



<p></div>



<p></p>



<p>Referencing is an important step when it comes to recording. Most professional studios have a different set of monitors especially made for this step. </p>



<p>But when<a data-lasso-id="142595" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/must-have-for-recording-guitar/" data-type="post" data-id="4877" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> <strong>recording at home</strong></a>, most of us don&#8217;t have $5000 monitors to use for playback and referencing. So we use our cars, or our home stereo to try and get the mastered track to the right volume, and <em>nail the mix</em>. </p>



<p>You do this with EVERY genre when recording.</p>



<p>Picking a reference track can be intimidating at first. You want to pick a professional track that is recent, and in the same genre as what you are trying to record. </p>



<p>If your song has quiet/loud dynamics, then pick a track that does that too! Then put it at the bottom of your mixdown, on a separate track in your<a data-lasso-id="142596" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/getting-started-with-reaper-daw/" data-type="post" data-id="7991" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><strong> DAW</strong></a>. Keep it muted, and toggle back and forth with your song, to see if the volumes and dynamics match.</p>



<p>Your mixed and mastered track should be as close as possible in volume and dynamics to your reference track. Once you have done this, and you are satisfied with the results, then we move on to the playback phase. </p>



<p>This is where the Cowin Audio Headphones come in, and <em>any other device</em> that you may have.</p>



<p><strong>Cowin Audio is kind of a &#8220;best kept secret brand&#8221; when it comes to headphones.</strong> It started 3 years ago as an Amazon brand based in the USA and UK, but has since moved on to bigger things. </p>



<p>The company has taken active noise canceling technology and really boosted it to a new level. So even if you are not a musician, Cowin Audio might be right up your alley.</p>



<p><strong>That being said, Cowin Audio Headphones are not made for mixing.</strong> When you are recording, it really is best to use studio monitors that are &#8220;flat&#8221; in EQ. </p>



<p>These are usually small speakers that are known as &#8220;near field monitors&#8221;. You CAN use headphones to mix, but that requires a pair of &#8220;flat EQ&#8221; headphones. </p>



<p><strong>These Cowin Audio Headphones that we are talking about today are for playback and referencing.</strong></p>



<p>Today we are going to go over two models that are almost completely noise canceling, and perfect for listening back to your recordings and reference tracks. </p>



<p>Cowin Audio makes some solid stuff for budget prices, and today we are only looking at the &#8220;over the ear&#8221; models. Earbuds are good for listening to the final product, but they lack depth.</p>



<p><strong>I cannot stress how important it is to check your mixes BEFORE you upload them. </strong>The finished tracks may sound amazing through your studio monitors, but with bass boost and other EQ options, they may be clipping or muddy! </p>



<p>You won&#8217;t know until you have listened on another device. I actually have a few different methods of checking a track before I upload them to Spotify, Apple Music, or YouTube.</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>1. Make a private Soundcloud or dropbox account for your test mixes.</strong></li><li><strong>2. Make sure your track and your reference track are as close as possible in your DAW.</strong></li><li><strong>3. Have 3 devices that you check your finished song on. For example: Car, Headphones, Stereo.</strong></li><li><strong>4. Have an extra set of ears, like a <a data-lasso-id="142597" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/starting-a-band-part-2/" data-type="post" data-id="6850" target="_blank" rel="noopener">bandmate</a>, or friend to listen with you.</strong></li><li><strong>5. make sure your track fits the parameters (Luffs) of the platform you use.</strong></li></ul>



<p>In a separate article, I will go over how to mix and master a song. These Cowin Audio Headphones are more for people that are <em>already familiar</em> with the process. </p>



<p>So with all of that out of the way, let&#8217;s check out the two models – the <strong>Cowin Audio E7 and the Cowin Audio SE8</strong> – that I think are the best! </p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What Is Active Noise Cancelling?</h2>



<div class="custom-snippet-div"></p>



<p><strong>Active Noise Cancellation (ANC) uses a system to reduce unwanted background noise. The system is based on microphones that “listen” to the sounds outside and inside of the earphones. </strong></p>



<p><strong>The ANC chipset inverts the soundwaves and a speaker inside the earphone. This then neutralizes sound waves from the outside. The chip works all by itself, and the user does not have to do anything.</strong></p>



<p></div>



<p></p>



<p>Active noise canceling (ANC) is a great feature, and the Cowin Audio E7 set has a 3 microphone technology to pick up and filter the noise. It also uses a single mic for picking up telephone calls or communications for text-to-speech. </p>



<p>Active noise canceling essentially filters the outside world and brings it down a few decibels while raising it in the earphones. BOSE made this tech a long time ago, but Cowin Audio has made it affordable.</p>



<p>The active noise canceling feature is not as good on the Cowin Audio E7, as the next set that we are going to talk about today. But the company states that you get around 90% of outside noise canceled, and I think it may actually be a <em>little bit lower</em> than that. </p>



<p>I tested these with the leaf blower going outside, and I could still hear the outside noise just a little bit. I would say the E7s probably cancel about 80% of the noise.</p>



<p>The Bluetooth range is pretty substantial for budget headphones. I was surprised that I could go out to my parking lot and these still worked fine! </p>



<p>I measured this to be about 30 feet from my computer, so these blow my earbuds away when it comes to distance. The padding and overall comfort were excellent, and I had no problem wearing these for hours.</p>



<p>I also tried these with my studio interface and my <a data-lasso-id="142599" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/boss-katana-100-mkii-review/" data-type="post" data-id="8377" target="_blank" rel="noopener">BOSS Katana</a> amplifier. </p>



<p>Using the Cowin Audio E7s in this manner was delightful. I love having the active noise canceling when practicing my guitar quietly. If you&#8217;re the type of person that like to jam with backing tracks, these are perfect for that as well. </p>



<p>I spent over an hour playing and tried tons of different genres to play along with. </p>



<p>The EQ Curve that is built into the E7s is a little bit different than what I was expecting. Most headphones these days have <strong>entirely too much bass response</strong>, and it can make the details of most tracks really get lost in the mix. </p>



<p>The Cowin Audio E7s have a pretty balanced EQ, and they work great with just about any genre. Bass-heavy tracks have plenty of bass response, but I feel like these &#8220;breathe&#8221; better than something like BEATS.</p>

<p>The high-mids that would usually catch cymbals are turned down pretty low, but the treble boost makes up for that. Snares and high hats are crisp and clean through all of the mixes I tried out.</p>



<p>So altogether, the Cowin Audio E7 Headphones are an absolute steal in my opinion at only 50 bucks. </p>
