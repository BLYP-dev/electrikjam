<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Let&#8217;s Talk About The Genesis of The &#8220;Doom&#8221; Sound&#8230;</h2>



<p><strong><a class="rank-math-link" type="post" id="1042" data-lasso-id="886" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/doom-metal-beginners-guide/">Doom Metal</a></strong> without a doubt got its roots from<strong> Black Sabbath</strong> throughout the 1960s and 1970s. Doom Metal is known as a genre for having slower tempos and sparse arrangements that are focused on heavy guitars.</p>



<p>While Black Sabbath got the genre started, during the 1980s many other bands from England started taking what Sabbath did, and made it heavier. Bands like <strong>Pagan Altar</strong> and <strong>Witchfinder General </strong>paved the way for many other bands.</p>



<p>Around the same time, in America and Sweden, <a data-lasso-id="2791" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/90s-metal-bands/">Doom Metal</a> was catching on with bands like <strong>Candlemass</strong>, <strong>Pentagram</strong>, and <strong>Saint Vitus</strong>. By the end of the &#8217;80s, Doom Metal started catching on and lending influence to bands like <strong>Kyuss, <a class="rank-math-link" data-lasso-id="887" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/buzz-osbornes-guitar-rig-35-years-of-evolution/">Melvins</a></strong>, <strong><a class="rank-math-link" data-lasso-id="888" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/matt-pike-dopesmoker-sleep/">Sleep</a></strong>, and<strong> Fu Manchu</strong>.</p>



<p>While <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/best-practice-amps-for-beginners/" data-type="post" data-id="532" data-lasso-id="889">amps and effects</a> make up a good portion of what Doom/Sludge guitarists use to tailor their sound, it all starts with the guitar. It <em>always </em>starts with a guitar.</p>



<p>We have already discussed tunings in a different article, that <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/doom-metal-tunings-the-top-5-best/" class="rank-math-link" data-lasso-id="890">you can find here. </a> But what are the best guitars for Doom Metal?</p>



<p>This list is a tough one since Doom Metal has always been very DIY.  What are the best guitars for Doom Metal? What brands? What styles? What shape?</p>



<p>Well, every player has a favorite but there are also some trends that we can follow that covers the entire history of the best guitars for Doom Metal. We can look at the gear the pioneers used, and we can also look at what the new generation uses.</p>



<p>Many guitarists in the genre use whatever guitar they can afford. Some people use rare and difficult-to-find guitars. But today we are going to check out the ones you can buy just about anywhere.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">#5 Gibson Standard Les Paul P-90</h2>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="768" src="/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Gibson-p90s-for-doom-1024x768.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-932" title="" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Gibson-p90s-for-doom-1024x768.jpg 1024w, /wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Gibson-p90s-for-doom-scaled-600x450.jpg 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Gibson-p90s-for-doom-300x225.jpg 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Gibson-p90s-for-doom-768x576.jpg 768w, /wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Gibson-p90s-for-doom-1536x1152.jpg 1536w, /wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Gibson-p90s-for-doom-2048x1536.jpg 2048w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /><figcaption>Gibsons with <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/what-are-p90-pickups-good-for-lets-find-out/" data-lasso-id="24906">P90s</a></figcaption></figure>



<p>Gibsons are used a lot by Doom/Sludge artists. So you may see more than one on this list! When it comes to specs, this is a regular Les Paul, through and through. The difference is the amazing Custom Shop pickups.</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Mahogany Neck and Body</li><li>Nitro lacquer</li><li>22 Frets</li><li>Grover Tuners</li><li>Rosewood Fretboard</li><li>Classic 50&#8217;s neck profile</li><li>Gibson Custom Shop P-90 Pickups</li></ul>



<p>So it is essentially <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/best-les-paul-copy/" data-lasso-id="4661">your basic Les Paul</a>, right?</p>



<p>What sets this model apart from <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/the-best-gibson-les-paul-alternatives-a-look-at-the-other-side/" class="rank-math-link" data-lasso-id="891">other Les Paul models</a></strong> is <strong><a class="rank-math-link" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/humbuckers-vs-single-coil-pickups/" data-lasso-id="892">the P-90 pickups</a></strong>. Gibson has been making these in their Custom Shop for decades. Originally they were fitted for their semi-hollow guitars, used in Jazz.</p>



<p>The P-90 is essentially a very large single-coil pickup. Since so much of Metal music requires copious amounts of gain/fuzz/distortion, having a P-90 pickup adds clarity and plenty of high-end. The bridge pickup in particular sounds really dirty. <em>Filthy</em>, even.</p>



<p>Gibson P-90s are pretty famous for not only having a clear tone that can deal with high gain tones but retain midrange and clarity.  Even better, they produce feedback like <em>crazy</em>.</p>



<p>The feedback can be used in some creative applications, or you could dampen it with a noise gate. At any rate, P-90 pickups are a great choice for Doom/Sludge due to their powerful sound, and &#8220;cutting&#8221; nature.</p>



<p>Clear-sounding pickups are a godsend to guitarists that plan on <a data-lasso-id="6308" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/aadgbe-guitar-tuning-drop-a-gettin-proper-low-n-dirty/">tuning down their guitar</a>. Since most Doom/Sludge is tuned down, the Gibson P-90 is an obvious choice.</p>



<p><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/gibson-vs-epiphone-in-2021/" data-lasso-id="893">Gibson is an obvious choice for Metal musicians</a> everywhere, but what about something a little less expensive?</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">#4 Epiphone SG Tony Iommi Signature Guitar</h2>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="1024" src="/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Tony-Iommi-1024x1024.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-934" title=""><figcaption>Tony Iommi Signature</figcaption></figure>



<p>Here we have the <a data-lasso-id="14314" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/the-best-signature-guitars-in-2021/">signature guitar</a> of the legend himself: Tony Iommi of Black Sabbath. The Epiphone version of his signature is much less expensive than its Gibson counterpart. <a data-lasso-id="896" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/best-guitar-for-beginners/" data-type="post" data-id="505">We kind of love Epiphone around here&#8230;</a></p>



<p>Tony has played SG models since the inception of Black Sabbath.  Tony bought his first Gibson SG in a guitar shop near Brighton in 1967. He had briefly flirted with the idea of <a data-lasso-id="897" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/the-best-fender-strat-alternative-in-2021/">using Fender Stratocasters</a> but ended up using the SG to record with. The rest is history.</p>



<p>Tony put his own touches on the classic model:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Mahogany Body</li><li>1960&#8217;s Slim Taper neck</li><li>Cross Inlays</li><li>Ebony Fretboard</li><li>Gibson Custom Shop Iommi pickups</li><li>2 Tone/ 2 Volume setup</li><li>Nickle hardware</li><li>Black Finish</li><li>Bound Fretboard</li></ul>



<p>Being a huge Black Sabbath fan, I needed to own one of these. I had one for a few years, the year after they hit the market. I have owned a fair share of SG guitars over the years, both <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/gibson-vs-epiphone-2021-which-is-best/" class="rank-math-link" data-lasso-id="898">Epiphone and Gibson</a></strong>. <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/what-guitar-does-tony-iommi-use-a-complete-overview/" data-lasso-id="254824">The Tony Iommi signature is pretty amazing</a> value for the money.</p>



<p>The Gibson USA Tony Iommi pickups sound huge and crushing. They are definitely based on the original PAF pickups that Gibson designed. This gives them a warm tone that sounds great under high gain. Tony is the only guitar player, so he has to cover a lot of sonic territories.</p>



<p>The bridge pickup sounds great with <strong><a class="rank-math-link" data-lasso-id="899" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/best-guitar-pedals-for-metal-our-top-choices-for-2020/">fuzz or distortion</a></strong> and it definitely chugs hard. The bass and mid-response allow the guitar to cut through the mix really well.</p>



<p>The neck is the same as most SG guitars and practically plays itself. The slim taper is perfect for riffing or shredding. The SG has been around for so long because of its versatility. The Tony Iommi signature takes that versatility and sharpens it for Metal.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">#3 Reverend Double Agent</h2>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="762" src="/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Reverend-1024x762.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-936" title="" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Reverend-1024x762.jpg 1024w, /wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Reverend-600x446.jpg 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Reverend-300x223.jpg 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Reverend-768x571.jpg 768w, /wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Reverend-1536x1142.jpg 1536w, /wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Reverend.jpg 1600w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /><figcaption>The Double Agent</figcaption></figure>



<p>Reverend likes to take a classic guitar idea, and tweak it until it becomes its own design. The Double Agent has been around for 20 years now, and it&#8217;s the wildcard pick for this list.</p>



<p>Why is it a wildcard? Well, I am sure Reverend doesn&#8217;t cross many people&#8217;s minds when it comes to the best guitars for doom metal. But I have some personal experience with them.</p>



<p>I picked the Double Agent because it is extremely versatile, hence the name. The Double Agent has a hot-rodded humbucker in the bridge and a single-coil P-90 in the neck. This creates a &#8220;best of both worlds&#8221; scenario.</p>



<p>The specs for the Reverend Double Agent are all over the place:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list"><li>12&#8221; radius</li><li>Korina Body</li><li>Maple neck</li><li>Locking tuners</li><li>Bone Nut</li><li>Upgraded electronics</li><li>Comes with or without tremolo</li></ul>



<p>The Double Agent is truly a &#8220;jack of all trades&#8221; guitar. They are used by Metal players, Country players, and Blues guitarists. If you&#8217;re looking for something that can do Doom and everything in between, Reverend has your back.</p>



<p>The upgraded electronics are pretty cool. Most guitars in this price range come with standard controls and wiring. Reverend steps it up a notch using custom capacitors for the volume and tone. This is a mod that the pros use, and it is very nice to come stock on a guitar.</p>



<p>Lots of heavy, innovative players use Reverend these days:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Billy Corgan</strong></li><li><strong>Robin from NIN</strong></li><li><strong>Shade from Code Orange</strong></li><li><strong>Bob Balch from Fu Manchu</strong></li><li><strong>Kyle Shult from THE SWORD</strong></li></ul>



<p>Reverend is not a company that you usually see on these kinds of lists, and I think it&#8217;s time Reverend got it&#8217;s fair shake. These guys that play Reverend had endorsements with <em>much </em>larger companies before, and still made the switch.</p>



<p>That&#8217;s a testament to their quality, and innovation.
