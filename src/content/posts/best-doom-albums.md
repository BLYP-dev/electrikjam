<p><strong><a href="/doom-metal-beginners-guide/" data-type="post" data-id="1042" data-lasso-id="258816">Doom metal</a></strong>, a subgenre that evolved from the heavy metal scene, is known for its slow tempos, thick sound, and an aura of sorrow and dread. It started with <strong><a href="/what-guitar-does-tony-iommi-use-a-complete-overview/" data-type="post" data-id="13306" data-lasso-id="258817">bands like Black Sabbath</a></strong>, Saint Vitus, and Trouble in the 1970s and has been growing and evolving ever since. </p>



<p>There are now numerous subgenres and hundreds of artists and bands working in this particular field. Most people know SLEEP and Electric Wizard but there&#8217;s plenty of other gems to found if you&#8217;re new to the genre. </p>



<p>We&#8217;re big fans of the heavy stuff here at ELECTRIKJAM, so here&#8217;s our pick of the 25 most essential doom albums spanning from 1970 to the 2020s. <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="Best_Doom_Albums_of_All_Time">Best Doom Albums of All Time</h2>



<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="707" src="/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Doom-Metal-A-Total-Beginners-Guide…-1024x707.jpg" alt="doom metal" class="wp-image-1046" title=""></figure>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="Pioneers_and_Founding_Fathers_s-s">Pioneers and Founding Fathers (1970s-1980s)</h3>



<ol class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Black Sabbath &#8211; Black Sabbath (1970)</strong> – This groundbreaking album birthed the doom genre with its heavy blues and occult themes, setting the standard for decades to come.</li>



<li><strong>Saint Vitus &#8211; Saint Vitus (1984)</strong> – Defined the essence of doom with its slow, heavy riffing and melancholic vocals.</li>



<li><strong>Trouble &#8211; Psalm 9 (1984)</strong> – Pioneered epic doom, featuring operatic vocals and dark, haunting lyrics.</li>



<li><strong>Candlemass &#8211; Epicus Doomicus Metallicus (1986)</strong> – Established epic doom metal with majestic melodies and dramatic operatic vocals.</li>



<li><strong>Witchfinder General &#8211; Death Penalty (1982)</strong> – A fusion of NWOBHM with doom elements, paving the way for future subgenres.</li>



<li><strong>Celtic Frost &#8211; Morbid Tales (1984)</strong> – Combined doom with thrash metal aggression, influencing the evolution of death metal.</li>



<li><strong>Paradise Lost &#8211; Icon (1990)</strong> – Bridged doom and death metal, laying the groundwork for the emergence of gothic metal.</li>
</ol>



<p></div>



<div class="custom-snippet-div"></p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="Evolution_and_Subgenre_Creation_s-s">Evolution and Subgenre Creation (1990s-2000s)</h3>



<ol class="wp-block-list" start="8">
<li><strong>My Dying Bride &#8211; Turn Loose the Swans (1993)</strong> – Defined melodic doom with its sorrowful beauty and clean, emotive vocals.</li>



<li><strong>Cathedral &#8211; Forest of Equilibrium (1991)</strong> – Established stoner doom with psychedelic influences and fuzzed-out guitars.</li>



<li><strong>Electric Wizard &#8211; Dopethrone (2000)</strong> – Pioneered ultra-slow, sludgy doom with psychedelic and occult undertones.</li>



<li><strong>Neurosis &#8211; Souls at Zero (1992)</strong> – Combined sludge, doom, and hardcore, creating a crushing and chaotic sound.</li>



<li><strong>Sleep &#8211; Dopesmoker (1999)</strong> – A monolithic stoner doom masterpiece with hypnotic riffs and epic song structures.</li>



<li><strong>Type O Negative &#8211; October Rust (1996)</strong> – Fused gothic rock with doom metal, achieving mainstream success.</li>



<li><strong>Sunn O))) &#8211; Black One (2000)</strong> – Pioneered drone metal with minimalist, atmospheric soundscapes.</li>



<li><strong>Draconian &#8211; Arcane Rain Fell (2005)</strong> – Championed gothic doom with soaring operatic vocals and orchestral arrangements.</li>
</ol>



<p></div>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="Modern_Heavyweights_and_Future_Directions_s-Present">Modern Heavyweights and Future Directions (2010s-Present)</h3>



<ol class="wp-block-list" start="16">
<li><strong>Pallbearer &#8211; Foundations of Burden (2012)</strong> – Reinvigorated traditional doom with emotional depth and technical songwriting.</li>



<li><strong>Bell Witch &#8211; Mirror Witch (2013)</strong> – Atmospheric funeral doom with mournful clean vocals and crushing weight.</li>



<li><strong>YOB &#8211; Clearing the Path to Ascend (2014)</strong> – Experimental doom with progressive influences and complex song structures.</li>



<li><strong>The Body &#8211; Corpsepaint (2014)</strong> – Merged doom with noise and black metal, creating visceral and unsettling atmospheres.</li>



<li><strong>Conan &#8211; Blood Eagle (2018)</strong> – Revived epic doom with barbarian themes and Conan the Barbarian influences.</li>



<li><strong>Lingua Ignota &#8211; Caligula (2019)</strong> – Avant-garde black metal infused with doom and dark wave, exploring themes of trauma and abuse.</li>



<li><strong>Amenra &#8211; Mass III (2017)</strong> – Post-metal influenced doom with spiritual themes and soaring clean vocals.</li>



<li><strong>Thou &#8211; Magus (2014)</strong> – Sludge doom with raw honesty and introspective lyrics.</li>



<li><strong>Mizmor &#8211; Cairn (2017)</strong> – Atmospheric funeral doom with minimalist instrumentation and profound melancholy.</li>



<li><strong>Undeath &#8211; Lesions of a Different Kind (2020)</strong> – Melodic death metal fused with doom, featuring powerful vocals and technical riffs.</li>
</ol>
