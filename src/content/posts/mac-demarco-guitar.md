<p><strong>What guitar does Mac Demarco use to create his iconic and instantly recognisable tone? Here’s a breakdown of Mac’s current guitar collection…</strong></p>



<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity"/>



<p>With a slew of incredible albums under his belt, the King of Bedroom Producers is not only an extremely accomplished guitarist but he is also known for playing extremely inexpensive guitars from brands like Squier and Teisco.</p>



<p>Part of Mac Demarco’s appeal, save for his immensely likeable personality, is his approach to not only recording but also song writing –&nbsp;the guy is utterly prolific and shows no signs of slowing down.</p>



<p>Case in point: the latest Mac Demarco album, <strong>One Wayne G</strong>, is a collection of 199 songs that spans over <strong>EIGHT HOURS</strong>. Yeah&#8230; Mac literally does not give a f**k about convention. And his ever-growing collection of guitars, his constant experimentation with genre and production techniques, as well as his  love of old, thrift-store-acquired gear, is testament to this fact…</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="what-guitar-does-mac-demacro-use">Mac Demarco Guitar Collection</h2>



<p>As you can see below, Mac has acquired plenty of guitars over the years. This list is as comprehensive as we could make it, covering all the different types of guitars Mac Demarco has used throughout his career.</p>



<p>Is it 100% definitive? Impossible to say; Mac is a gear-head in the truest sense of the word, he loves guitars of all shapes and sizes, and vintage, analog recording gear and synths, so he’s probably adding new stuff in all the time.</p>



<p>But for now, these are the guitars that Mac most commonly uses either to record or in a live situation. And they’re about as varied as his albums too, covering everything from classic Strats right down to obscure, boutique brands like Teisco and Art &amp; Lutherie.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Here&#8217;s a brief overview of Teisco, the Japanese guitar company.</h3>



<p>Teisco was a Japanese company that manufactured affordable guitars from the late 1940s until 1969. These guitars were exported worldwide but were particularly popular in the United States and Canada. And you can still <strong><a href="https://guitar-center.pxf.io/q4W6Kn" target="_blank" rel="nofollow sponsored noopener" data-lasso-id="235707" data-lasso-name="Used Teisco Sunburst | Guitar Center">pick them up today second-hand</a></strong>. </p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Design and Build</strong>: Teisco guitars are known for their distinctive, often unconventional designs. They feature a variety of shapes and colors that were bold and innovative for their time. Many models have multiple pickups &#8211; often as many as four &#8211; and separate switches for each, providing a wide range of tonal options. The body of the most popular models is typically solid wood, often with a sunburst finish, and the fingerboard is usually rosewood.</li>



<li><strong>Pickups and Hardware</strong>: One of the defining characteristics of many Teisco guitars is their &#8220;gold foil&#8221; pickups, so named because of the gold-colored material visible through little windows in the pickup covers. These pickups have a unique, warm, and clear tone that&#8217;s been described as &#8220;microphonic,&#8221; picking up the vibrations of the strings but also the resonant qualities of the guitar&#8217;s body. The hardware, like the vibrato systems and bridges, while not as high-quality as some contemporaneous American-made guitars, have a quirky charm and contribute to the unique Teisco sound.</li>



<li><strong>Playability and Sound</strong>: Teisco guitars are generally considered to have a decent playability, with a comfortable neck and a good action. The sound is where these guitars truly shine, though. Those gold foil pickups provide a distinctive, vintage tone that&#8217;s been described as jangly and clear, perfect for surf, rockabilly, and indie rock styles.</li>



<li><strong>Legacy</strong>: While Teisco guitars were originally seen as beginner or budget instruments, they&#8217;ve since become collector&#8217;s items, prized for their unique aesthetics and tone. Artists like Mac DeMarco and Ry Cooder have used Teisco guitars, contributing to a resurgence of interest in these unique instruments.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What Guitar Does Mac DeMarco Use in Salad Days?</h2>



<p>As noted above, Mac DeMarco is well known for using a vintage Japanese Teisco guitar for many of his recordings and performances, and that includes during the period when he recorded the album <strong>&#8220;Salad Days.&#8221;</strong></p>



<p>The Teisco has a unique, somewhat jangly tone that contributes to DeMarco&#8217;s signature laid-back sound. It&#8217;s notable for its hollow metal pickguard, individual pickup switches, and vibrato system.</p>



<p>However, it&#8217;s also worth noting that DeMarco, like many artists, <strong><a href="#what-guitar-does-mac-demacro-use">uses a variety of guitar models</a></strong> from a range of brands in the studio to achieve the sound he&#8217;s looking for on a given track. In addition to his Teisco, <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/fender-guitars/" data-type="page" data-id="11532" data-lasso-id="235708">he&#8217;s been known to use Fender guitars</a></strong> and a variety of other brands as well.</p>
