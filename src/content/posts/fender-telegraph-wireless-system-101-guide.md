<p>Fender has announced its new Telepath Wireless System, aiming to address common issues plaguing wireless guitar setups. The system operates on a 5.8 GHz frequency, promising improved stability and reduced interference compared to standard 2.4 GHz models.</p>



<p>This system could offer significant advantages for live performers, particularly in environments where multiple wireless devices are in use. Fender claims the Telepath will provide reliable, high-quality sound transmission without the typical dropouts or delays associated with many wireless setups.</p>



<p>The Telepath is scheduled for release in late summer 2024, with pre-orders currently available. As wireless technology becomes increasingly prevalent in music performance, Fender&#8217;s entry into this market may signal a shift towards more robust, professional-grade wireless solutions for guitarists at various levels.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>The Fender Telepath Packs 8 Hours of Battery Life, 4ms of Latancy &amp; Has A Range of 21 Metres</strong></h2>



<p>Fender&#8217;s Telepath system is promising to be the Gandalf of wireless guitar tech – &#8220;You shall not pass&#8230; any interference!&#8221; </p>



<p>Here&#8217;s what makes it tick:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>5.8 GHz Frequency</strong>: While your budget-friendly systems are crowding the 2.4 GHz band like it&#8217;s a <a class="wpil_keyword_link" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/best-black-friday-guitar-deals/" title="Black Friday" data-wpil-keyword-link="linked" data-lasso-id="267576">Black Friday</a> sale, Telepath&#8217;s operating at 5.8 GHz. It&#8217;s like having your own VIP frequency!</li>



<li><strong>Low Latency</strong>: 4 milliseconds of latency? That&#8217;s faster than you can say &#8220;What latency?&#8221; Your notes will reach the amp before you even finish playing them. (Okay, not really, but you get the idea!)</li>



<li><strong>Battery Life for Days</strong>: 8 hours of juice means you can play through your entire setlist, encore, after-party jam, and still have power left for a hotel room acoustic session.</li>



<li><strong>Range That Rivals Your Stage Moves</strong>: 21 meters (70 feet) of freedom. That&#8217;s enough to high-kick your way across any stage without losing signal. David Lee Roth would be proud.</li>

</ul>
