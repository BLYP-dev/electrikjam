<p><strong>Memorizing the fretboard notes on your guitar, even if it is just the bottom string, is one of the best things you can do to improve your playing…</strong></p>



<div class="wp-block-ugb-divider ugb-divider ugb-e5667f9 ugb-divider--v2 ugb-divider--design-basic ugb-main-block"><style>.ugb-e5667f9 hr.ugb-divider__hr{margin-left:auto !important;margin-right:auto !important}</style><div class="ugb-inner-block"><div class="ugb-block-content"><hr class="ugb-divider__hr"/></div></div></div>



<p>Ah the fretboard. The home of all the greatest guitar riffs ever created and the bane of all new guitarists.</p>



<p>Learning the fretboard, knowing all of its notes, might seem impossible if you’re just starting out.</p>



<p>But do not worry, with time, it does start to feel more familiar. Apply some focussed effort, however, and you can pick it up even quicker.</p>



<p>As always, when it comes to learning new things, you want to start with bite-sized chunks. And in the context of your guitar’s fretboard, that means starting with the bottom string.</p>



<p><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/what-are-locking-tuners/" data-lasso-id="28686">This guide</a> will show you <strong>A) how to quickly memorize the notes on your top and bottom string, B) how sharp notes work, C) how to find another note once you&#8217;ve located one note, and D) how to test yourself,</strong> so you&#8217;ve got it. </p>



<div class="wp-block-ugb-divider ugb-divider ugb-4ed2688 ugb-divider--v2 ugb-divider--design-basic ugb-main-block"><style>.ugb-4ed2688 hr.ugb-divider__hr{margin-left:auto !important;margin-right:auto !important}</style><div class="ugb-inner-block"><div class="ugb-block-content"><hr class="ugb-divider__hr"/></div></div></div>



<p><strong>FYI: when I say “bottom string” on guitar, I am referring to the fattest string on the guitar, your e string. The thinnest string is called the top string.</strong></p>



<div class="wp-block-ugb-divider ugb-divider ugb-862580f ugb-divider--v2 ugb-divider--design-basic ugb-main-block"><style>.ugb-862580f hr.ugb-divider__hr{margin-left:auto !important;margin-right:auto !important}</style><div class="ugb-inner-block"><div class="ugb-block-content"><hr class="ugb-divider__hr"/></div></div></div>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>How To Quickly Learn The Notes On Your Guitars String</strong></h2>



<p>The first thing you need to know is how the notes on a guitar actually work –&nbsp;don’t worry, this guide will not feature ANY <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/beginner-music-theory-for-guitarists/" data-lasso-id="102926">music theory</a>.</p>



<p>Just simple, actionable stuff you can easily understand and put to use right away.</p>



			
			<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="853" src="/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/How-To-EASILY-Remember-Fretboard-Notes-on-Guitar-1024x853.png" alt="" class="wp-image-3040" title="" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/How-To-EASILY-Remember-Fretboard-Notes-on-Guitar-1024x853.png 1024w, /wp-content/uploads/2021/05/How-To-EASILY-Remember-Fretboard-Notes-on-Guitar-600x500.png 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2021/05/How-To-EASILY-Remember-Fretboard-Notes-on-Guitar-300x250.png 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2021/05/How-To-EASILY-Remember-Fretboard-Notes-on-Guitar-768x640.png 768w, /wp-content/uploads/2021/05/How-To-EASILY-Remember-Fretboard-Notes-on-Guitar-1080x900.png 1080w, /wp-content/uploads/2021/05/How-To-EASILY-Remember-Fretboard-Notes-on-Guitar.png 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>
			
		


<p>The notes on your guitar’s bottom string, <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/10-metal-bands-that-play-in-standard-tuning/" data-lasso-id="142223">in standard tuning</a>, look like this:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>F</strong></li><li><strong>F#</strong></li><li><strong>G</strong></li><li><strong>G#</strong></li><li><strong>A</strong></li><li><strong>A#</strong></li><li><strong>B</strong></li><li><strong>C</strong></li><li><strong>C#</strong></li><li><strong>D</strong></li><li><strong>D#</strong></li><li><strong>E</strong></li></ul>



<p>This takes you from the first fret right down to the 12th fret –&nbsp;<strong>this is one whole octave</strong>. After the 12th fret, the pattern listed above just repeats again –&nbsp;the notes run the same:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>F</strong></li><li><strong>F#</strong></li><li><strong>G</strong></li><li><strong>G#</strong></li><li><strong>A</strong></li><li><strong>A#</strong></li><li><strong>B</strong></li><li><strong>C…</strong></li></ul>



<p><strong>The # symbol next to a note means it is sharp</strong>. You don’t need to worry about this for now, not for the purposes of this guide.</p>



<p>All you need to remember is that <strong>ALL the notes have a sharp version</strong> of them which comes after the standard version –&nbsp;F# follows F, for instance, and A# follows A.</p>



<p><strong>The ONLY two notes that DO NOT have a sharp are B and E.</strong></p>



<p>This means, with respect to the <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/fretboard-hacks-that-every-beginner-should-know/" data-lasso-id="12760">guitar fretboard</a>, if you’re playing an E on the bottom string, <strong>the next note IS NOT E# but F instead.</strong></p>



<p>Basically, whenever you hit a B or an E on a fret, the next note down is a full step to either C or F, not B# or E#.</p>



<p>Simple, right?</p>



<p>And remember: <strong>if you know the alphabet, you can memorize all the notes on the bottom string</strong>.</p>



<p>F follows E, G follows F, C follows B –&nbsp;so, <strong>it’s really just A, B, C, D, E, F, and G</strong>.</p>



<p>Once you get to G, well, G#, you go back to A again.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Why Is Fret One on The Guitar F And Not E-Minor?</strong></h2>



<p>Remember what I said about the notes B and E?</p>



<p><strong>They DO NOT have a minor note</strong>, so the first fret on your guitar fretboard is an F –&nbsp;F follows E, after all.</p>



<p>And after that you have an <strong>F# (fret 2), G (fret 3), and G# (fret 4).</strong></p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="437" src="/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/notes-on-fretboard.png" alt="" class="wp-image-3041" title="" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/notes-on-fretboard.png 1024w, /wp-content/uploads/2021/05/notes-on-fretboard-600x256.png 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2021/05/notes-on-fretboard-300x128.png 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2021/05/notes-on-fretboard-768x328.png 768w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /><figcaption><strong>The Notes On Your Guitar Fretboard</strong></figcaption></figure>



<p>When you get to the fifth fret on the bottom string, you’re at A – <strong>this is where blues and much of rock and roll lives.</strong></p>



<p>From this fret, you can rock out with at the <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/how-hard-is-it-to-learn-guitar/" data-type="post" data-id="2958" class="rank-math-link" data-lasso-id="1410">A-Minor pentatonic scale.</a></strong></p>



<p>Jimmy Page loves this spot, and so too do most <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/metallica-guitar-tunings-over-the-years/" data-type="post" data-id="2988" class="rank-math-link" data-lasso-id="1411">rock guitarists of note</a></strong>.</p>



<p>The fifth fret is a great place to practice your <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/theory-for-guitarists-the-minor-scale/" data-lasso-id="127899">minor pentatonic scale</a> because it is mid-way down the guitar neck and isn’t too much of a stretch for your fingers.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What About Learning The Other Strings?</strong></h2>



<p>Again, it’s pretty simple: just apply what you learned above.</p>



<p>The second string on a guitar, the one below the bottom string, is A, so <strong>the first fret is A#, then B, then C</strong> –&nbsp;remember, there’s no B# (or E#).</p>



<p><strong>The third string is tuned to D</strong>, so the first fret would be D#, then E, then C, then C# and so on.</p>



<p>You know what each string is <strong>EADGBE</strong>, so you can quickly figure out the notes on each string using the method above.</p>



<p>And once you know the bottom string, you also know the top string too because they’re both tuned to E.</p>



<p>This means <strong>you only really have to worry about four strings.</strong></p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Do NOT Overload Yourself</strong></h2>



<p>The stupidest thing you can do is to try and memorize all of the notes on the guitar –&nbsp;that’s insane. And incredibly boring.</p>



<p>Instead, use this method to understand the E string, then apply it to A string, and then the D string.</p>



<p>Once you’re comfortable with the procession of notes, and <strong>you can remember that there is no B# or E#</strong>, do yourself a little test.</p>



<p>Find an E on the D string, then find an A on the E string, and then find a B on the E string.</p>



<p>It’ll take a while the first few weeks, but with diligent practice you’ll soon start to feel way more at home on the fretboard, knowing where all the notes are more or less instinctively.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>And If You Can Find One Note, You Can Find Another Too</strong></h2>



<p>This little fretboard hack helped me immensely when I was first starting out. If you can find an E, for instance, it doesn’t matter what string it is on, you can instantly find another one without even thinking about it.</p>



<p>Just do this: <strong>go down two strings and move a long two frets – that is the same note</strong>.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="853" src="/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/octaves-on-guitar-fretboard-1024x853.png" alt="" class="wp-image-3042" title="" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/octaves-on-guitar-fretboard-1024x853.png 1024w, /wp-content/uploads/2021/05/octaves-on-guitar-fretboard-600x500.png 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2021/05/octaves-on-guitar-fretboard-300x250.png 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2021/05/octaves-on-guitar-fretboard-768x640.png 768w, /wp-content/uploads/2021/05/octaves-on-guitar-fretboard-1080x900.png 1080w, /wp-content/uploads/2021/05/octaves-on-guitar-fretboard.png 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /><figcaption><strong>How To Find Note Octaves On Fretboard – It&#8217;s Super Simple (And They Sound Great)</strong></figcaption></figure>



<p>For instance, if you play an <strong>A on the bottom string (fret 5), you can find another A on the D string located at Fret 7.</strong></p>



<p>This little hack works for ALL the notes and on all the frets up and down the guitar neck.</p>
