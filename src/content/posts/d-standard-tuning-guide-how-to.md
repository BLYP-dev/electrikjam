<p><strong>D Standard tuning is a great option if you’re going for a heavier sound. Mastodon uses D Standard a lot. Here’s everything you need to know about <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/guitar-tuning/" data-lasso-id="1704">tuning your guitar </a>down to D Standard…</strong></p>



<hr class="wp-block-separator is-style-wide"/>



<p>You can play metal in standard. People claim you can’t or that it doesn’t sound heavy enough. Well, I have two words for you: <strong>OPETH and Dillinger Escape Plan</strong>. They both play in <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/learning-guitar-guide-what-to-expect/" data-type="post" data-id="737" data-lasso-id="1639">standard tuning (EADGBE)</a> </strong>and they’re both heavier than a tourist bus outta Mississippi.</p>



<p>But if you want a guitar sound that is <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/aadgbe-guitar-tuning-drop-a-gettin-proper-low-n-dirty/" data-lasso-id="6307">lower than standard tuning</a> but doesn’t require you to relearn anything, D Standard might be just what the doctor ordered. <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/new-mastodon-album-2021-release-date-track-list-artwork-detailed/" data-lasso-id="2555">Mastodon</a> plays in D Standard a lot, for instance, and I think we can agree that they sound pretty huge.</p>



<p>What I like most about D Standard is that it is just like playing in Standard; all the <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/how-hard-is-it-to-learn-guitar/" data-type="post" data-id="2958" data-lasso-id="1640">chords and scales and power chords</a></strong> remain the same, despite the fact every string has been tuned down a whole step –&nbsp;this preserves the intervals between the strings (but they sound LOWLIER which is what you want if you want to play chugga-chugga metal.</p>



<p>Because the intervals between the strings are persevered, you can play just as you would in standard. All the <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/chord-voicing-extensions-for-metal/" data-type="post" data-id="2151" data-lasso-id="1641">shapes and chords and scales</a></strong> remain the same. They will be different notes because each string is tuned down a whole step, and they’ll sound lower, but you don’t need to relearn anything else which is great.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>D Standard Tuning</strong></h2>



<p>This is how you tune your guitar to <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/most-common-guitar-tunings/" data-lasso-id="12170">D Standard Tuning</a>. If you have a guitar tuner or a tuning app, start on the bottom string and <strong>tune it from E down to D, on the next string, you want G, then C, then F, then A, and finally D. Once you’re done it should look like this: </strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/dgcfad-the-guitar-tuning-of-the-gods/" data-lasso-id="1642">DGCFAD.</a></p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-full aligncenter"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="683" src="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/D-Standard-Tuning.png" alt="D Standard Tuning" class="wp-image-3693" title="" srcset="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/D-Standard-Tuning.png 1024w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/D-Standard-Tuning-600x400.png 600w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/D-Standard-Tuning-300x200.png 300w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/D-Standard-Tuning-768x512.png 768w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<p>As you can see, each string is a whole step down from standard. When you play the bottom string open, it is a D instead of an E. If you want to play a power chord, however, it is exactly the same as it is in E Standard. All the pentatonic scales remain exactly the same too.</p>



<p>The notes on the <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/fretboard-hacks-that-every-beginner-should-know/" data-lasso-id="12761">fretboard</a> have moved, however, so on the fifth fret, in standard, you’d be playing A but in D Standard Tuning you’ll be playing G# –&nbsp;you’re a whole step down remember. If you know all the notes on the bottom E string in standard, a quick way to know where you are is to just move the note down one.</p>



<p><strong>D Standard Tuning is great for metal and heavier music</strong>. It’s also good if your band’s singer struggles to hit higher notes; this is why <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/new-metallica-album-release-date-new-songs-update/" data-lasso-id="17578">Metallica</a> and Black Sabbath often play in D Standard Tuning live –&nbsp;the vocals don’t need to strain quite as hard to hit the higher notes. D Standard Tuning also sounds lower and, arguably, “heavier” than E Standard –&nbsp;although both tunings in the right hands can sound heavy as hell.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Bands That Use D Standard Tuning</strong></h2>



<p>Because D Standard is <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/10-metal-bands-that-play-in-standard-tuning/" data-lasso-id="142214">similar to standard tuning</a>, unlike drop tunings, plenty of bands have used this tuning to great effect over the years. <strong>Mastodon uses D Standard</strong> quite a bit and so too does <strong><a data-lasso-id="1643" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/metallica-guitar-tunings-over-the-years/" data-type="post" data-id="2988">Metallica</a></strong>, as well as <strong>CKY, Judas Priest, Dream Theatre, and Cradle of Filth</strong> to name just a few bands off the top of my head. <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/converge-x-chelsea-wolfe-join-forces-for-bloodmoon-i-album/" data-type="post" data-id="4732" data-lasso-id="12045">Chelsea Wolfe</a></strong> also learned to play in D Standard. And a lot of her <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/queens-of-the-stone-age-guitar-tuning-a-guide/" data-lasso-id="16014">music is in either D Standard</a> or C Standard. </p>



<p>Bands looking for an even heavier sound often tune down even further to <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/doom-metal-tunings-the-top-5-best/" data-type="post" data-id="1491" data-lasso-id="1644">C Standard</a></strong>. This being a whole step down from D Standard. Again, <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/what-guitars-do-mastodon-use/" data-lasso-id="9128">Mastodon uses this tuning</a> quite a bit. Josh Homme did too on early <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/90s-metal-bands/" data-lasso-id="2805">Queens of The Stone Age and KYUSS</a>. <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/matt-pike-guitar-guide-a-complete-list-of-what-he-uses/" data-type="post" data-id="3565" data-lasso-id="1645">Sleep’s Matt Pike</a></strong> uses C Standard on most of <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/matt-pike-dopesmoker-sleep/" data-type="post" data-id="2815" data-lasso-id="1646">SLEEP’s discography</a></strong>.</p>



<p>D Standard is great because you get all the versatility of standard tuning, everything remains in the same place, just a whole step down, and you get a heavier, beefier sound that lends itself really well to heavier music like metal, doom, and stoner rock.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Is D Standard The Same As Drop D?</strong></h2>



<p>D Standard is NOT the same <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/deftones-songs-in-drop-d-a-complete-list/" data-type="post" data-id="3419" data-lasso-id="1647">Drop D tuning</a></strong>. With Drop D tuning, you’re only tuning down the bottom string a whole step from E to D. The rest of the strings stay the same, so Drop D tuning looks like this: DADGBE.</p>



<p>The upshot of Drop D tuning is that you can play power chords with one finger, no need to barre. Because only the bottom string has been <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/best-strings-for-drop-d-tuning/" data-lasso-id="99321">tuned down in drop d</a>, you will need to relearn your positions for your scales. The top string is tuned down a whole step but the other strings remain the same as they are in standard. This means the interval between the D and the A are not preserved, unlike in D Standard.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Which Sounds Heavier?</strong></h2>



<p>Drop D and D Standard both sound heavy but if you’re after a generally lower tone, you’ll want to go with D Standard. Why? Simple: <strong>all the strings are tuned down a whole step in D Standard</strong>. <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/drop-d-vs-drop-c-whats-the-difference/" data-lasso-id="69693">In Drop D</a>, only the top string is tuned down a whole step –&nbsp;the rest of the strings are the same as they are in standard.</p>



<p>For this reason, <strong>you get a lower and “heavier” sound with D Standard</strong>. You can also <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/the-easy-way-to-play-an-f-chord-on-guitar/" data-lasso-id="84714">play chords easily</a> and you do not need to relearn your scales. Oftentimes, this makes playing fast or shredding through scales easier in <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/bands-that-play-in-d-standard/" data-lasso-id="86082">D Standard</a>. But Drop D does have its benefits such as one-finger power chords which are great for faster riffs.</p>



<p>Two of the biggest bands on the planet that use Drop D tuning are <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/adam-jones-epiphone-1979-les-paul-custom-release-date-specs/" data-type="post" data-id="2979" data-lasso-id="1648">TOOL</a></strong> and Lamb of God. <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/buzz-osbornes-guitar-rig-35-years-of-evolution/" data-type="post" data-id="1565" data-lasso-id="1649">Melvins use Drop D</a></strong> too. All of these bands are VERY different but it does go to show just what you can do with Drop D tuning. Both <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/adam-jones-names-the-guitarists-that-influenced-him-most/" data-lasso-id="10866">TOOL</a> and Lamb of God sound huge, so if you are after something that sounds like either of these awesome bands, you should definitely check out Drop D.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Best Strings For D Standard Tuning?</strong></h2>



<p>Because you’re tuning your strings down a whole step in D Standard, there will be less tension on the string compared to standard tuning. For this reason, you’ll want to <strong><a href="https://www.gear4music.com/Guitar-and-Bass/Ernie-Ball-Skinny-Top-Beefy-Bottom-Guitar-Strings-10-54/3BL2" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener" data-lasso-id="1650">use either 10’s or 11’s</a></strong> –&nbsp;both are fine for D Standard and Drop D tuning.</p>



<p>The best strings for D Standard will depend a lot on the scale length of your guitar too. If you’re using a Les Paul-style guitar, <strong><a href="https://www.gear4music.com/Guitar-and-Bass/Ernie-Ball-Skinny-Top-Beefy-Bottom-Guitar-Strings-10-54/3BL2" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener" data-lasso-id="1651">you’ll want to use 11-48 strings</a></strong>. With a Fender-style scale length, you should use <strong><a href="https://www.gear4music.com/Guitar-and-Bass/Ernie-Ball-Skinny-Top-Beefy-Bottom-Guitar-Strings-10-54/3BL2" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener" data-lasso-id="1652">11-54 strings</a></strong>. This is my personal preference. My advice would be to try a few different variants and see which you prefer.</p>



<p>Most electric <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/every-time-i-die-guitar-tunings/" data-lasso-id="3078">guitars in standard tuning</a> will happily tune down to D Standard. I’ve never had any issues with this. The only time you might run into problems is if you’re using really thin strings. But even then it’ll probably still work fine.</p>
