<p><strong>There are plenty of fundamentals that come with being a Metal guitarist. But have you have found yourself in a rut when it comes to composing riffs? Today we explore some lesser known chord voicing and extensions that will spice up your playing!</strong></p>



<hr class="wp-block-separator"/>



<p>There are so many things you have to learn when you become a Metal-focused guitar player. You have to learn how to<a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/how-to-palm-mute/" class="rank-math-link" data-lasso-id="925"> <strong>palm mute correctly</strong></a> to get different types of &#8220;chugs&#8221;. Then comes picking techniques, and how to use different amounts of <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/best-guitar-pedals-for-doom-metal/" class="rank-math-link" data-lasso-id="926"><strong>fuzz and gain</strong>.</a></p>



<p>But your number one weapon of choice, is usually the standard power chord. It&#8217;s such a <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/the-easy-way-to-play-an-f-chord-on-guitar/" data-lasso-id="84713">popular way to play chords</a>, that power chords can be found in all kinds of different genres. But for me, I find it very easy to get stifled when using nothing but power chords to write riffs.</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>RELATED: <a class="rank-math-link" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/how-hard-is-it-to-learn-guitar/" data-lasso-id="927">Can I Learn Guitar In 2 Months? Probably Not – Here&#8217;s Why…</a></strong></li></ul>



<p>Power chords work great to build the &#8220;skeleton&#8221; of a song or riff. But if you want your riffs to really stand out, you need more in your arsenal than just your standard power chord progression. And that&#8217;s why we are looking at the <strong>13 lesser known chord voicing and extensions</strong> today!</p>



<p>These idea will not only improve your playing and sound, but they&#8217;ll also make your recordings –&nbsp;<strong><a class="rank-math-link" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/how-to-record-electric-guitar-at-home-guide/" data-lasso-id="928">if you&#8217;re trying to record at home</a></strong> –&nbsp;sound a lot fuller and more dynamic. </p>



<p>Some of these are totally bonehead ideas, that you might just have never thought of, or maybe you already use some of them! They may only add a single note to a chord. And then some of them are more refined and <em>classy</em>. </p>



<p>These classy chord voicing usually come from Jazz guitar ideas.</p>



<p>The criteria for these lesser known chord voicing and extensions, is that they can come from literally any play style. At the same time they can be used specifically for Metal players to form something out of the norm, or spice up one of your existing riffs!</p>



<p>We will be checking out voicing and extensions in Standard tuning, <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/bands-that-use-drop-c-tuning/" class="rank-math-link" data-lasso-id="929">Drop tunings</a></strong>, and even a couple of 7 string ideas! </p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The 13 Lesser Known Chord Voicing and Extensions</h2>



<hr class="wp-block-separator"/>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">#13 – Suspended Bass Chord Voicing</h2>



<ul class="wp-block-list"><li><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/standard-tuning-guitar-explained-history/" data-lasso-id="221308"><strong>Standard Tuning</strong></a></li><li><strong>Extended Voicing</strong></li></ul>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="282" src="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/B-SUS-1024x282.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-2152" title="" srcset="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/B-SUS-1024x282.jpg 1024w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/B-SUS-600x166.jpg 600w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/B-SUS-300x83.jpg 300w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/B-SUS-768x212.jpg 768w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/B-SUS.jpg 1073w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<p>This is a really cool trick to use when you&#8217;re trying to make a regular power chord sound extremely heavy in standard Tuning. This is a lesser known chord voicing for Metal, that makes things definitely heavier. This is especially good for break downs.</p>



<p>The added bass note CAN make things muddier in the mix, so keep that in mind, and use this voicing sparingly.</p>



<hr class="wp-block-separator is-style-wide"/>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">#12 – Augmented Voicing</h2>



<ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Drop D Tuning</strong></li><li><strong>Works at the 5th fret with any drop tuning</strong></li></ul>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="282" src="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/AUG-1024x282.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-2308" title="" srcset="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/AUG-1024x282.jpg 1024w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/AUG-600x166.jpg 600w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/AUG-300x83.jpg 300w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/AUG-768x212.jpg 768w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/AUG.jpg 1073w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<p>This can work as an arpeggiated chord, or you can play all of the notes together. It&#8217;s really up to you. This voicing gives you a little bit of flavor that a regular power chord just doesn&#8217;t have!</p>

<p>This is another one that it great to use, just to add quick flair to an otherwise boring power chord. This is kind of a big stretch! So don&#8217;t feel bad if you can&#8217;t do it immediately. It takes practice!</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">#10 – Another SUS Variant</h2>



<ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Standard tuning</strong></li><li><strong>Movable </strong></li></ul>

This is another variant that can be moved around the fretboard. This is another one that requires you to stretch out that pinkie finger!</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">#9 – Dyad With An Open String</h2>



<ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Standard Tuning</strong></li><li><strong>Movable across the fretboard</strong></li></ul>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="282" src="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Dyad-1-1024x282.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-2311" title="" srcset="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Dyad-1-1024x282.jpg 1024w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Dyad-1-600x166.jpg 600w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Dyad-1-300x83.jpg 300w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Dyad-1-768x212.jpg 768w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Dyad-1.jpg 1073w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<p>At first glance, this looks like a backwards power chord, or inverted&#8230;and you&#8217;re right if you thought that! This is a great way to add some different sounds to your chord progressions. the bottom note is your root note.</p>



<p>So in this case, the above diagram would be used for a G chord.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="282" src="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Barre-1024x282.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-2312" title="" srcset="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Barre-1024x282.jpg 1024w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Barre-600x166.jpg 600w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Barre-300x83.jpg 300w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Barre-768x212.jpg 768w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Barre.jpg 1073w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">#7 – Full Open A Chord</h2>



<ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Standard Tuning</strong></li></ul>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="282" src="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/open-A-1024x282.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-2313" title="" srcset="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/open-A-1024x282.jpg 1024w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/open-A-600x166.jpg 600w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/open-A-300x83.jpg 300w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/open-A-768x212.jpg 768w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/open-A.jpg 1073w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>

<p>This makes your A Chord sound insanely heavy! Almost as heavy as Drop A!</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">#6 – Drop Tuned Dissonance</h2>



<ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Drop tuning</strong></li><li><strong>Movable around the </strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/fretboard-hacks-that-every-beginner-should-know/" data-lasso-id="12763">fretboard</a></li></ul>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="282" src="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/DRop-Diss-1024x282.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-2314" title="" srcset="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/DRop-Diss-1024x282.jpg 1024w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/DRop-Diss-600x166.jpg 600w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/DRop-Diss-300x83.jpg 300w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/DRop-Diss-768x212.jpg 768w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/DRop-Diss.jpg 1073w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>

<p>I use this chord form often in in Drop A Tuning, and it makes the chord much more<em> sinister</em>.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">#5 – Tritone Accent</h2>



<ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Standard tuning</strong></li><li><strong>Movable chord shape</strong></li></ul>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="282" src="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Tritone-accent-1024x282.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-2315" title="" srcset="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Tritone-accent-1024x282.jpg 1024w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Tritone-accent-600x166.jpg 600w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Tritone-accent-300x83.jpg 300w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Tritone-accent-768x212.jpg 768w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Tritone-accent.jpg 1073w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<p>This works very well, when played as an arpeggiation.  But it also works really well as a palm <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/john-frusciante-explains-how-to-play-cant-stop-properly/" data-lasso-id="103268">muted chord</a>. This accents the root note (in this case, B) and accents the Tritone to sound absolutely <em>evil</em>.</p>



<p>So this would be used as a B rooted chord. But like most dissonant sounding chords, it needs to be at the right place at the right time.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">#4 – Drop Accents</h2>



<ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Drop tuning</strong></li><li><strong>Moveable chord shape</strong></li></ul>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="282" src="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Drop-Accent-1024x282.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-2316" title="" srcset="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Drop-Accent-1024x282.jpg 1024w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Drop-Accent-600x166.jpg 600w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Drop-Accent-300x83.jpg 300w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Drop-Accent-768x212.jpg 768w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Drop-Accent.jpg 1073w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<p>This can make your plain old Drop chord sound very <em>pretty</em>! That&#8217;s right, we are moving away from evil sounding chords for a second, and checking out how to make some chords prettier.</p>



<p>This voicing can change the key of your song, and should be used wisely. Usually in a drop chord, the bass note is what carries the progression. If we assume the above diagram is in <a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/drop-d-vs-drop-c-whats-the-difference/" data-lasso-id="69694">Drop D tuning</a>, the bass note is in F. But now you also have notes that make the C chord.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">#3 – Dissonant Chords</h2>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="282" src="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Dissonant-chord-1024x282.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-2317" title="" srcset="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Dissonant-chord-1024x282.jpg 1024w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Dissonant-chord-600x166.jpg 600w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Dissonant-chord-300x83.jpg 300w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Dissonant-chord-768x212.jpg 768w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Dissonant-chord.jpg 1073w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<p>This is a popular chord to use in Metal, especially if you chug first, and then hit this chord. It is very dissonant, and noisy when you&#8217;re using a lot of gain.</p>



<p>If you listen to <strong>ZAO</strong>, <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/90s-metal-bands/" data-lasso-id="2806">Converge</strong>, or<strong> Dillinger Escape Plan</strong></a> then this chord will sound very familiar! These bands use this chord form a LOT.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">#2 – 7 String Augment</h2>



<ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Standard 7 String tuning: B to B</strong></li><li><strong>Not movable</strong></li></ul>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="282" src="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/7-string-aug-1024x282.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-2318" title="" srcset="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/7-string-aug-1024x282.jpg 1024w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/7-string-aug-600x166.jpg 600w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/7-string-aug-300x83.jpg 300w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/7-string-aug-768x212.jpg 768w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/7-string-aug.jpg 1073w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<p>Depending on how you use this chord voicing, it can be very pretty or very dissonant. This utilizes the open B string on your 7 string for a bass note, while adding in some augmented sweetness!</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">#1 – The Devil&#8217;s Tritone</h2>



<ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Standard Tuning</li><li>Lots of variants</li></ul>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="282" src="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/devils-tritone-1024x282.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-2319" title="" srcset="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/devils-tritone-1024x282.jpg 1024w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/devils-tritone-600x166.jpg 600w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/devils-tritone-300x83.jpg 300w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/devils-tritone-768x212.jpg 768w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/devils-tritone.jpg 1073w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<p>The diagram above is just one way of many to get this ultimately evil sounding chord. This chord form, or any form of this series of notes is known as the Devil&#8217;s Tritone or &#8220;diabolus in musica&#8221;.</p>



<p>It was once even banned from popular music in the classical days! It was thought to summon evil!</p>



<p>The song, &#8220;Black Sabbath&#8221; by <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/doom-metal-beginners-guide/" class="rank-math-link" data-lasso-id="930">Black Sabbath</a></strong> is one of the most popular uses of this series of notes. It is not only dissonant, but it sounds very unresolved. The &#8220;evil&#8221; aspect of these forms is definitely apparent.</p>



<p>Strangely enough, these chords are also used in Jazz music! </p>



<p><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/line-6-3-5-update-explained/" data-lasso-id="197570">Need to update</a> your axe? Check out our pick for <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/best-guitar-pedals-for-metal-our-top-choices-for-2020/" class="rank-math-link" data-lasso-id="931">the best new guitars designed specifically for metal, sludge, and doom. </a></strong></p>
