<p>Squier is the entry-point to the world of Fender guitars. They&#8217;re the cheapest models and, because they&#8217;re not branded &#8220;Fender&#8221; they&#8217;re often dismissed as sub-par. Now, the true, entry-level models (the sub-$200 ones) aren&#8217;t gig-ready for professionals, obviously. </p>



<p>But that&#8217;s not what they&#8217;re designed for; instead, they&#8217;re designed for players that are first starting out, learning the ropes. </p>



<p>But <strong><a href="/guitar-brands/squier-electric-guitars/" data-type="guitar-brands" data-id="14164">Squier&#8217;s more expensive models like the &#8217;60&#8217;s Classic Vibe</a></strong> are bloody great intermediate guitars that pack in a ton of great hardware and playability for around 40% less than a basic, bare-bones &#8220;proper&#8221; Fender. </p>



<p>Brant Bjork has played a Squier Stratocaster for most of his career; he now uses a MIM Fender, but he did some of his best work with a Squier. My point? Don&#8217;t write-off Squier&#8217;s more premium models; they serve up <strong><a href="/brand-of-guitar/squier/">some of the best value for money on the market right now. </a></strong></p>



<p>The Squier Paranormal baritone, for instance, is one of my most prized possessions. It is a phenomenal guitar. If you go the Squier route &#8212; either cheap or premium &#8212; get it setup properly and it&#8217;ll play beautifully. </p>

<p>The Player Series is what we used to just call the &#8220;Standard&#8221; series. For the most part, the Player Series is the best value if you ask veteran guitarists. </p>



<p>These are the original &#8220;Made in Mexico&#8221; line, and takes the place of the &#8220;Standard&#8221; series. You won&#8217;t find a bunch of features with these, but they did receive a bump up in quality from the original Standard Series. </p>



<p>For instance, you get a more modern trem system with the two point pivot bridge and better pickups than before. Of course, this new attention to details comes with a price hike. </p>



<p>Combine that with inflation, and even the most basic Fender is pushing $1000.</p>



<p>But just because its basic, doesn&#8217;t mean its bad by any means. Many gigging guitarists use the Player Series because a Mexican Strat is still a great guitar. </p>



<p>Leo Fender had a rare case of &#8220;Getting it right the first time&#8221; with these guitars. It may be lowest on the Fender price tiers, but these guitars come in all kinds of pickup options and finishes. </p>



<p>These guitars are ready for the stage after a good setup, and they provide an excellent platform for modifying your guitar. The genius of Leo&#8217;s original design was the Strat is every piece is modular, and replaceable!</p>



<p>The upgrades that Fender made with the player series are not really a big deal, but just small details that should have been addressed long before, in my opinion. </p>



<p>Like finally having a full sized 22 fret neck, instead of the usual Mexican 21 fret models. The Player Series offers some small changes to the &#8220;Standard&#8221; Series, and the Player II series was a massive step-up in terms of quality and spec. </p>



<p>If I were in the market to buy a Fender guitar right now, and I had a solid decade or so of playing under my belt, <strong><a class="thirstylink" rel="nofollow" target="_blank" title="Fender Player II Stratocaster HSS Deal" href="/recommends/fender-player-ii-stratocaster-hss-deal/" data-shortcode="true">I&#8217;d be looking squarely at Fender&#8217;s Player II series of guitars. </a></strong></p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="htoc-tier-5-vintera-road-worn-noventa-mim"><strong>Tier 5 – Vintera, Road Worn, Noventa (MIM)</strong></h3>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="683" src="/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Fender-Vintera-Series-Guitars-1024x683.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-17869" title="" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Fender-Vintera-Series-Guitars-1024x683.jpg 1024w, /wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Fender-Vintera-Series-Guitars-600x400.jpg 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Fender-Vintera-Series-Guitars-300x200.jpg 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Fender-Vintera-Series-Guitars-768x512.jpg 768w, /wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Fender-Vintera-Series-Guitars.jpg 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li id="htoc-price-1-100-1-400"><strong>Price:</strong> $1,100–$1,400</li>



<li id="htoc-vintera-vintage-specs-7-25-radius-period-correct-pickups"><strong>Vintera:</strong> Vintage specs, 7.25” radius, period-correct pickups.</li>



<li id="htoc-road-worn-relic-treatment-straight-from-the-factory"><strong>Road Worn:</strong> Relic treatment straight from the factory.</li>



<li id="htoc-noventa-p90-loaded-fenders-with-a-mix-of-vintage-looks-and-modern-playability"><strong>Noventa:</strong> P90-loaded Fenders with a mix of vintage looks and modern playability.</li>
</ul>



<p>This is the newest of the MIM Fender price tiers, and it allows you to step back into the past and experience the golden age of Fender, or step forward. Unfortunately, this is also the most confusing! </p>



<p>The Vintera Series offers a &#8220;New Old Stock&#8221; type of guitar, offering vintage specs that you would find on a 60&#8217;s Fender. </p>



<p>But the Vintera Standard Series asks the question: &#8220;What if you bought a guitar in the 1960&#8217;s and never touched it?&#8221;. </p>



<p>These are brand new in look, but have all of the features and appointments that a vintage guitar would have. These are pristine recreations of vintage Fender guitars, and they come in all kinds of vintage finishes and body styles.</p>



<p>The Vintera Road Worn Series is exactly the opposite. These still have all of the vintage features, but they have been given the &#8220;relic treatment&#8221; to make them look and feel like a Fender that has been played for the last 50 years. </p>



<p>These have been roughed up a little bit to give the guitar the look and feel of a well-played guitar from the past. Relic Treatment guitars are a subject of controversy in the guitar community. </p>



<p>Some people think you should bang up the guitar yourself over the years. Personally, I think they look pretty cool, and it gives the impression of a guitar that has seen a lot of shows, and been through a little bit of hell. Relic jobs were once limited only to Custom Shop offerings.</p>



<p>The Vintera Series does have vintage specs though, and these may not be for everyone. The most common complaint is the 7.25 fretboard radius, which can cause problems when playing up high on the neck. </p>



<p>Since the fretboard has a curve to it, notes can easily &#8220;choke out&#8221; if your guitar isn&#8217;t set up properly. Everything on these models is vintage, down to the strap buttons. </p>



<p>The pickups are recreations of the popular 60&#8217;s single coils, and they have that glorious bell-like chime to them. I would love to own one of these, but the neck radius and 21 frets just doesn&#8217;t jive with my play style.</p>



<p>The Vintera Series also offers a Modified version, where we take a look at a vintage instrument that may have seen some upgrades over the years. The Vintera Modified offers the same vintage feel in the body shape and pickups, but it has a more modern neck. </p>



<p>This eliminates the problem of the classic 7.25 fretboard radius and replaces it with a modern C profile 9.5 radius neck. These also offer the S1 switch that allows you to access even more tones from pushing the volume knob down. acting as a passive boost that you would usually see in active pickups. </p>



<p>Basically, this is a hot rodded version of the Vintera Series that has all kinds of modern mods, but retains the vintage style.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="htoc-the-p90-loaded-noventa-series">The P90 loaded Noventa Series</h3>



<p>The final model in this series is the Noventa Series. These are more of a &#8220;what if&#8221; type of model, that makes you wonder what Fender would have done with some high output P90 pickups. </p>



<p>Gibson had the P90 first, and it wasn&#8217;t until much later that the design was allowed to the public. So these Noventa models are special in a way, sporting MP-90 single coil pickups that offer a wide dynamic range of tones. </p>



<p>Over the years, many players have stated that the P90 is one of the most perfect pickups ever invented, and to be fair…this might be true! The P90 offers a tone that neither Humbucker or Single coil can really offer. The Noventa comes in several finishes and body shapes.</p>



<p>The P90 pickup was designed in 1946 by Gibson, and it held the patent for many years. If you have ever wondered what is so special about a P90, then the Noventa is a great introduction. </p>



<p>The MP-90 pickups designed by Fender are great for all kinds of music, and was favored in the 70&#8217;s by punk rockers. It has often been called a &#8220;lazy humbucker&#8221; because of the tone. It has all of the power of a vintage PAF style humbucker, but the bite of a single coil. </p>



<p>These fender versions are a little hotter than your usual P90, and the Noventa series may look vintage, but they sport modern specs:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list is-style-list-check">
<li id="htoc-c-shaped-neck">C Shaped neck</li>



<li id="htoc-9-5-radius-fretboard">9.5 radius Fretboard</li>



<li id="htoc-21-medium-jumbo-frets">21 medium jumbo Frets</li>



<li id="htoc-classic-finishes">Classic finishes</li>



<li id="htoc-3-body-styles-jazzmaster-strat-tele">3 body styles (Jazzmaster, Strat, Tele)</li>



<li id="htoc-deluxe-gig-bag">Deluxe gig bag</li>
</ul>

<p>As we go Further up the Fender price tiers, we start seeing some big improvements. The Player Plus Series is still made in the Ensenada, Mexico factory but these have the specs of the famous American-made Fenders. </p>



<p>This is also a new part of the Fender price tiers, and it acts as a more professional version of the basic Player Series. Adding more features and detail to the instrument, across all models in the Plus Series:</p>

<p>The Player Plus series has tons of different models, and they all offer the best that the Mexican factory can produce. </p>



<p>These get the same attention to detail that the American models get, and the difference is pretty noticeable. The rolled fretboard edges sounds like something very arbitrary when you first hear about it, but it makes the neck feel worn in and well played…as if you have owned the guitars for years. </p>



<p>The 12&#8221; radius and medium jumbo frets also add a modern touch that allows you to play faster, and more accurately when you get further up the neck.</p>



<p>All in all, this series may seem a little expensive for what you get. But the Player Plus Fenders that I have played have all been in a league of their own. Some could use a few setup tweaks, but overall these are ready for the more modern player right out of the box. </p>



<p>These are perfect for gigging guitarists that want a Fender, but need a guitar that isn&#8217;t living in the past. The Player Plus Series is a direct result of Fender listening to its customer base. Guitarists that wanted a classic look, but a more sophisticated modern feel will absolutely love these guitars.</p>

<p>Choosing the right guitar out of the Fender price tiers is going to rely on your budget, first and foremost. </p>



<p>The Fender price tiers start pretty low with the classic designs, and <strong><a href="/best-guitar-deals/">there is even a HUGE SALE going on right now</a></strong> for various models.</p>



<p>But budget would definitely come first when deciding which of the Fender price tiers you need to buy from.</p>



<p>But there is also the features, and country of origin to consider. It has long since been known that the Mexican factory is just a few miles away from the main American one. It has been a constant debate as to which is better, and most players ignore the Fender price tiers completely. </p>



<p>The rumors have always said that the Mexican guitars are just rejected USA necks and bodies. They may have slight imperfections, and not make the USA grade. So they go to the Mexican factory, and considering that most Fender guitars are pretty simple and share the same features…this is not hard to believe.</p>



<p>I can agree with this sometimes, since I have owned some really nice Mexican Fender guitars. In some cases, I would say that my MIM Fenders might have rivaled an American made one. </p>



<p>I think this is still true to some extent, but it has never been more important to actually play the guitars first, and try them out. Like any brand, there are some great playing guitars along with some absolute duds across all of the lineups. </p>



<p>The Fender price tiers is a good guide to start with, but playing the guitar will tell you all that you need to know.</p>



<p>However, the new Fender price tiers also help to put the factory myths to rest with the addition of the Ultra and Luxe models. These are clearly superior to the MIM models in almost every way, and that comes down to the attention to detail. </p>



<p>You&#8217;re not going to have to perform surgery to get these playing well. But you&#8217;re going to pay for that, so you need to weigh the pros and cons. I would order an Ultra II Fender sight unseen, from a website. </p>



<p>I&#8217;m not so sure I would do the same when it came to a MIM model these days. There is a huge leap in quality between those models, and I would need to play any MIM model first.</p>



<p>So which one is right for you? If you know how to do a setup on a guitar, and fix minor issues, then any of the MIM models will be good for you. Even the Player series is a pro-level guitar, and with some upgrades it will be more than sufficient to gig with. </p>



<p>However, a more experienced player that would notice details like rolled fretboard edges, and use things like the S1 switch…they might be happier with some of the American models.</p>



<p>The point is, there is a Fender out there for everyone, and hopefully this sheds a little bit of light on the huge array of models. </p>
