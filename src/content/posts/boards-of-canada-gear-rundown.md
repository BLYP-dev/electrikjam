<li>Boards of Canada&#8217;s signature synth is the Yamaha CS-70M, an analog polysynth from the 1980s.</li>



<li>They also use the Roland SH-101 monophonic synth for its thick, beefy bass tones.</li>

<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity is-style-dots"/>



<p>Scottish electronic music duo Boards of Canada, known for their ambient, downtempo style, have crafted a distinctive sound using a surprisingly minimalist setup. </p>



<p>Despite the complex, layered nature of their music, the gear they rely on is relatively simple and straightforward.</p>



<p>And this, in our age of GAS and myriad plugins, is something we should all learn a lesson from. </p>



<p>Whether you’re making metal, doom, ambient or electro music, cutting down on the accoutrements is something a lot of us could benefit from.&nbsp;</p>



<p><strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/electrikjam-podcast/" data-lasso-id="264273">Chris and I talk about this A LOT on the podcast</a></strong>. Stop chasing gear for specific sounds and/or tones, work with what you have, get experimental, actually limit yourself (gear-wise) and see what you can come up with.&nbsp;</p>



<p>Boards of Canada&#8217;s entire ethos is based around less is more. Creating sounds and unique palettes from equipment that most people wouldn&#8217;t even look twice at&nbsp;– aging samplers, cheap, thrift-store samplers.</p>



<div class="custom-snippet-div"></p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>The Gear Boards of Canada Use To Make Music</strong></h2>



<p>At the heart of their setup are a handful of classic synthesizers. The Yamaha CS-70M, in particular, has been cited as their &#8220;trademark&#8221; synth. </p>



<p>This analog polysynth from the 1980s is featured prominently across their discography. They also make use of the Roland SH-101, a compact monophonic synth known for its thick, beefy bass tones.</p>



<p>For sampling, Boards of Canada turn to the tried-and-true Akai S1000 and S3200 samplers. </p>



<p></div>



<div class="custom-snippet-div"></p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Synths, Samplers &amp; Dusty, Old Casio Keyboards</strong></h2>



<p>In a 2005 interview, they praised the S1000 as their favorite, noting <strong>&#8220;It&#8217;s an old tank now, and the screen has faded so that I almost can&#8217;t read it, but I know it inside out. It&#8217;s the most spontaneous thing for making up little tunes.&#8221;</strong></p>



<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-full"><img decoding="async" width="460" height="177" src="/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/AKAI-S1000.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-14740" title="" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/AKAI-S1000.jpg 460w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/04/AKAI-S1000-300x115.jpg 300w" sizes="(max-width: 460px) 100vw, 460px" /></figure>



<p>When it comes to recording and arranging their tracks, the duo keeps things relatively old-school. </p>



<p>They often record extended jams onto tape using vintage machines like the Tascam MSR-16 reel-to-reel, Revox recorders, Grundig machines, and even ordinary cassettes. </p>



<p>Of course, you cannot escape using computers when it comes to things like arrangements and getting stuff ready for release. And Boards of Canada do have a preference when it comes to DAWs. </p>



<p></div>



<div class="custom-snippet-div"></p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Boards of Canada Uses Logic Pro To Arrange Everything</strong></h2>



<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="683" src="/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Logic-Pro-101-1024x683.png" alt="" class="wp-image-14599" title="" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Logic-Pro-101-1024x683.png 1024w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Logic-Pro-101-600x400.png 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Logic-Pro-101-300x200.png 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Logic-Pro-101-768x512.png 768w, /wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Logic-Pro-101.png 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<p><strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/what-is-logic-pro/" data-lasso-id="264274">They use Apple Logic Pro as their main DAW</a></strong>, but generally eschew plug-ins and soft synths in favor of their hardware.</p>



<p>Other notable pieces in their studio include the Yamaha AN1x, used during their Lighthouse Party performance, a Taylor Big Baby steel-string acoustic guitar, and a Soundcraft Spirit Folio SX mixer for live shows. </p>



<p>Even the humble Casio CZ-101, an affordable digital synth from the 80s, has been spotted in their live rig. I saw them at The Deadbeat Festival YEARS ago and can confirm that this synth was used on a few tracks. </p>



<p>The gear choices of Boards of Canada demonstrate that you don&#8217;t always need the latest, most hyped equipment to create groundbreaking music. </p>



<p>By focusing on a core set of flexible, characterful tools – <strong>and knowing them inside and out</strong> – you can actually create something that not only sounds brilliant but will also stick out from the droves of </p>
