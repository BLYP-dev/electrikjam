<p>If you want to sound like Mastodon there&#8217;s a few core concepts you need to understand. From the way you tune your guitar to your amp settings, there&#8217;s a fair amount of nuance that goes into Mastodon&#8217;s sound.  </p>



<p>Mastodon has been dominating the metal and rock scene for over a quarter of a century. As the guys enter their 50s, they’re not showing any signs of slowing down.</p>



<p>Hushed &amp; Grim was an incredible album. Arguably, the band’s most proggy to date. And that’s fine. But Bill Kelliher has officially confirmed that with <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/new-mastodon-album/" data-type="post" data-id="10681" data-lasso-id="216596">the band’s next album will be A LOT HEAVIER</a></strong>.</p>

<p>I love Crack The Skye too, but for me its biggest hitters, riff-wise, are Leviathan and Blood Mountain. Ever since I first heard these albums, I’ve been obsessed with Brent and Bill’s guitar tone and style.</p>



<p>If you&#8217;re a guitarist looking to emulate their style, there are a few key elements to consider. In this guide, we&#8217;ll explore the guitars, amps, and pedals used by Brent Hinds and Bill Kelliher of Mastodon.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="mastodon-guitar-tuning"><strong>Mastodon Guitar Tuning</strong></h2>



<p>In order to properly dial-in Mastodon’s trademark sound, you’ll need to tune your guitar down. </p>



<p>Plenty of Mastodon is in <strong><a data-lasso-id="216601" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/d-standard-tuning-guide-how-to/" data-type="post" data-id="3692">D Standard (DGCFAD)</a></strong> which is a whole step down from Standard Tuning (EADGBE).</p>



<p>Mastodon also uses Drop B tuning, so BGCFAD, whereby you run the guitar in D Standard but drop the bottom string down two whole steps to B and, for really special occasions, they’ll drop the lowest string down another step to A for a tuning that looks like this: <strong>AGCFAD</strong>.</p>



<p>Keep in mind, if you want to use these lowered tunings you will need to <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/best-strings-for-drop-tuning/" data-type="post" data-id="7372" data-lasso-id="216602">use strings designed for lower tunings.</a></strong></p>



<p>For the most part, though, Mastodon likes to play in D Standard. The vast majority of its most popular songs are in this tuning, so if you want to learn some iconic Mastodon riffs, dropping your guitar down to D Standard is the place to start.</p>



<p>The upshot of D Standard is that you can also learn the riffs and parts in Standard Tuning as well. It won&#8217;t sound as &#8220;heavy&#8221; or as &#8220;low&#8221; as the record but it&#8217;ll all transpose the same. </p>



<p>All the patterns and shapes are the same, ditto chords, only with D Standard you&#8217;re a whole step lower which is what gives Mastodon that &#8220;heavier&#8221; sound when compared to <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/10-metal-bands-that-play-in-standard-tuning/" data-type="post" data-id="8536" data-lasso-id="264302">metal bands that play in Standard Tuning. </a></strong></p>

<p>Mastodon&#8217;s guitar tone is also heavily influenced by their choice of amplifiers. Brent Hinds and Bill Kelliher have used a range of amps and cabs over the years. </p>



<p>With Brent Hinds, his current amp setup is made up of two Marshall JMP Mark II Lead series heads, a &#8217;76 and a &#8217;77, and a 1998 Diezel VH4. </p>



<p>Each amplifier is connected to two Orange PPC412 4&#215;12 Cabinets finished in a custom color. When he&#8217;s playing his lap steel, Hinds uses an Orange CR120 combo amp.</p>



<p>Hinds has his own signature model with Orange, The Brent Hinds Terror. </p>



<p>With Bill Kelliher&#8217;s amp setup, it&#8217;s a little more nuanced and a little more modern. </p>



<p>Inspired by Alice in Chains guitarist Jerry Cantrell&#8217;s tones, Kelliher currently uses a Friedman JJ Cantrell Signature 100-watt head, which drives 65-watt Celestion G12M-65 Creambacks housed in a Friedman 4&#215;12 cabinet. </p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="Mastodon_Guitar_Techniques"><strong>Mastodon Guitar Techniques</strong></h2>



<p>Mastodon&#8217;s music draws from a wide palette of influences, including metal, stoner rock, progressive rock, Southern rock, and even country. </p>



<p>This eclectic mix of styles which includes techniques like hybrid picking and the use of odd time signatures is what made the band initially stand out amongst their peers. </p>



<p>One of the key elements of Mastodon&#8217;s guitar playing is their use of uncommon time signatures. </p>



<p>The band frequently incorporates 12/8 and 6/8 time signatures into their songs, adding a layer of complexity and depth to their compositions. </p>

<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity is-style-dots"/>



<p>One of the essential guitar techniques they employ is muted alternate picking. This technique involves rapidly alternating between downstrokes and upstrokes while muting the strings with the palm of the picking hand. </p>



<p>Muted alternate picking is a crucial component of many Mastodon riffs, allowing them to create tight, precise, and aggressive-sounding passages.</p>



<p>You&#8217;ll also need to <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/online-guitar-modes-tool-learn-all-the-shapes-tabs/" data-type="page" data-id="14564" data-lasso-id="264306">get acquainted with the Phrygian mode</a></strong>. This mode, which is often used in metal music, is what adds that eerie, iconic sound to many of the band&#8217;s best riffs</p>



<p>Mastodon frequently employs the <strong>A Phrygian mode</strong> in songs that are played in drop A tuning, where the lowest string is tuned down to an A note. This combination of the Phrygian mode and drop A tuning creates a powerful and ominous atmosphere that is central to Mastodon&#8217;s musical identity.</p>
