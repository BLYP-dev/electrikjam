<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="Akai_Professional_MPK_Mini_MK_Review">Akai Professional MPK Mini MK3 Review</h2>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="Key_Feel:_A_Step_Up,_But_Not_a_Leap_Forward">Design</h3>



<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="683" src="/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Akai-Professional-MPK-mini-mk3-Joystick-1024x683.png" alt="Akai Professional MPK mini mk3 Joystick" class="wp-image-12707" title="" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Akai-Professional-MPK-mini-mk3-Joystick-1024x683.png 1024w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Akai-Professional-MPK-mini-mk3-Joystick-600x400.png 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Akai-Professional-MPK-mini-mk3-Joystick-300x200.png 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Akai-Professional-MPK-mini-mk3-Joystick-768x512.png 768w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Akai-Professional-MPK-mini-mk3-Joystick.png 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<div class="wp-block-affiliate-plugin-lasso">[lasso id=&#8221;12698&#8243; link_id=&#8221;249659&#8243; ref=&#8221;akai-professional-mpk-mini-mk3-keyboard-controller-grey-guitar-center&#8221; type=&#8221;button&#8221;]</div>



<p>The MPK Mini MK3 has improved its keybed, making it more responsive than its predecessor. The keys feel somewhat similar to the M32 from Native Instruments, which is a good thing. </p>



<p>However, if you&#8217;re looking for a keybed that will &#8220;wow&#8221; you, you might want to check out the Arturia Minilab MK2. The MPK Mini MK3&#8217;s keys are good but not groundbreaking.</p>



<p>One of the more polarizing features of the Akai Professional MPK Mini MK3 is its joystick, which serves as a replacement for the traditional pitch and modulation strips found on many other MIDI keyboards. </p>



<p>Now, let&#8217;s get into the nitty-gritty of why this matters.</p>



<p>The joystick provides a two-in-one control for both pitch bending and modulation. Push it left or right, and you&#8217;re bending pitch. Move it up or down, and you&#8217;re controlling modulation. </p>



<p>This dual functionality can be a space-saver and offers a different tactile experience compared to touch strips.</p>



<p>From a user experience standpoint, the joystick can be more intuitive for some. It gives you a physical point of reference, allowing for potentially more precise control. </p>



<p>You can feel the joystick&#8217;s position, which can be beneficial for live performances where you might not have the luxury of looking down at your keyboard often.</p>



<p>However, if you&#8217;re transitioning from a keyboard with touch strips, there might be a slight learning curve. </p>



<p>Touch strips often provide visual indicators, like LEDs, to show the level of pitch or modulation. With the joystick, you&#8217;re flying a bit more blind, relying solely on feel.</p>



<p>It&#8217;s also worth mentioning that some advanced users might find touch strips more versatile. </p>



<p>For instance, some keyboards allow you to customize the touch strip&#8217;s functionality, assigning it to different parameters beyond just pitch and modulation. </p>



<p>As of now, the joystick on the MPK Mini MK3 doesn&#8217;t offer this level of customization.</p>



<p>Ultimately, the joystick vs. touch strip debate boils down to personal preference. Some users swear by the joystick for its tactile feedback and intuitive control. </p>



<p>Others prefer the versatility and visual cues offered by touch strips.</p>



<p>So, if you have a strong preference for one over the other, this could be a deciding factor in whether the MPK Mini MK3 is the right fit for you. </p>



<p>It&#8217;s not a deal-breaker by any means, but it&#8217;s an important consideration that could impact your overall satisfaction with the keyboard.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="Knobs:_Endless_but_Loose">Knobs: Endless but Loose</h3>



<p>The knobs on the MPK Mini MK3 are a noteworthy upgrade from its predecessor, the MK2. Here&#8217;s why:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Endless Rotation</strong>: Unlike traditional knobs that have a start and end point, these are endless. This means they sync seamlessly with your DAW, providing a continuous range of parameter adjustments. No more worrying about the knob&#8217;s physical position not matching up with the software setting.</li>



<li><strong>DAW Integration</strong>: This feature is particularly useful when you&#8217;re <strong>working in a digital audio workstation (DAW)</strong>. As you turn the knob, it directly correlates with the parameter you&#8217;re adjusting on-screen, offering a more intuitive and efficient workflow.</li>



<li><strong>Improved Size</strong>: The knobs are also larger than those on the MK2, making them easier to manipulate. This is a welcome change for those who found the MK2&#8217;s knobs too small for precise control.</li>
</ul>



<p>However, it&#8217;s not all sunshine and rainbows:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Loose Feel</strong>: One downside is that these knobs feel a bit loose, especially when compared to the Arturia Minilab&#8217;s knobs, which offer more resistance. This could be a matter of personal preference, but if you&#8217;re someone who likes a more tactile, &#8220;weighted&#8221; feel to your controls, this could be a point of contention.</li>
</ul>



<p>So, while the endless knobs are a significant step up in terms of functionality and DAW integration, the tactile experience might not be for everyone. </p>



<p>It&#8217;s a nuanced feature that offers both pros and cons, and it&#8217;s worth considering how much the &#8220;feel&#8221; of a knob matters to you in your music production setup.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="Build_Quality:_Still_Room_for_Improvement">Build Quality: Still Room for Improvement</h3>



<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="683" src="/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Akai-Professional-MPK-mini-mk3-review-1024x683.png" alt="Akai Professional MPK mini mk3 review" class="wp-image-12706" title="" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Akai-Professional-MPK-mini-mk3-review-1024x683.png 1024w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Akai-Professional-MPK-mini-mk3-review-600x400.png 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Akai-Professional-MPK-mini-mk3-review-300x200.png 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Akai-Professional-MPK-mini-mk3-review-768x512.png 768w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Akai-Professional-MPK-mini-mk3-review.png 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<div class="wp-block-affiliate-plugin-lasso">[lasso id=&#8221;12698&#8243; link_id=&#8221;249660&#8243; ref=&#8221;akai-professional-mpk-mini-mk3-keyboard-controller-grey-guitar-center&#8221; type=&#8221;button&#8221;]</div>



<p>When it comes to build quality, the MPK Mini MK3 falls short of the robustness you might find in competitors like the Arturia Minilab and Native Instruments&#8217; M32. </p>



<p>Here&#8217;s a breakdown:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Material &amp; Weight</strong>: The MPK Mini MK3 is primarily made of plastic, which contributes to its lighter weight but also gives it a somewhat toy-like feel. This is in contrast to the more solid, substantial build of the Arturia Minilab and M32.</li>



<li><strong>Budget-Friendly</strong>: It&#8217;s important to remember that the MPK Mini MK3 is positioned as a budget-friendly option. You&#8217;re not paying premium prices, so expecting a premium build might be unrealistic.</li>



<li><strong>Competitive Comparison</strong>: If you&#8217;ve ever laid hands on an Arturia Minilab or M32, you&#8217;ll immediately notice the difference. Those keyboards feel more solid and less flimsy, providing a sense of durability that the MPK Mini MK3 lacks.</li>



<li><strong>Limited Editions</strong>: On a positive note, the MPK Mini MK3 does come in limited edition colors like white and black, which some users find aesthetically pleasing. However, aesthetics don&#8217;t necessarily translate to build quality.</li>
</ul>



<p>In summary, if build quality ranks high on your list of priorities, the MPK Mini MK3 might not be your go-to choice. It&#8217;s a functional, budget-friendly option, but it doesn&#8217;t offer the same tactile satisfaction or sense of durability that some of its competitors do.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="The_Winning_Feature:_MPC-Style_Pads">The Winning Feature: MPC-Style Pads</h3>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="683" src="/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Akai-Professional-MPK-mini-mk3-1024x683.png" alt="Akai Professional MPK mini mk3" class="wp-image-12699" title="" srcset="/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Akai-Professional-MPK-mini-mk3-1024x683.png 1024w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Akai-Professional-MPK-mini-mk3-600x400.png 600w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Akai-Professional-MPK-mini-mk3-300x200.png 300w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Akai-Professional-MPK-mini-mk3-768x512.png 768w, /wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Akai-Professional-MPK-mini-mk3.png 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>

<p>The MPC-style pads on the MPK Mini MK3 are the keyboard&#8217;s crown jewel, especially if you&#8217;re into beat-making or finger drumming. </p>



<p>These pads are not just an afterthought; they are responsive, have a satisfying amount of travel, and offer a tactile experience that can make your drumming sessions more engaging and precise.</p>



<p>What sets these pads apart is the dual-bank feature. You&#8217;re not limited to just eight pad assignments; you can switch between two banks, effectively giving you 16 different pads to work with. </p>



<p>This is particularly useful for those who like to have multiple samples or effects at their fingertips without having to constantly remap their pads.</p>



<p>So, if the quality of the drum pads is a significant factor for you, the MPK Mini MK3&#8217;s MPC-style pads could be a game-changer. </p>



<p>They offer a level of responsiveness and versatility that can elevate your beat-making process, making the keyboard a worthy contender in its price range.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="Screen:_A_Missed_Opportunity">Screen: A Missed Opportunity</h3>



<p>The screen on the MPK Mini MK3 is a feature that, at first glance, promises a lot but currently delivers little in terms of DAW integration. </p>



<p>While it does show MIDI Control Change numbers (CCs) and their corresponding values, it falls short of displaying the actual parameter names you&#8217;re adjusting in your DAW. </p>



<p>This can be a bit of a letdown, especially when you consider that the keyboard comes with program modes specifically designed for various DAWs like Ableton, Logic Pro, and FL Studio.</p>



<p>This lack of DAW feedback on the screen feels like a missed opportunity. It&#8217;s a feature that could significantly enhance the user experience by providing real-time, at-a-glance information. </p>



<p>The hope is that Akai will address this in future firmware updates, turning what is currently a basic display into a more interactive and informative feature. </p>



<p>Given that competitors like Arturia and Novation don&#8217;t even offer screens on their comparable models, Akai has the chance to set the MPK Mini MK3 apart in a meaningful way.</p>

<p>The MPK Mini MK3 does offer some DAW control, but it&#8217;s clear that <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/what-is-ableton-live/" data-type="post" data-id="12564" data-lasso-id="249657">Ableton users will get the most out of it </a></strong>right out of the box. </p>

<p>This isn&#8217;t a plug-and-play experience for these DAWs, and it can be a bit of a hassle, especially for beginners or those who want to get up and running quickly.</p>

<p>The MPK Mini MK3 is equipped with a built-in arpeggiator and a note repeat feature, both of which elevate its performance capabilities. </p>

<p>What&#8217;s particularly impressive is that these features are available at this price point, making the MPK Mini MK3 an even more compelling option for budget-conscious musicians and producers. </p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="Software_Bundle:_A_Good_Starter_Kit">Software Bundle: A Good Starter Kit</h3>

<p>Included in the package is Akai&#8217;s MPC Beats software, a user-friendly DAW tailored for beat-making. Additionally, you get a selection of Air instruments, including Hybrid 3, a versatile synthesizer; Mini Grand, a realistic piano plugin; and Velvet, an electric piano emulator.</p>



<p>For those who are new to the world of music production, this bundled software offers a solid foundation. You&#8217;re essentially getting a mini-studio right out of the box, allowing you to dive into creating music without the need for additional software purchases. </p>



<p>It&#8217;s a value-added proposition that makes the MPK Mini MK3 not just a MIDI controller, but a comprehensive solution for aspiring musicians and producers.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="Final_Thoughts">Final Thoughts</h2>



<p>The Akai Professional MPK Mini MK3 is a decent keyboard for its price. It excels in certain areas like pad quality and has some useful performance features. </p>



<p>However, it also leaves room for improvement in build quality, key feel, and DAW integration. If Akai can address these issues in future updates, this keyboard has the potential to be a game-changer in the budget segment.</p>



<p>So, should you buy it? </p>



<p>If you&#8217;re an Ableton user who values pad quality and performance features, this could be a solid choice. But if you&#8217;re looking for a more robust build and better DAW integration, you might want to explore other options.</p>



<p>Remember, the best gear is the one that fits <em>your</em> needs. Choose wisely.</p>
