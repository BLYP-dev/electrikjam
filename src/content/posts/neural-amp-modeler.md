<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="What_Is_Amp_Modeling?_Exactly?">Amp Modeling is The Future Whether You Like It Or Not&#8230;</h2>



<p>Amp modeling has really taken off in the last decade or so, and tons of guitarists have &#8220;gone digital&#8221;. Even mainstream artists use amp modelers on tour, since it reduces the problems that guitar amplifiers can sometimes cause.</p>



<p>There&#8217;s no tube maintenance, and you do not have to lug around different amps for different tones anymore. Amp modelers used to be a luxury, but now I see products like the Helix being used at my local dive bar by cover bands.</p>



<p>There are many different ways to use amp modelers. You have physical devices like Helix, Headrush, and Quad Cortex that work like a pedalboard. But you also have computer-based amp modelers that you can use in the studio, or your bedroom with headphones.</p>



<p>Most guitarists seem to enjoy Neural DSP (unrelated to THIS Neural) which was developed to capture the sounds and tones of certain artists. </p>



<p>You can then take the tones that <strong>Gojira</strong> or <strong>Polyphia</strong> used on their albums, and make your own tones and presets.</p>



<p>But that is just one type of amp modeling, and this past year has seen the <strong><a data-lasso-id="221243" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/headrush-prime-first-look/" data-type="post" data-id="11193" target="_blank" rel="noopener">launch of the Headrush Prime</a></strong> and the TONEX, which can basically clone your physical amps at home. </p>



<p>Previously, only the Kemper Profiler could do this, but technology has really caught up.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="what-is-amp-modeling">What Is Amp Modeling?</h2>



<p><strong>Amp modeling is a digital recreation of the tones and sounds that you would get from a physical amplifier. Amp models react much like a &#8220;real&#8221; amp, and can be used in the studio and on stage. Most modelers have &#8220;presets&#8221; that capture a certain amp and speaker combo.</strong></p>



<p> <strong>This is usually comprised of two parts: </strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>The amp itself, and the different channels of the amp at various gain stages</strong></li>



<li><strong>An impulse response, also called an IR. This mimics the speaker&#8217;s response to the amp.</strong></li>
</ul>



<p></div>



<p>Not everyone is so impressed with modeling technology in the guitar community, and these players prefer a real amp. </p>



<p>I was one of those guitarists until recently, and I honestly think that modeling tech is better than ever. </p>



<p><strong>Especially computer plugins, in my case.</strong></p>



<p><strong><a data-lasso-id="220175" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/stl-amphub-review/" data-type="post" data-id="5892" target="_blank" rel="noopener">I started using STL Tones AmpHub </a></strong>a year ago in the studio, and I have been incredibly impressed with the sounds I am able to get using only my computer. <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/line-6/" data-type="page" data-id="4586" target="_blank" rel="noopener" data-lasso-id="221244">Helix Native from Line 6 </a></strong>is also very popular, and I have used that in the studio as well.</p>



<p>But all of these products cost money, either upfront or by subscription. The cost can pile up quickly in a studio, especially if you are using other plugins for actual production. </p>



<p>But there are some &#8220;free&#8221; options out there&#8230;</p>



<p>Most of the free plugins allow you to try out the quality of one or two amps and IRs. So they are limited when it comes to customization.</p>



<p>We even tried out <strong><a data-lasso-id="220176" href="https://www.electrikjam.com/the-best-free-amp-simulators-in-2021/" data-type="post" data-id="2699" target="_blank" rel="noopener">the &#8220;best&#8221; free amp sims </a></strong>a year or two ago. <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="but-where-do-tones-come-from">But Where Do &#8220;Tones&#8221; Come From?</h2>



<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="683" src="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Re-amp-Neural-Amp-Modeler-1024x683.png" alt="neural amp modeler" class="wp-image-11221" title="" srcset="https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Re-amp-Neural-Amp-Modeler-1024x683.png 1024w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Re-amp-Neural-Amp-Modeler-600x400.png 600w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Re-amp-Neural-Amp-Modeler-300x200.png 300w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Re-amp-Neural-Amp-Modeler-768x512.png 768w, https://www.electrikjam.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Re-amp-Neural-Amp-Modeler.png 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<p>This can be really confusing, and almost every company that sells amp sims and impulse responses have a different way of &#8220;capturing&#8221; the amplifier characteristics. But the &#8220;idea&#8221; is what we are going to talk about today, not the geeky stuff.</p>



<p>The idea, is to take a real amplifier, and find every metric. This means going through different volumes, gain stages, and depending on the amp; the channels. This can be a long complex process, and recent products like the Headrush Prime or the TONEX have simplified this to an extent.</p>



<p>With the new <strong><a href="https://www.electrikjam.com/headrush-prime-first-look/" data-type="post" data-id="11193" target="_blank" rel="noopener" data-lasso-id="221245">Headrush Prime </a></strong>or TONEX, it takes a &#8220;capture&#8221; of your amp. This is a lot like taking a screenshot on your computer and then turning it into a picture. You plug up the amp, and get a &#8220;copy&#8221; of whatever the amp tone is, at the time.</p>



<p>But that is recent technology, and Neural Amp Modeler is using this recent tech to make guitar tones. These are <strong>snapshots</strong>, rather than full-fledged amplifier profiles. So you have limited control over the actual tone.</p>



<p>When it comes to doing cabinets, making an impulse response is a little easier. The process is a lot like measuring your studio room for frequencies, and adding sound barriers to &#8220;treat&#8221; your room. </p>



<p>You have to get the &#8220;feel&#8221; of the cabinet, as well as the sounds of microphones placed in different positions on the speaker. The frequencies are then recorded and turned into a WAV file.</p>



<p>This is called &#8220;convolution&#8221; and you should read this outstanding article about how speaker IRs work, and what convolution does. </p>



<p><strong><a data-lasso-id="221246" href="http://www.earlevel.com/main/2012/03/05/convolution%E2%80%94in-words/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">Check out this article by Nigel Redmon</a></strong> if you want a deep-dive into modeling and IRs. </p>



<p>What makes Neural Amp Modeler so cool, is that it allows to take these amplifier &#8220;snapshots&#8221; and save them as a file. Much like the way that you would make an IR, but the process is a little different.</p>



<p>You let the Neural Amp Modeler capture the amp tone, but then you have to &#8220;train&#8221; it. This is a lot like a TONEX Pedal in many ways, except it is FREE. NAM is currently only computer-based, with no hardware to accompany it.</p>



<p><strong>But it is totally up to you to find amps, and IRs to use.</strong> This is a community-based project, and NAM does not come with any amp models or IRs by default. Luckily, the community is world-wide and VERY enthusiastic.</p>



<p>Still, it sounds too good to be true? Well, it isn&#8217;t, and today we are going to go over how you can download NAM and get started making tones!</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="nam-how-does-it-work">NAM: How Does It Work?</h2>



<p><strong>Disclaimer: I have no idea how this stuff works at a professional level. So I am going to try and explain how NAM works the BEST I can.</strong></p>



<p>Most modelers and capture tech like TONEX or Kemper take an analog sound, and transfer it it an algorithm, that then gets transferred into a digital sound. That is about as simple as I can explain it, but the thing is, the TONEX and similar products have EPOCH limitations.</p>



<p>I have no idea what an EPOCH is, but it deals with the information involved with modeling software, and how it transfers these computer codes into sound. In the NAM files that you download, you can see some of this code.</p>



<p><strong>I&#8217;m just a guitar tech/player/teacher. I am not great when it comes to modern computer tech.</strong> <strong>But I am still able to download tones and play around!</strong></p>



<p>The Neural Amp Modeler does not have the same limitations when getting a &#8220;capture&#8221; of your amp. This means that you can model your amp just like TONEX, but you will then have to &#8220;train&#8221; the model that you just &#8220;captured&#8221; to get it sounding its best.</p>



<p>Training is just using NAM to compare the real amp signal to the captured model. There is an online way to train the models, and this is how you get them to sound as close to the &#8220;real&#8221; amp. This is all done with machine learning, and I have not captured any amps myself.</p>



<p>NAM can do a lot of this for you, with AI from what I understand, but I have never made a capture of an amp on either device. While TONEX does most of the work for you, NAM will need some work on your part if you plan on capturing your amp accurately.</p>



<p>So TONEX has a better interface, and it has big company support. It also has the advantage of having a piece of actual hardware in the form of a pedal. But NAM is stuck on your computer, and it would be tough to get it running in a live situation.</p>



<p>Now if you are just wanting to download Neural Amp Modeler and use the presets that other users have captured? Then that is totally fine, and you don&#8217;t need to be a tech wizard to get it up and running.</p>

<p>Both work by downloading captures from users, and both require similar experience. I think the NAM Plugin could be very alarming to most companies that are making amp sims. The NAM plugin sounds GREAT, so why would you buy an expensive amp sim for home use?</p>



<p>Well&#8230; TONEX has the advantage of being an actual pedal, and it has support from IK Multimedia. Steve could disappear on us, and never update the Neural Amp Modeler again. This is totally open source, so someone else could take up the torch. But would it still be free?</p>



<p>Likewise, other amp sim companies have name brand recognition. NAM is a simple program that looks like it is still in a testing stage&#8230; because it IS. If you are looking for &#8220;ease of use&#8221; then something like STL Tones or Neural DSP are going to be better plug and play options.</p>



<p><strong>I honestly don&#8217;t see NAM as a threat, just an alternative.</strong> This is something you can download and mess around with if you can&#8217;t afford some of the other products. IK Multimedia really doesn&#8217;t have anything to worry about, because it really comes down to <strong>user experience</strong>.</p>



<p>I really like NAM, but it would greatly affect my workflow in the studio. Minutes and seconds are money, and I have presets and templates for all kinds of projects already dialed in, with other brand&#8217;s products. I think this is the case for most of us, when it comes to recording studios.</p>



<p>So Neural Amp Modeler is a very cool project, and I really want to learn how amp profiling &#8220;works&#8221;. If you are that type of person, then you should download NAM and play around with it! I really want to learn how to capture an amp, and being able to see and manipulate code is awesome.</p>



<p>If you can&#8217;t afford the mainstream plugins, and you have been using free plugins this whole time to play guitar&#8230; then NAM is just another free tool to add to your inventory. </p>



<p>After spending the last week or so playing with Neural Amp Modeler, I think it can become something much more refined. But until then, the big companies like Line 6, Headrush, and Kemper shouldn&#8217;t be too worried.</p>



<p>Again, this all comes down to user experience, and you have to have a little more knowledge than just plugging up your guitar and picking a preset. NAM in its current state is not as user-friendly for most guitarists as something like the Helix.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="final-thoughts">Final Thoughts&#8230;</h2>



<p>Neural Amp Modeler is a very cool program, and it can do some things that no other plugin on the market can accomplish. The fact that it is open source, and free for all to use, was a long time coming.</p>



<p>A long time coming? What do I mean by that?</p>



<p>Have you ever wondered how amp modeling worked? I know I have, and I often wished I knew how to create a program to model amps. I would love to not be constricted to the parameters that a company like Line 6 has in place. Well, now we know how it works on a base level, by using NAM!</p>



<p>For most of us, amp modeling has been very mysterious. I know that Line 6, for example, takes a room full of real amps and turns them into digital presets. But I had no idea how this was actually done, until now. </p>



<p>Maybe Line 6 does something <em>completely different</em> to capture amps, but now I understand the idea. Like I stated before, NAM is a great learning tool for those of us that are interested in amp modeling. This gives us a little bit of insight into modeling tech.</p>



<p>Whatever the technology that these big name brands use, is probably not the same thing a Neural Amp Modeler. But at least we get a glimpse into how all of this works. I know it has been very enlightening to me.</p>



<p>There will always be people that think that amp modeling doesn&#8217;t sound as &#8220;good&#8221; as a real amp in the studio. But it is hard to deny that something like NAM captures the EXACT sound of that amp. I think NAM changes the &#8220;real or fake&#8221; argument, and challenges some of the guitarists that despise modelers.</p>



<p>We are getting much closer to closing the gap between analog and digital, and even the most experienced producers would have a hard time trying to &#8220;find the real amp&#8221; in a mix. Despite all of this, there will always be naysayers.</p>



<p>I don&#8217;t know what the future holds for Neural Amp Modeler, and there are some issues. It is still a work in progress, and it does not work properly all of the time. Certain operating systems struggle with the plugin, as well as some reported DAWs.</p>



<p>Then there&#8217;s the question of ethics, which can be a VERY blurry line. Where do you draw the line, if NAM can recreate and copy something like the Quad Cortex, or Helix? Is it ok to <em>model a modeler </em>and give it away for free?</p>



<p>Unfortunately, I do not think NAM will be &#8220;free&#8221; for much longer. I can see people trying to monetize presets for NAM, and it seems like there are already a few people out there charging money for these captures.</p>



<p>I can also see the big companies swooping in and putting a cease and desist order on NAM. While I don&#8217;t really see anything wrong with the idea, in its current state, I also do not know the laws regarding amp profiles. That&#8217;s not exactly something you think about as a musician.</p>



<p>On the other hand, the Reaper DAW is a &#8220;free&#8221; program that is also open source. While you SHOULD pay for the program, it doesn&#8217;t FORCE you to pay anything. You can make it work, and look just like Pro Tools if you want, or any other popular DAW.</p>



<p><strong>So that line is very&#8230; blurry.</strong></p>



<p>Honestly, if you want to play around with the Neural Amp Modeler, I would definitely go ahead and get it. Let&#8217;s be real here, nothing stays &#8220;free&#8221; for very long. We should definitely enjoy this while it lasts, and maybe learn from NAM to educate ourselves on how amp sims work.</p>



<p>Steve designed it, and absolutely will not take any money for creating NAM from what I understand. This may be a legal loophole, and a smart choice on his part. But that could always change, in the future.</p>



<p>We are living in some pretty wild times for amp modeling, and I hope the Neural Amp Modeler will only get better as Steve adds more features. There are still quite a few bugs when working with Reaper DAW, as well as Mac OS. </p>



<p>But the product is still in its infancy, and I am sure NAM will continue to improve as long as it is allowed to stay functional. The question is&#8230; for how long? And will it still be free once it becomes more mainstream?</p>



<p>I guess we will see&#8230;</p>
